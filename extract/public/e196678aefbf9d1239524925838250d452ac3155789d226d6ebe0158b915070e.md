# FBI Files Show 1947 Flying Disc Reports From New Jersey to Wisconsin

**FBI · N/A**

> This section of FBI case file 62-HQ-83894 contains internal memos, teletypes, and inter-agency letters from the summer of 1947 documenting FBI and military investigations into flying disc sightings across multiple U.S. states. The reports range from civilian eyewitness accounts to a military observation claiming speeds of up to 6,000 mph, though none reached firm conclusions.

This document is a collection of FBI internal communications from the summer of 1947 — memos, teletypes, and letters between field offices and headquarters — all dealing with reported sightings of "flying discs." It falls under FBI case file 62-HQ-83894 and covers incidents in New Jersey, Michigan, Illinois, Wisconsin, and Chicago.

The most detailed investigation concerns a sighting in Hackensack, New Jersey on August 3, 1947. Three witnesses — Charles Caselia Jr., William Truex (a Private stationed at Fort Dix), and Joyce McFarland — reported seeing a round, black object roughly 30 to 40 inches across, moving horizontally about 200 yards above a hilltop. Truex described it as oval on top and pointed on the bottom, "like a large flat child's cup." McFarland called it round, silver-blue, and apparently revolving. The sighting was reported to local police, who took no further action. Notably, witnesses gave conflicting accounts: McFarland denied saying it looked like a bird, and a Mrs. Booth denied seeing anything at all.

A separate incident in Midland, Michigan involved Raymond Edward Lane, a long-time Dow Chemical employee, who brought a sample of fused sand to the company's physics lab around July 9, 1947. He claimed he and his wife Laura had found it burning in a field. Dow's lab determined the material was ordinary non-radioactive sand giving off ammonia gas, plus a small nearly-pure silver nugget. A former Los Alamos employee at Dow noted the fused sand had some characteristics of Los Alamos sand but was not believed to be the same. The FBI treated the story with suspicion: there were discrepancies between husband and wife accounts, and neither could locate the exact site of the incident. Lane was described by colleagues as "very peculiar" and had a 1924 burglary conviction.

The file also includes a military report from July 7, 1947 describing flying saucers observed over southern Wisconsin. One observation calculated a saucer covering 25 miles in 15 seconds (approximately 6,000 mph); another calculated 22 miles in 20 seconds (approximately 3,960 mph). A saucer was reported to have descended vertically edgewise through clouds, stopped at 4,000 feet, then assumed a horizontal position. Separately, an anonymous telegram directed the Army Air Forces to contact Richard F. Shaver of Lily Lake, Illinois, a known science fiction writer, about flying disc origins. The War Department forwarded this to the FBI for investigation. An 18-year-old Chicago woman, Mrs. Robert Little, also reported seeing a disc over Lake Michigan at 3:30 AM on August 8, 1947.

None of the investigations in this file reach a definitive conclusion about what the witnesses saw. The FBI documented the reports, noted inconsistencies, and in some cases forwarded materials to the War Department. Many pages are in poor condition, with several nearly illegible due to age and scan quality, and the document shows moderate evidence of redactions.

## What to know

- The Wisconsin speed estimates (3,960–6,000 mph) were calculated from observer distance estimates made from aircraft — inherently imprecise and not verified by any instruments.
- Several witness accounts directly contradict each other. For example, one witness denied a description attributed to her, and another denied seeing anything at all.
- The anonymous telegram about Richard F. Shaver came from an unknown source, and Shaver was already known as a science fiction writer associated with fringe theories — not a scientific or military authority.
- The Lane/Dow Chemical incident was treated with suspicion by both Dow and the FBI: the couple's accounts had discrepancies, and neither could find the site where the event supposedly occurred.
- Many pages are partially or fully illegible due to the age and quality of the scans, and the document shows moderate redaction, meaning some information has been deliberately withheld.
