# Learjet Crew Reported Bright Lights at 45,000 Feet Over Kansas

**FAA · 2023**

> An FAA SKYWATCH incident report documents the crew of a commercial Learjet 40 observing multiple bright lights high above their aircraft while flying at 45,000 feet, roughly 184 nautical miles west of Kansas City on 7 February 2023. Other pilots in the area said the lights appeared to be satellites. The case is closed with no stated resolution.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report — a standardised form the Federal Aviation Administration uses to log reports of unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP) from pilots and air traffic controllers. It was filed under case number 237UAP00202 and assigned Mandatory Occurrence Report ID ZKC-M-2023/02/07-0003.

On 7 February 2023, the crew of commercial flight OKC618, a Learjet 40 flying eastbound at Flight Level 450 (approximately 45,000 feet), reported seeing multiple bright lights off the left side of the aircraft and high above. The aircraft was about 184 nautical miles west of Kansas City (MCI). The lights were described as changing direction, moving both north and south. The flight had originated from Kansas City Downtown Airport (MKC) and was headed to Wiley Post Airport (PWA) in Oklahoma City. The report was filed through Kansas City Center (ZKC), the regional air traffic control facility.

Other aircraft in the area offered a prosaic assessment: the lights appeared to be satellites. The FAA report does not include any radar data, sensor readings, or independent technical analysis to confirm or rule out that explanation.

The case status is listed as closed. No formal conclusion or resolution is recorded in the document. No tail number for the reporting aircraft is included, and no named individuals appear in the report.

## What to know

- This is a single-source pilot observation — no radar data, photographs, or sensor recordings are included in the report.
- Other pilots nearby attributed the lights to satellites, which is a common and plausible explanation for bright, high-altitude lights.
