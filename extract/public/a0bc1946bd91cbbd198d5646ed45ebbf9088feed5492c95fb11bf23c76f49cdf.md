# Pentagon Memo Authorized Insiders to Share UAP Secrets with Investigators

**AARO**

> This undated Department of Defense memorandum formally authorized people bound by government secrecy agreements to share UAP-related classified information with the All-Domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO). It establishes the legal basis and security procedures for those disclosures but does not itself contain any UAP findings or evidence.

This document is an official memorandum from the Office of the Secretary of Defense, signed by Major General David W. Abba of the DoD Special Access Program Central Office (SAPCO). Its purpose is narrow but significant: it grants AARO — the Pentagon's office responsible for investigating unidentified anomalous phenomena — the authority to interview current and former government personnel who hold or once held sensitive information related to UAP.

The core problem the memo addresses is that many individuals with knowledge of UAP-related programs are bound by nondisclosure agreements (NDAs) that would normally prevent them from discussing what they know. This memorandum states that sharing such information with AARO representatives is an authorized disclosure under Section 1673 of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2023. In practical terms, it tells people: you can talk to AARO without violating your secrecy oath.

The memo comes with strict conditions. All conversations must take place in secure facilities appropriate to the classification level of the information being discussed. If someone believes the information they are sharing is classified at the TOP SECRET or special-access level, AARO must handle it at that level until the classification is formally determined. Crucially, the authorization only covers disclosures to AARO — it does not permit individuals to discuss classified information publicly or with anyone else.

The memo also makes clear that this authorization does not relieve anyone of their ongoing duty to protect classified information from unauthorized disclosure. The fact that the signatory is the director of the DoD Special Access Program Central Office indicates this memo specifically concerns information at the Special Access Program (SAP) level — among the most tightly restricted categories of U.S. government secrets.

Notably, the memorandum is undated, so when exactly it was issued is unknown. It is a procedural document — it sets up the rules for information sharing but contains no UAP-related findings, evidence, or conclusions of its own.

## What to know

- This memo is procedural: it authorizes a process for sharing information. It does not contain any UAP evidence, sighting reports, or investigative conclusions.
- The document is undated, so the exact timing of its issuance relative to AARO's investigations is unclear.
- Authorization to disclose applies only to AARO representatives — nothing in this memo permits public release of classified information.
- The signatory's role at the Special Access Program Central Office indicates this concerns SAP-level information, which is among the most restricted in the U.S. government, but the memo does not describe what specific programs or information are involved.
- This is a single administrative document. It confirms that a disclosure mechanism exists but says nothing about what, if anything, was actually disclosed under its authority.
