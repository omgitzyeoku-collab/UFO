# Military Report Documents Triangular Metallic Object Over the Aegean Sea

**Department of War · N/A · Mediterranean Sea**

> A U.S. Department of War mission report describes a single triangular, metallic unidentified object observed at roughly 25,000 feet over the Aegean Sea near the Greek islands. The document is heavily redacted, with the observer's identity, unit, and operational details all withheld.

This document is a U.S. military Mission Report (MISREP) from the Department of War, originally classified SECRET//NOFORN (meaning secret, not releasable to foreign nationals). It records an observation of one unidentified anomalous phenomenon — a UAP — over a stretch of sea near the Greek islands in the eastern Mediterranean.

According to the report, the observer spotted the object at 1319Z (1:19 p.m. UTC) while returning to base. The UAP was described as triangular in shape and metallic in appearance. It was flying at an altitude of 24,989 feet above mean sea level and traveling at 168 knots — a speed well within the range of conventional aircraft or large drones. The coordinates given (36°34'53"N, 025°59'43"E) place the sighting in the Aegean Sea area, despite the report's title referencing the broader Mediterranean.

The document is heavily redacted under exemption 1.4(a), which covers classified national security information. The observer's identity, military unit, aircraft or platform type, and other operational details are all blacked out. No date for the incident appears in the available text.

No conclusion or explanation for the object is provided in the material available. The report records what the observer described seeing, and the document itself notes that details such as shape and material are the observer's subjective interpretation.

## What to know

- The report is based on a single observer's account. No corroborating sensors, radar data, or additional witnesses are mentioned.
- Heavy redaction throughout the document hides the observer's identity, unit, platform, and other operational context.
- The described speed of 168 knots is within the range of conventional aircraft, drones, or other ordinary explanations.
- All physical descriptions (triangular, metallic) are the observer's subjective interpretation, as the document itself acknowledges.
- No incident date is provided in the available text, limiting the ability to cross-reference with other records.
