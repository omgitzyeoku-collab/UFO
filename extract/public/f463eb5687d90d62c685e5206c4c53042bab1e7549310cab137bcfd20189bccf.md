# Five Commercial Flights Reported Orbiting Lights Over New York State

**FAA · 2023**

> An FAA incident report documents that JetBlue flight 811 and four other commercial aircraft reported seeing multiple orbiting lights at 36,000 feet near Rome, New York on the night of September 25, 2023. The crew compared the lights to aircraft landing lights near the Big Dipper constellation, and no evasive action was taken.

This document is an FAA Washington Operations Center incident notification — a standard form the Federal Aviation Administration uses to log unusual events reported by pilots. It records a sighting of an unidentified aerial phenomenon (UAP) on the night of September 25, 2023, at 11:13 PM Eastern time.

JetBlue flight 811, flying from Boston to Chicago at 36,000 feet, reported multiple lights orbiting directly ahead of the aircraft (the 12 o'clock position) while approximately 8 miles west-southwest of Rome, New York. The crew described the lights as similar in appearance to aircraft landing lights, and noted they were near the Big Dipper constellation. No evasive action was taken, and no law enforcement was notified.

The sighting was not limited to one aircraft. Four additional flights — identified by callsigns LXJ652, DAL2685, CMP422, and UAW5WR — reported similar observations. Boston Air Route Traffic Control Center was advised of the reports.

The FAA report does not include any conclusion about what the lights were. There is no reference to radar data, photographs, or any follow-up investigation. The document is a factual operational log of what pilots reported, not an analytical assessment.

## What to know

- This is a single operational report, not an investigation. The FAA logged what pilots said but did not document any follow-up analysis or conclusion.
- No radar data, photographs, or sensor evidence is referenced in the document.
- The crew themselves compared the lights to aircraft landing lights near the Big Dipper, which raises the possibility of an astronomical explanation such as bright stars or planets near Ursa Major.
- While four additional flights corroborated the sighting, all aircraft were in the same area and would have shared similar viewing angles, so the reports are not fully independent.
- There is minimal redaction in this document. The location field in the original record is blank, but the report body includes specific coordinates and a named location.
