# Military Checklist Forms Logged Dozens of UFO Sightings in Summer 1947

**Department of War · N/A**

> This Department of War document is a collection of standardized incident summary forms recording unidentified flying object sightings, mostly from July 1947. The reports come from military officers, police, pilots, and civilians across the western United States, describing disc-shaped, spherical, and cigar-shaped objects moving at high speeds — with no final explanation offered for most sightings.

This document is a set of military check-list forms used to log early reports of unidentified flying objects, compiled by the Department of War. The incidents are concentrated in the summer of 1947, with dates ranging from June to October of that year. Most sightings occurred across the western United States, at locations including Muroc Air Field in California, Portland, Oregon, Boise, Idaho, and several sites in Washington state.

The witnesses include trained military personnel, police officers, and civilians. At Muroc Air Field on 8 July 1947, 1st Lt. Joseph C. McHenry reported seeing two silver, disc-like objects at roughly 8,000 feet altitude, traveling at about 300 MPH. His sighting was confirmed by multiple other witnesses, including a staff sergeant and a technical sergeant. The form notes the objects traveled against the prevailing wind, which the witness cited as reason to rule out weather balloons. In another Muroc incident, Captain John Paul Stapp described a silvery ovular object about 25 feet across with two fin-like projections, and stated he believed it was man-made based on its outline and functional appearance.

Police and pilot witnesses filed similar reports. Patrolman Earl E. Patterson, a former Air Corps pilot in Portland, described an aluminum disc at roughly 30,000 feet performing sharp 50-degree turns at speeds faster than anything he had seen — he suggested it might be radio-controlled. United Air Lines pilot Captain E. J. Smith reported following nine objects in loose formation for about 45 miles, describing them as thin and smooth on the bottom and rough on top. The document also references Kenneth Arnold's famous sighting near Mt. Rainier, in which a military intelligence officer was openly skeptical, suggesting Arnold should be writing fiction rather than reporting flying discs.

The forms do not present any final conclusions or explanations for the bulk of these sightings. The document was originally classified as RESTRICTED, with partial declassification markings now visible. Several incident numbers are missing from the sequence, and numerous pages are nearly or completely illegible due to the age and quality of the scans.

## What to know

- Most sightings rely on a single witness or small group with no instrument data or physical evidence to back up the observations.
- The document's scan quality is poor, and OCR has produced many garbled words — some details, including speeds and distances, may be misread from the original text.
- Several incident numbers are missing from the sequence and multiple pages are illegible, meaning the record as presented is incomplete.
- The document was originally classified RESTRICTED; while partially declassified, some content may remain obscured or absent.
- No photographs appear in the extracted text, even though one witness (Coast Guardsman Frank Ryman) reportedly took news photographs of an object over Lake Washington.
