# U.S. Military Infrared Video Captures Unidentified Object Over East China Sea

**Department of War · 2021 · East China Sea**

> This 23-second infrared video, recorded by a U.S. military platform on 9 June 2021 over the East China Sea, shows a sensor tracking an unidentified area of contrast for about 18 seconds before losing sight of it. It was among 51 UAP-related records requested by eight members of the U.S. House of Representatives in March 2026, and the assessing office notes the materials lack a confirmed chain of custody.

On 9 June 2021, a U.S. military platform operating over the East China Sea recorded a short infrared video of something it could not identify. The footage, catalogued as DOW-UAP-PR075, runs just 23 seconds. For roughly 18 of those seconds, the sensor tracks what is described only as "an area of contrast" before losing sight of the object. The remaining five seconds contain no content.

The video was uploaded to a classified network in June 2021. It resurfaced publicly after eight members of the U.S. House of Representatives requested access to 51 potentially UAP-related records on 6 March 2026. The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) identified this video as one of those responsive materials.

AAro's assessment is limited. The office says the video is "likely" derived from an infrared sensor aboard a U.S. military platform — the word "likely" indicating they are not fully certain. No details about the object's shape, size, speed, or behaviour are provided. The name of the military platform is redacted from the title, shown only as "[Platform]".

AAro also flags that many of the materials in this collection lack a substantiated chain of custody, meaning the provenance of this video has not been independently confirmed. No extracted text exists from the source document itself; what is known comes from metadata and the official video description.

In short, this is a very brief, low-detail recording. It documents that something was seen and tracked by an infrared sensor, but offers almost nothing about what that something was.

## What to know

- The platform identity is redacted, so there is no way to independently verify which military asset captured the footage.
- AARO itself uses the qualifier 'likely' when attributing the video to a U.S. military infrared sensor, signalling uncertainty about its origin.
- The materials lack a substantiated chain of custody, meaning the video's provenance is unconfirmed.
- The object is described only as 'an area of contrast' — no shape, size, speed, or behaviour details exist in the record.
- The entire video is 23 seconds long with only 18 seconds of actual tracked content, making independent analysis extremely limited.
