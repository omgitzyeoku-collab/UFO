# Pentagon UAP Office Received 757 Reports, Resolved Most as Ordinary Objects

**ODNI · 2023**

> This is the Department of Defense's fiscal year 2024 annual report on unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP), covering May 2023 through June 2024. Of 757 reports received, 118 were resolved as mundane objects like balloons, birds, and drones, 444 lacked enough data to analyse, and 21 were flagged for further study. The office states it has found no evidence of extraterrestrial origin.

The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), a Pentagon unit tasked with investigating unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP), published its consolidated annual report covering the period from May 1, 2023 to June 1, 2024. During that window, AARO received 757 UAP reports, bringing the cumulative total to 1,652 as of October 2024. Of the 757, roughly half — 392 reports — came from the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) in a single bulk transfer covering all FAA UAP reports since 2021, which inflates the period's numbers compared to prior years.

Of the cases AARO examined, 118 were resolved. All of those turned out to be prosaic objects: balloons, birds, unmanned aerial systems (drones), satellites, and conventional aircraft. None pointed to advanced foreign technology or breakthrough aerospace capabilities. However, 444 cases — 59 per cent of the total — were placed into an "Active Archive" because they lacked sufficient data for analysis. Another 21 cases were flagged as meriting further investigation based on reported anomalous characteristics, though the unclassified version of the report does not describe these cases in detail.

The vast majority of reports — 708 out of 757 — involved sightings in the air domain, with 49 in the space domain and none in the maritime or transmedium categories. Notably, none of the space-domain reports came from space-based sensors; all originated from pilots or ground observers. The report highlights specific geographic clusters: 100 reports came from U.S. military assets over the East Asian Seas and 57 from the Middle East. Eighteen reports concerned incidents near U.S. nuclear infrastructure, all of which were categorised as drone activity. One specific incident involved a crashed drone recovered at the D.C. Cook Nuclear Power Plant in Michigan on August 3, 2023. A commercial aircrew also reported a near miss with a cylindrical object over the Atlantic Ocean off the coast of New York.

AARO states it has found no evidence of extraterrestrial beings, activity, or technology. No observers reported adverse health effects during the period. The office also notes it possesses no data indicating the capture or exploitation of any UAP. On the transparency side, AARO launched its public website on October 31, 2023, released Volume I of its Historical Record Report in March 2024, and published documents related to KONA BLUE — a proposed but never approved Special Access Program. The office also tested a prototype sensor system called GREMLIN in March 2024. Separately, Oak Ridge National Laboratory analysed material claimed to be from a UAP, though the report does not detail the results in this unclassified version.

## What to know

- This is a self-reported government document — AARO is summarising its own work, and the findings have not been independently audited.
- 59% of cases (444 out of 757) were shelved due to insufficient data, meaning the majority of reports remain unresolved rather than explained.
- No national-level intelligence sensors (satellite imagery, signals intelligence, or measurement intelligence) were applied during the reporting period, so cases were assessed primarily from operational military reports and FAA logs.
- The 21 cases flagged for further analysis are not described in the unclassified version of this report, so the public cannot assess what made them anomalous.
- The report acknowledges a geographic collection bias toward areas near U.S. military installations and sensors, meaning UAP activity elsewhere may be underrepresented.
