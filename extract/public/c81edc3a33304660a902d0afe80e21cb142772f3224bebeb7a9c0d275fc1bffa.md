# American Airlines Pilot Reported Three Unidentified Lit Objects at 32,000 Feet

**FAA · 2023**

> This FAA SKYWATCH incident report documents a commercial pilot on American Airlines flight AAL2347 observing three well-lit unidentified objects above the aircraft at 32,000 feet on 13 September 2022. The case was closed with no explanation or resolution provided.

On 13 September 2022, at 04:45, the pilot of American Airlines flight AAL2347 — a commercial aircraft flying northwest-bound at 32,000 feet — reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon to the FAA. The aircraft was near the MMB navigational fix, in airspace managed by the Kansas City Air Route Traffic Control Center (ZKC).

The pilot described seeing three distinct, well-lit objects above and to the right of the aircraft. Notably, the pilot stated the objects were not behaving as they would expect satellites to behave, suggesting deliberate or unusual movement.

The FAA initiated a Mandatory Occurrence Report (MOR ID: ZKC-M-2022/09/13-0001) in response to the sighting. No corroborating radar data or additional witness accounts are included in the report. No tail number was recorded for the reporting aircraft, which is unusual for FAA documentation.

The case status is marked as "Closed," but the report provides no resolution, identification, or explanation for what the pilot observed. The document contains minimal redaction, but the lack of follow-up detail means there is simply very little information beyond the pilot's own account.

## What to know

- This is a single-source report based solely on one pilot's observation — no radar data, photographs, or additional witness accounts are included.
- The case was closed without any stated resolution or explanation for what was observed.
- MMB is a navigational fix used by air traffic control, not a city or landmark — the precise geographic location is not stated in the report.
- No aircraft tail number was recorded, which is unusual for FAA incident documentation.
- The report dates from September 2022 and was released as part of the Department of War's public UAP disclosure. It documents a routine FAA reporting process, not a special investigation.
