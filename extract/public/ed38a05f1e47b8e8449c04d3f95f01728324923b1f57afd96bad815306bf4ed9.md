# Helicopter crew reported 3-5 fast-moving lights near Arizona airport

**FAA · 2023**

> This FAA incident report documents a March 2022 sighting near Show Low Airport in Arizona, where a helicopter crew reported 3-5 unidentified aerial phenomena with white lights moving rapidly up to 8,000 feet. The case was closed with no explanation provided.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report — the system the Federal Aviation Administration uses to log unidentified aerial phenomenon sightings in US airspace. It records an event on 23 March 2022 near Show Low (SOW) Airport in Arizona, handled by Albuquerque Center (ZAB), an FAA air traffic control facility.

According to the report, an unidentified aircraft broadcast on the Guard frequency — the emergency radio channel monitored by all aircraft — reporting 3-5 unidentified aerial phenomena with white lights in the vicinity of the airport. The phenomena were described as moving rapidly both vertically and laterally up to 8,000 feet. The reporting aircraft was a general aviation helicopter, and FAA reviewers tentatively identified its callsign as N712GA based on playback of radio recordings, though this identification is noted as only "possible."

A Mandatory Occurrence Report was initiated under ID ZAB-M-2022/03/23-0002. The case is marked as closed. The document provides no resolution, no explanation for what the phenomena were, and no mention of corroborating radar data or additional witnesses.

Notably, the document's own incident date is 23 March 2022, while the metadata in the FAA's release lists the incident year as 2023 — a discrepancy that is unexplained.

## What to know

- This is a single-source report from one helicopter crew. No corroborating radar data or independent witnesses are mentioned in the document.
- The callsign N712GA is described as a 'possible' identification based on audio playback review — it is not confirmed.
- The case is marked as closed with no resolution or explanation for what was observed.
- There is a date discrepancy: the document records the incident on 23 March 2022, but the FAA's release metadata lists the incident year as 2023.
- Redaction levels appear low, but the document is brief and provides minimal detail beyond the initial sighting report.
