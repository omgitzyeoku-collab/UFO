# Cargo Pilot Reports Metallic Object at 40,000 Feet Near Louisville

**FAA · 2023**

> This FAA incident report documents a Boeing 767 cargo crew's sighting of a metallic unidentified object at 40,000 feet, roughly 21 nautical miles southwest of Louisville, Kentucky on 19 September 2023. The pilot described the object only as 'metallic' and could not identify it; the FAA closed the case without resolution.

On 19 September 2023 at 23:01 UTC, the crew of a Boeing 767 cargo flight — callsign ATN3443, operated by Air Transport International — reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon to the FAA. The aircraft was flying northwest-bound at 40,000 feet, approximately 21 nautical miles southwest of Louisville's SDF airport, en route from Lakeland, Florida to Rockford, Illinois.

The pilot-in-command described the object as metallic but provided no further detail and was unable to identify what it was. Because the flight was a cargo operation, there were no passengers aboard who might have offered additional witness accounts.

The FAA logged the event under its SKYWATCH programme and assigned the primary code 'Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon.' The case is now marked as closed. The report contains no mention of corroborating radar data, sensor readings, or follow-up investigation, and no resolution or identification of the object is noted.

The document shows minimal redaction. What limits its evidentiary value is not what was hidden but what was never captured: a single pilot's brief visual impression, with no instrument data to support or challenge it.

## What to know

- This is a single-source report based entirely on one pilot's visual observation — no radar, sensor, or photographic data is mentioned.
- The pilot declined or was unable to describe the object beyond the word 'metallic,' so the record contains very little detail about what was seen.
- The FAA closed the case without identifying the object or noting any follow-up investigation.
- As a cargo flight, there were no passengers who could serve as independent witnesses.
- Low redaction does not mean high evidence — the report is thin because little information was collected, not because information was removed.
