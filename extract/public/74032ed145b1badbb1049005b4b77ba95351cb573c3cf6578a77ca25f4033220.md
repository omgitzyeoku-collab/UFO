# U.S. Embassy reported unidentified high-speed aircraft over Papua New Guinea in 1985

**Department of State · 1/24/85 · Papua New Guinea**

> A 1985 U.S. State Department cable from the Embassy in Port Moresby reported that Papua New Guinea's intelligence agency asked about unidentified high-altitude, high-speed aircraft seen over the country on the evening of January 24, 1985. The Embassy denied any knowledge of U.S. aircraft in PNG airspace that night and asked U.S. Pacific Command for confirmation.

On January 28, 1985, the U.S. Embassy in Port Moresby, Papua New Guinea, sent a diplomatic cable marked "Limited Official Use" to USCINCPAC (the U.S. Commander in Chief, Pacific) in Honolulu. The cable reported that PNG's National Intelligence Organization (NIO) had made an informal inquiry about sightings of unidentified aircraft over the country four days earlier, on the evening of January 24.

According to the cable, an Air Niugini commercial pilot reported that his radar picked up aircraft flying south to north at high altitude and high speed while he was over Angoram, a town in the East Sepik region. Multiple witnesses across PNG also reported seeing contrails. At 7:00 PM local time, one aircraft was observed moving north to south; at 10:00 PM, six to eight aircraft were seen traveling south to north. The objects were described as fast-moving with lights, contrails, and noise. Local residents were frightened enough that the provincial premier called a public meeting, which was attended by the Prime Minister, who happened to be in his electoral district for the weekend.

The Embassy told PNG's intelligence agency that it knew of no B-52 overflights and no U.S. aircraft in PNG airspace on January 24. The cable asked U.S. Pacific Command to confirm this. Notably, the cable itself acknowledged that the information was "very sketchy" and that sources were unsure of the directions the aircraft were flying.

This document was originally classified as "Limited Official Use" and has since been released in full by the U.S. Department of State. No follow-up cable or resolution is included — from this document alone, the outcome of the Embassy's inquiry to Pacific Command is unknown.

## What to know

- The witness accounts are secondhand — relayed from observers to PNG's intelligence agency and then to the U.S. Embassy. No one at the Embassy directly witnessed the events.
- Only one radar detection was reported, from a single commercial airline pilot. No military radar confirmation is mentioned in the document.
- The cable itself notes the information was 'very sketchy' and sources were unsure about the direction the aircraft were flying.
- No follow-up cable or resolution is included. We don't know whether U.S. Pacific Command confirmed or denied anything in response.
- There is minimal redaction in this document — it has been released in full — but the lack of a follow-up means readers are seeing an open question, not a conclusion.
