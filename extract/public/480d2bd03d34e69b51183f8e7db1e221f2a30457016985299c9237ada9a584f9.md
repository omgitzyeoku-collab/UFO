# FAA Report: Pilot Spots White Oval Object at 43,000 Feet

**FAA · 2023**

> This is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report from April 2023 documenting a general aviation pilot's sighting of a white, oval-shaped unidentified aerial phenomenon while flying at 43,000 feet. The object appeared on the aircraft's collision-avoidance system, manoeuvred toward the aircraft before turning away and climbing, and the case was closed without identification.

On 27 April 2023, the crew of a general aviation aircraft with callsign N899SC filed a report with the Federal Aviation Administration after encountering an unidentified aerial phenomenon during a flight from SNA (Santa Ana) to HOU (Houston). The aircraft was flying eastbound at flight level 430 — roughly 43,000 feet — when the crew observed the object.

According to the report, the phenomenon was white and oval in shape. It was initially travelling toward the aircraft at approximately the same altitude before turning away and climbing to about flight level 470 — around 47,000 feet, some 4,000 feet above the aircraft. The closest the object came was 10 nautical miles.

Notably, the object registered on the aircraft's TCAS (Traffic Collision Avoidance System), a radar-based system designed to detect nearby aircraft. However, the system did not issue a resolution advisory — the automated instruction pilots receive when a collision risk is imminent — suggesting the object was detected but not assessed as an immediate threat.

The FAA marked the case as closed. The report contains no resolution or identification of the object. No corroborating radar data from air traffic control is included in the document, and no tail number or operator name was recorded.

## What to know

- This is a single-source report from one flight crew. No independent radar data or additional witnesses are included in the document.
- The object appeared on TCAS, which suggests some radar-reflective presence, but the report provides no detail on the nature of that return.
- The case is marked 'Closed' with no explanation of what the object was — closure does not mean identification.
- No operator name or aircraft tail number is recorded in the report, limiting the ability to independently verify the account.
- The report has low evidence of redaction, but limited detail overall — absence of information is not the same as withheld information.
