# Military Infrared Video Tracks Unidentified Object Over CENTCOM in 2020

**Department of War · 2020 · CENTCOM**

> This is a nearly five-minute infrared video, likely recorded by a U.S. military sensor platform in the Central Command area of responsibility in 2020, showing a sensor tracking an unidentified area of contrast. It was among 51 potentially UAP-related records requested by eight members of Congress in March 2026 and released by the Department of War.

In 2020, a U.S. military sensor platform operating in the United States Central Command (CENTCOM) area of responsibility recorded roughly five minutes of infrared video. The footage shows a sensor tracking an unidentified area of contrast — zooming and panning to keep it in frame and highlighting it with a targeting reticle. The term "area of contrast" is a sensor-display description: it does not confirm the presence of a physical object and could represent an atmospheric phenomenon, a sensor artefact, or a genuine unidentified object.

The video was uploaded to a classified network in June 2024 — four years after the alleged incident. The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) later identified it as part of a collection of responsive materials when eight members of the U.S. House of Representatives requested access to 51 potentially UAP-related records on 6 March 2026. AARO itself noted that the sensor origin is assessed but not confirmed, using the qualifier "likely" when describing the platform.

The Department of War released this record under document ID DOW-UAP-PR059, with an uploader-defined title of "NAG UAP 1 Jun 20". No text is extracted from the video itself; the available facts come from metadata and the description provided by war.gov. AARO noted that many of the materials in this collection lack a substantiated chain-of-custody, meaning the handling history and authenticity of the video cannot be fully verified.

No agency conclusion about what the object is — or whether it constitutes a genuine UAP — has been published alongside this release. The video description available from war.gov appears to cut off at around the 3-minute-38-second mark, leaving the final portion of the footage undescribed.

## What to know

- The term "area of contrast" describes what the sensor displayed, not a confirmed physical object — it could be an artefact, atmospheric effect, or genuine unidentified object.
- AARO uses the word "likely" when attributing the video to a U.S. military infrared sensor, meaning provenance is assessed but not confirmed.
- The video was uploaded to a classified network four years after the incident, and many materials in this collection lack a substantiated chain-of-custody.
- No extracted text exists from the video itself; all available facts derive from metadata and the war.gov CSV description, which appears truncated after timestamp 03:38.
- No official determination of what the observed object is has been published with this release.
