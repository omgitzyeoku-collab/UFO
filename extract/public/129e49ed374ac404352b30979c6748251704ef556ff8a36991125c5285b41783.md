# United Airlines Crew Reported Erratic Lights Over Illinois in 2023

**FAA · 2023**

> This FAA incident report documents a sighting by the crew of United Airlines flight UAL1605 on 23 December 2023. While cruising at 35,000 feet roughly 80 nautical miles west of Springfield, Illinois, the crew observed lights moving erratically in random directions off the left side of the aircraft. Air traffic control radar did not detect anything, and the case was closed without explanation.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report — a standardised form the Federal Aviation Administration uses to log unidentified aerial phenomena reported by pilots and air traffic controllers. It covers a single event on 23 December 2023 involving United Airlines flight UAL1605, an Airbus A320 flying from Los Angeles (LAX) to Baltimore (BWI).

At 11:35, while heading eastbound at flight level 350 (35,000 feet) and approximately 80 nautical miles west of Springfield, Illinois, the crew reported seeing lights off the left side of the aircraft. The lights were described as moving erratically and travelling in random directions at an unknown altitude. The sighting was logged through Kansas City Center (ZKC), the air traffic control facility covering that airspace.

Notably, the phenomenon did not appear on the ATC facility's radar system. The report records no tail number for the aircraft, no information on how long the crew observed the lights, and no indication of how many crew members witnessed the event. The case is marked as closed, with no resolution or explanation documented.

## What to know

- This is a single-source pilot report with no corroborating radar data, photographs, or independent witness accounts.
- The case was closed without any documented explanation or resolution — 'closed' does not mean 'explained'.
- No duration of observation or number of witnesses is recorded, limiting the evidentiary weight.
- The document shows minimal redaction, but also contains minimal detail — key fields such as aircraft tail number are blank.
- 'Unidentified' means the FAA did not identify the lights; it does not imply any particular origin or explanation.
