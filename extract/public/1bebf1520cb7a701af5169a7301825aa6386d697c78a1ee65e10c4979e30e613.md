# Delta Crew Reports Lights Above Their Jet Over Arkansas

**FAA · 2023**

> An FAA incident report records that the crew of Delta flight 722, an Airbus A321 flying from Las Vegas to Atlanta, spotted an unidentified aerial phenomenon above their aircraft at roughly 35,000 feet over Fayetteville, Arkansas on 14 January 2023. The pilot described what appeared to be two aircraft with red and white lights at very high altitude; the report is preliminary and includes no corroborating radar data.

This document is a preliminary incident report from the FAA's Washington Operations Center (reference WOC 7-3333 DJ/RC). It records a sighting reported by the crew of Delta Airlines flight 722 — an Airbus A321 operating the Las Vegas to Atlanta route — on 14 January 2023 at 04:30 CST.

At the time of the sighting, the aircraft was flying eastbound at flight level 350 (approximately 35,000 feet) over the Razorback VORTAC navigation point near Fayetteville, Arkansas (coordinates 36.005°N, 94.170°W). The pilot reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon above and to the left of the aircraft, at the 11 o'clock position.

The pilot described the UAP as appearing to be two aircraft with red and white lights at a very high altitude. The report does not include any radar data, additional witness accounts, or follow-up analysis that might identify or rule out what the objects were.

The document is marked as preliminary information and does not represent a final determination by the FAA. There is little evidence of redaction in the record. No conclusion is drawn about the nature of the objects — the pilot's own description suggests they may have been conventional aircraft at higher altitude, though this is not confirmed either way.

## What to know

- This is a single-source report based solely on the pilot's visual observation — no radar data or independent witnesses are cited.
- The pilot's own words ('appeared to be two aircraft with red and white lights') suggest the objects could have been conventional high-altitude air traffic. The report neither confirms nor rules this out.
- The document is marked as preliminary information and may not reflect any final FAA finding.
- There is no indication of significant redaction, but the brevity of the report means limited detail is available.
- A pre-dawn sighting at high altitude can make visual identification of distant lighted objects difficult, which should be factored into any assessment.
