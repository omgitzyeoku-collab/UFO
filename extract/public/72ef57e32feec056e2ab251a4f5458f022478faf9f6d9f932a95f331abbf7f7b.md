# UPS Cargo Crew Reported White Lights Over Maryland in 2023

**FAA · 2023**

> This FAA incident report documents a sighting by the crew of UPS flight 9765, a Boeing 767, who observed a string of white lights at an unknown altitude while flying at 36,000 feet near Hagerstown, Maryland on 14 June 2023. The FAA queried whether the lights were Starlink satellites but reached no conclusion, and the case was closed without a stated resolution.

On 14 June 2023 at 03:09, the crew of UPS flight 9765 — a Boeing 767 cargo aircraft flying from Bradley International Airport in Connecticut to Louisville, Kentucky — reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon to Washington Center (ZDC), the FAA facility managing their airspace. The aircraft was westbound at 36,000 feet, approximately 20 nautical miles west of Hagerstown, Maryland.

The crew described the phenomenon as a string of white lights travelling at an unknown altitude. No estimate of the object's speed, size, or distance was recorded in the report.

FAA headquarters queried whether the lights could have been Starlink satellites — a common explanation for linear formations of lights in the night sky. The determination was inconclusive: the satellite explanation was neither confirmed nor ruled out.

The case is marked as closed. The report contains no mention of radar data, sensor recordings, or corroborating observations from other aircraft or ground stations. No follow-up investigation is described.

## What to know

- This is a single-source report based solely on the flight crew's visual observation — no radar, sensor, or corroborating data is mentioned.
- The Starlink satellite explanation was inconclusive, not disproven. 'Inconclusive' does not mean the phenomenon was anomalous.
- No altitude, speed, or size estimate for the observed lights was provided, limiting any independent analysis.
- The case was closed with no stated resolution or follow-up investigation.
- The report shows low evidence of redaction, but minimal detail was recorded in the first place.
