# Pentagon Office Attributes 2013 Puerto Rico UFO Video to Sky Lanterns

**OSD · 2023**

> AARO, the Pentagon's UFO investigation office, formally resolved the well-known 2013 'Puerto Rico Object' infrared video in March 2025, concluding with moderate confidence that the two objects were sky lanterns drifting at wind speed. The office found no anomalous behavior, attributing the apparent high speed and ocean entry to optical illusions caused by the moving aircraft and thermal effects on the infrared sensor.

On the night of April 26, 2013, at 9:22 p.m. local time, an infrared sensor aboard a U.S. Customs and Border Protection aircraft recorded two small objects drifting near Rafael Hernandez Airport in Aguadilla, Puerto Rico. The objects, estimated at less than one metre across, were floating at roughly 656 feet altitude. The video circulated publicly for years and became one of the most discussed UAP cases, with some analysts claiming the objects moved at high speed and plunged into the ocean.

In March 2025 — twelve years after the incident — AARO (the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office, the Pentagon body responsible for investigating unidentified anomalous phenomena) published its formal case resolution. Using trajectory reconstruction software called STK and pixel-level analysis of the footage, AARO calculated the objects were moving at approximately 8 mph in a straight line, consistent with the recorded wind speed of 9.8 mph from the east-northeast. The apparent high speed seen in the video, AARO concluded, was an illusion caused by motion parallax — the camera platform itself was moving, making slow-drifting objects appear fast.

AARO also addressed the dramatic moment in the video where the objects appear to dive into the Atlantic Ocean. According to the reconstruction, the objects stayed over land the entire time and never entered the water. The apparent disappearance was caused by 'thermal crossover' — the recording took place about 90 minutes after sunset, within a window when an infrared sensor can lose the ability to distinguish warm objects from their background. As the aircraft climbed and its distance from the objects nearly tripled, sensor quality degraded further.

The office assessed with moderate confidence that the objects were a pair of sky lanterns, noting that local hotels and resorts in the Aguadilla area commonly release sky lanterns during celebrations. AARO also considered and rejected alternative explanations including marine birds and mylar balloons. With high confidence, the office stated the objects did not exhibit anomalous behaviour or transmedium capabilities.

Notably, an AARO partner organisation had previously estimated the objects' speed at between 35 and 130 mph — a figure sharply at odds with AARO's own 8 mph calculation. The report also carries a disclaimer that AARO is not a member of the intelligence community and the document should not be treated as finished intelligence.

## What to know

- The sky lanterns explanation is given at moderate confidence, not high — AARO acknowledged that poor video quality prevented categorical identification of the objects.
- There is a significant disagreement between AARO's speed estimate (8 mph) and a partner agency's estimate (35–130 mph), reflecting genuine analytical uncertainty about the footage.
- The video had been publicly available for years before this official analysis; independent analysts reached different conclusions about the objects' speed, trajectory, and behaviour.
- The incident occurred in 2013 but was not formally resolved until March 2025 — a twelve-year gap with no explanation provided for the delay.
- AARO states explicitly that it is not part of the intelligence community and this report should not be considered finished intelligence.
