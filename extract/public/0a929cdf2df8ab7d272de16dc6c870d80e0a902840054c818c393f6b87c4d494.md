# Alaska Airlines Pilot Reported Slow-Moving Light at 40,000–50,000 Feet

**FAA · 2023**

> This is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report from February 2019 in which an Alaska Airlines pilot flying from Los Angeles to Seattle reported a slow-moving object with a white flashing light at an estimated 40,000–50,000 feet. Seattle Center radar picked up a primary target at the reported location showing just 20 knots, and the Western Air Defense Sector was notified, but the report was closed with no identification or explanation.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report filed under record number 237UAP00037. It records a UFO sighting made by the pilot-in-command of Alaska Airlines flight 428, a Boeing 737-900 operating from Los Angeles (LAX) to Seattle (SEA). The reporting facility was Seattle Center (ZSE), the FAA's air traffic control centre for that region.

While flying southbound at approximately 35,000 feet (flight level 350), the pilot reported seeing an object with a white flashing light moving slowly to the northeast. The pilot estimated the object was at 40,000 to 50,000 feet — above typical commercial airline cruising altitude. A Seattle Center controller then observed a primary radar target in the same area showing a groundspeed of just 20 knots, which is unusually slow for anything at that altitude.

The Western Air Defense Sector (WADS), the military unit responsible for air defence over the western United States, was notified of the sighting. Despite this, the report contains no follow-up investigation details, no identification of the object, and no resolution. The case is simply marked as closed.

The document itself is brief and administrative in nature. It contains no photographs, no detailed radar data, and no witness statements beyond the pilot's initial report. There is little evidence of redaction, but equally little evidence that any further inquiry took place.

## What to know

- This is a single-source pilot report. No other aircrew or ground observers are recorded as having seen the object.
- Radar showed a primary target only — meaning no transponder return, which rules out most conventional aircraft but does not confirm what the object was.
- A 20-knot groundspeed at 40,000–50,000 feet is extremely unusual. It could indicate a near-stationary object, a radar artefact, or unusual atmospheric conditions affecting the radar return.
- The report was closed with no identification, no follow-up, and no explanation — which is standard for many FAA SKYWATCH filings but means no conclusion was reached.
- The incident date in the document is 10 February 2019, though the record was released as part of a 2023-era FAA UAP disclosure batch.
