# Military Video Shows Unidentified Spherical Object Over CENTCOM Region

**Department of War · 2021 · CENTCOM**

> This is a nearly five-minute military video from 2021 showing a spherical unidentified object tracked by infrared and standard cameras in the U.S. Central Command area of operations. It was uploaded to a classified network in 2024 and later released after eight members of Congress requested access to UAP-related records.

In 2021, a U.S. military platform operating in the Central Command (CENTCOM) area of responsibility — a region covering the Middle East and parts of Central and South Asia — recorded a roughly four-minute, fifty-second video using both a standard camera and an infrared sensor. The video shows what the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) describes as an "area of contrast" moving across the sensor's field of view from right to left, while the operator pans and zooms to track it. The uploader titled the file "Spherical UAP," though AARO's own language stops short of confirming the object is a physical sphere — it could be a sensor artefact.

The video was uploaded to a classified network in June 2024. It came to public attention after eight members of the U.S. House of Representatives formally requested access to 51 potentially UAP-related records on 6 March 2026. AARO identified this video as one of the responsive materials held on classified systems.

AARO notes that many of the materials in this collection lack a substantiated chain of custody, meaning there is no fully verified record of who handled the video between capture and upload. The callsign in the title — which would identify the observing platform or unit — is redacted. AARO also uses the word "likely" when describing the video's origin, indicating this is an assessment rather than a confirmed fact.

The official description of the video's content covers only the first two minutes and four seconds. What happens in the remaining two minutes and forty-six seconds is undescribed in the released materials.

## What to know

- AARO calls the object an "area of contrast," not a confirmed physical object — it could be a sensor artefact rather than something solid.
- The word "likely" is used to describe the video's origin, meaning AARO has not confirmed exactly which platform captured it.
- Many materials in this collection lack a substantiated chain of custody, so the video's authenticity and handling history are not fully verified.
- The callsign identifying the observing platform or military unit is redacted, limiting independent verification.
- Nearly half the video (the final 2 minutes 46 seconds) has no official description — we do not know what it shows.
