# Commercial Pilot Reports Small Flashing Object Ten Feet From Aircraft

**FAA · 2023**

> This FAA SKYWATCH incident report documents a Republic Airways pilot's sighting of a small circular object with flashing white lights that passed approximately 10 feet from the nose of their aircraft at 33,000 feet. The case was closed with no follow-up investigation indicated.

This document is an FAA (Federal Aviation Administration) SKYWATCH incident report — a standardised form used to log pilot-reported sightings of unidentified aerial phenomena. It is filed under case number 237UAP00135 and references MOR ID ZOB-M-2022/12/17-0001, linking it to the Cleveland Air Route Traffic Control Center (ZOB).

On 17 December 2022, the crew of Republic Airways flight RPA3455, a commercial operator, reported an unidentified object while flying eastbound at FL330 (approximately 33,000 feet). The pilot described a small circular object with flashing white lights that appeared roughly 10 feet off the nose of the aircraft. The object then moved away, departing the field of view below and to the right.

The crew did not take evasive action, and no law enforcement agency was notified. The case is marked as closed, with no indication of any follow-up investigation or resolution.

Several place codes appear in the report — DEN (Denver), ORD (Chicago O'Hare), ROC (Rochester), and LFD — though the specific incident location is not stated. No tail number was recorded for the reporting aircraft, which is unusual for an aviation incident report.

## What to know

- This is a single-source report based entirely on the pilot's visual observation. No radar data, photographs, or other sensor evidence is included.
- The estimated distance of 10 feet is a visual guess made at night at 33,000 feet altitude — such estimates can be highly unreliable.
- The case is marked 'Closed' with no indication that any follow-up investigation took place and no explanation for the object.
- No aircraft tail number is recorded, which is atypical for an FAA incident report and limits independent verification.
- The document shows minimal redaction, but it also contains minimal information — the brevity itself is notable.
