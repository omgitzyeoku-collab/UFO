# Airline Crew Reports Three Bright Lights Over Nebraska at 39,000 Feet

**FAA · 2023**

> This FAA preliminary report documents a sighting by the crew of a commercial flight travelling from Las Vegas to Minneapolis on 4 October 2023. The crew reported three stationary bright white lights blinking in sequence off the left side of their aircraft at approximately 39,000 feet over western Nebraska. No evasive action was taken and no follow-up investigation is included in the document.

This document is a preliminary incident report from the FAA's Washington Operations Center, filed after a commercial airline crew reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon (UAP) during a routine flight.

On 4 October 2023 at 3:25 AM Mountain Daylight Time, the crew of a Boeing 737-900 operating from Las Vegas to Minneapolis reported seeing three stationary bright white lights from the left side of their aircraft. The lights were described as blinking left to right and right to left. The aircraft was at Flight Level 390 — roughly 39,000 feet — approximately 83 nautical miles northeast of Scottsbluff, Nebraska. Denver Air Route Traffic Control Center (ARTCC) was advised of the sighting.

The crew took no evasive action in response to the lights, and no law enforcement notification was reported. The report contains only the initial notification passed through the FAA's operations centre; no radar data, sensor recordings, or corroborating witness accounts are attached.

The FAA did not reach any conclusion about what the lights were. This is a preliminary report only — no follow-up investigation or resolution is documented. The record stands as a single data point: one flight crew's observation, logged through standard FAA channels.

## What to know

- This is a single-source report from one flight crew. No corroborating radar data, sensor recordings, or independent witness accounts are included.
- The document is a preliminary notification only — no follow-up investigation or resolution was conducted, at least as far as this record shows.
- The described lights — stationary, blinking in sequence — could be consistent with various conventional explanations (e.g. ground lights, atmospheric phenomena, other aircraft) that are not explored in the report.
- No redactions are evident in this document; the limited detail reflects the nature of a preliminary incident form rather than deliberate withholding.
- The aircraft type (Boeing 737-900) and route are taken from abbreviated codes in the report (B739, LAS–MSP) and carry moderate rather than absolute confidence.
