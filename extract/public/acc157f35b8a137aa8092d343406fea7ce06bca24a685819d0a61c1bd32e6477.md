# Multiple Pilots Reported Unidentified Lights Over Nebraska in 2024

**FAA · 2023**

> This FAA Washington Operations Center notification documents a UAP sighting reported by a NetJets flight and corroborated by multiple other aircraft near McCook, Nebraska on 2 March 2024. The rapidly moving lights were not detected on air traffic control radar.

This document is an incident notification from the FAA Washington Operations Center — essentially an internal alert that something unusual was reported in US airspace. It is not a full investigation or a final conclusion about what was seen.

On 2 March 2024 at approximately 03:56 CST, NetJets flight 316 — a light jet flying from Las Vegas to Omaha at 41,000 feet — reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon near McCook, Nebraska. The crew described rapidly moving dim and bright lights. Denver ARTCC (Air Route Traffic Control Center), the regional air traffic control facility, was advised of the report.

Notably, multiple other aircraft in the area reported seeing similar phenomena, giving the sighting more than a single-source basis. However, the lights were not observed on any ATC radar systems, meaning there is no independent electronic corroboration of a physical object.

The document contains no follow-up investigation, no identification of the phenomenon, and no conclusion from the FAA about what the lights were. It is a preliminary operational report, not an analytical product.

## What to know

- This is a preliminary FAA operations alert, not a completed investigation — no conclusion was reached about what the lights were.
- The phenomenon was visually observed only; it did not appear on air traffic control radar, so there is no independent sensor corroboration.
- Pilot observations of lights at night can be affected by atmospheric conditions, distance estimation errors, and other optical phenomena.
- Multiple aircraft reported similar sightings, which adds witness weight but does not confirm a physical object was present.
