# Pilot Reports Illuminated Unidentified Object Near Alaska Volcano in 2023

**FAA · 2023**

> This FAA incident report documents a pilot sighting of an unidentified, well-illuminated object near Mt. Augustine, Alaska, on 9 March 2023. The pilot was flying at 14,000 feet, saw the object slightly below the aircraft, and lost sight of it in twilight. The case is closed with no explanation offered.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report — a standard form the Federal Aviation Administration uses to log unusual aerial sightings reported by pilots. It covers an event on 9 March 2023 at 05:01 UTC, involving a BE10 aircraft flying under the callsign TN479AK. The flight originated from Port Heiden (PTH) and was headed to Merrill Field (MRI) in Anchorage.

While flying northeast at 14,000 feet over coordinates 59.30N, 153.23W — in the vicinity of Mt. Augustine, a volcano in Alaska's Cook Inlet — the pilot reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon off the right side of the aircraft. The object was described as "well illuminated but undefined" and positioned slightly below the plane. No other air traffic was reported in the area at the time.

The pilot-in-command lost visual contact with the object in twilight conditions. The FAA's Alaska Region (ANR) was notified. The report is assigned MOR ID ZAN-M-2023/03/08-0001.

The case status is marked as Closed. The report contains no follow-up investigation, no radar or sensor data, and no conclusion about what the object was. There is minimal redaction in the document.

## What to know

- This is a single-source report based entirely on one pilot's visual observation. No radar data, photographs, or corroborating sensor evidence is mentioned.
- The description "well illuminated but undefined" is subjective. It tells us what the pilot perceived but does not constitute technical evidence of any particular object.
- Twilight conditions can affect visual perception, potentially contributing to difficulty identifying distant or unusual light sources.
- The case is marked Closed with no stated conclusion — this does not mean the object was identified or that it remains unexplained. It simply means the FAA did not pursue it further.
- There is no indication of heavy redaction in this document, but the report is brief and contains limited detail overall.
