# Three Aircraft Report Bright Circling Lights Near Birmingham, Alabama

**FAA · 2023**

> An FAA operations center report from May 2023 documents a medevac pilot and two other aircraft independently observing bright lights changing in intensity and circling counter-clockwise at high altitude near Birmingham, Alabama. The document is a preliminary incident log with no follow-up investigation or identification of the phenomenon.

On 2 May 2023 at 1:21 a.m. Central Time, the FAA's Washington Operations Center logged an unidentified aerial phenomenon near Birmingham, Alabama. The report originated from Birmingham TRACON, the regional air traffic control facility.

The primary witness was the crew of a medevac Cessna C550 (callsign LN10UH) flying from Pensacola to Birmingham. They reported seeing bright lights off the front of the aircraft that were changing in intensity and circling in a counter-clockwise pattern at high altitude.

Two additional aircraft independently reported the same lights in the same location, though the document provides no details about those aircraft — no callsigns, altitudes, or flight paths are recorded.

The FAA document is a short operational incident report, not an investigation. It records what was reported but offers no analysis, no identification of the lights, and no follow-up conclusions. The report is filed under reference WOC 7-3333.

Notably, the document contains a labelling error: the longitude coordinate is marked as 'Latitude' in the original, though the negative value clearly indicates a western longitude near Birmingham. There is minimal redaction in the document.

## What to know

- This is a preliminary operational log, not an investigated case — the FAA recorded the sighting but did not analyse or identify what was seen.
- Three aircraft reported the same lights, which adds weight, but no details about the two corroborating aircraft are included in the document.
- The document contains no imagery, radar data, or sensor readings — it is a text-only incident report.
- No follow-up investigation or final determination is referenced; there is no conclusion about what the lights were.
