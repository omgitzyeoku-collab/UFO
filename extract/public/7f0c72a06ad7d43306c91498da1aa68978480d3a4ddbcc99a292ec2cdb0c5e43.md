# SkyWest crew reported two unidentified lights north of Austin in 2024

**FAA · 2023**

> This FAA SKYWATCH incident report documents the crew of SkyWest flight SKW3260 observing two maneuvering lights while flying at 37,000 feet near Austin, Texas. The lights were estimated at 100–150 nautical miles north of the aircraft, above its altitude, and were not picked up on air traffic control radar. The case is marked closed with no explanation provided.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report — a standardised form the Federal Aviation Administration uses to log unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP) reported by pilots and air traffic controllers. It covers an observation made on 18 May 2024 by the crew of SkyWest flight SKW3260, operating an E75L regional jet.

According to the report, the crew was flying eastbound at flight level 370 (approximately 37,000 feet) and was about 30 nautical miles west of Austin, Texas. They reported seeing two maneuvering lights off to one side of the aircraft, estimated to be roughly 100–150 nautical miles to the north and above their altitude. At that distance, visual detail would have been extremely limited.

Air traffic control checked their radar and found nothing corresponding to the lights. The report notes that FAA personnel were paged in response to the sighting, but the case is marked as closed with no resolution or follow-up details recorded.

One metadata oddity: the document is dated May 2024, but the FAA's own release index lists the incident year as 2023. Whether this reflects a filing delay or a data entry error is unclear from the record itself.

## What to know

- This is a single-source visual observation by one flight crew — no radar, infrared, or other sensor data corroborates the sighting.
- At an estimated distance of 100–150 nautical miles, reliable visual identification of any object is essentially impossible.
- The report is marked closed with no stated conclusion — the FAA did not explain the lights or rule out conventional explanations.
- There is a discrepancy between the document date (May 2024) and the officially listed incident year (2023), which is unexplained.
- No tail number was recorded for the reporting aircraft, limiting independent verification of the flight details.
