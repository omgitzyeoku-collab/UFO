# American Airlines Pilot Reports Formation of Colour-Changing Lights Near Nashville

**FAA · 2023**

> This FAA incident report documents an American Airlines pilot on flight AAL2399 observing red, green, and white lights flying in formation above the aircraft at 35,000 feet near Nashville on 1 February 2023. The case was closed with no explanation provided.

On 1 February 2023, the crew of American Airlines flight AAL2399, a commercial flight from Las Vegas to Charlotte, reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon to air traffic control. The aircraft was eastbound at flight level 350 (35,000 feet), approximately 10 nautical miles west of Nashville, Tennessee, when the crew spotted something off the left side of the aircraft.

The pilot described red, green, and white lights above the aircraft's altitude, travelling in formation. The lights were changing colours and brightness and appeared over Bowling Green. A UPS cargo flight (UPS3721, an Airbus A306) was noted as being 15 nautical miles ahead at a lower altitude of 29,000 feet on a route to Louisville, though no corroborating observation from that crew is included in the report.

The sighting was reported through the Memphis Air Route Traffic Control Centre (Memphis ARTCC, facility code ZME), and a Mandatory Occurrence Report was filed under ID ZME-M-2023/02/01-0001. This is part of the FAA's SKYWATCH reporting system for unidentified aerial phenomena.

The case status is listed as closed. No resolution, identification, or explanation for what the pilot observed is provided in the document. No radar data, photographs, or sensor evidence accompanies the report — the entire record rests on the pilot's visual observation.

## What to know

- This is a single-source pilot report with no corroboration from the nearby UPS flight or any other observer.
- No radar data, photographs, or sensor evidence is included — the report relies entirely on one visual observation.
- The case was closed with no resolution or explanation provided by the FAA.
- The lights described (red, green, and white, changing brightness) could be consistent with conventional aircraft navigation lights seen under unusual atmospheric conditions, though the document makes no such determination.
- The incident location is approximate, based on the pilot's estimate of distance from Nashville.
