# CIA Report: Triangular Aircraft Launched Near Baku in 1955

**CIA · November, 1955 · Baku, Azerbaijan**

> A formerly secret CIA information report documents a US corporate executive's eyewitness account of a triangular aircraft being launched from an airfield near Baku, Azerbaijan in October 1955. The object spiralled into the sky at extreme speed while a Soviet security officer ordered the train's window blinds shut.

This CIA information report, originally classified SECRET, records the debriefing of a 41-year-old American corporate executive who witnessed an unusual aircraft launch while travelling by train through Soviet Azerbaijan in 1955. The observer — a vice president of publicity and advertising with a political science background but no aviation training — was visiting the USSR as a tourist at the invitation of a senior Soviet official.

On 4 October 1955, the witness boarded a train in Baku bound for Tiflis (now Tbilisi) with three other US nationals. Roughly two hours and forty minutes after departure, he spotted a large airfield between the railway and the Caspian Sea coast, approximately 50 to 65 miles south of Baku. A powerful searchlight illuminated a triangular object on the ground. He described it as squat, shaped like an equilateral triangle, roughly the size of a US jet fighter, with three lights — one at each point of the triangle.

The object was then launched in what the witness described as a missile-like ejection rather than a normal takeoff. It made between three and seven rapid spirals before climbing at roughly 45 degrees at extreme speed, tracked by the searchlight throughout. A companion in the next compartment reported seeing a second launch in rapid succession. Shortly after, a Soviet MVD (Ministry of Internal Affairs) officer who had been aboard since departure ordered all window blinds drawn.

The CIA filed this as unevaluated information — meaning the agency recorded the account but did not assess whether it was accurate. The witness's identity and some distribution details are redacted. Notably, the group had been told by INTOURIST (the Soviet state travel agency) in Baku that no flights existed between Baku and Tiflis, but upon arrival in Tiflis, INTOURIST staff there said several flights operated daily — raising the possibility the group was deliberately routed by train past the area, or prevented from overflying it.

## What to know

- Single-source report: every detail comes from one civilian witness who stated he had no technical training or experience in aviation matters.
- The sighting occurred at night. The witness said the evening was dark but clear, which limits visual precision for estimating shape, size, and distance.
- The CIA marked this report 'THIS IS UNEVALUATED INFORMATION' — the agency did not verify the account or draw any conclusions about what the object was.
- The witness's identity is redacted, and some distribution details are also removed, making independent verification difficult.
- Distance and size estimates are approximate: the observer initially said five miles and later revised to two miles during debriefing.
