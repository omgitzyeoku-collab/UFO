# FAA Report: Aircraft Struck Silver Balloon at 11,000 Feet in 2021

**FAA · 2023**

> This FAA SKYWATCH incident report documents a commercial aircraft (callsign GJS4582, a CRJ7 regional jet) striking a silver balloon at 11,000 feet over BWZ on 24 July 2021. The object was classified as an "Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon" but the report itself identifies it as a balloon, and no damage or injuries resulted.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report filed under the primary code "Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon." It records an event on 24 July 2021 at 17:28 UTC in which a CRJ7 regional jet, callsign GJS4582, struck an object while flying from Detroit (DTW) to Newark (EWR) at 11,000 feet over the BWZ navigational fix.

The report describes the object as a silver balloon travelling upward at approximately 11,000 feet. Despite the UAP classification, the report parenthetically identifies the object as a balloon — the "unidentified" label appears to be procedural rather than an indication of a genuinely unknown object.

No damage to the aircraft and no injuries were reported. The case status is listed as closed.

The report is a single-page administrative record. It contains no radar data, no photographs, and no corroborating witness statements beyond the flight crew's account. The processing office is listed as DEN (Denver), though the incident occurred in northeastern US airspace — this likely refers to an administrative centre, not the location of the event.

## What to know

- The object was identified within the report itself as a balloon — the UAP classification is procedural, not an assertion that the object was unexplained.
- This is a single-source FAA incident report with no accompanying radar data, photographs, or independent witness statements.
- The processing office code DEN (Denver) does not indicate the incident location; the strike occurred over BWZ in the northeastern US.
- Redaction levels are low — the document appears to be released largely intact.
- The record is a routine aviation safety filing, not an intelligence assessment or investigation into unknown phenomena.
