# Pilot Reports Bright Objects Above Aircraft at 34,000 Feet

**FAA · 2023**

> An FAA SKYWATCH incident report documents a January 2011 sighting by the pilot of flight EGF3892, a Canadair CRJ7, who observed small luminous bright objects above the aircraft at approximately 34,000 feet. Two other aircraft in the area were asked if they saw anything; neither confirmed the sighting. The case is closed.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report — a standardised form the Federal Aviation Administration uses to log unusual sightings reported by pilots. It records an event from 26 January 2011 at 17:26 UTC, involving flight EGF3892, a Canadair Challenger 870 (CRJ7) operating from Chicago O'Hare (ORD) toward Philadelphia (PHL).

While passing through the Fort Wayne (FWA) sector at flight level 340 (roughly 34,000 feet), the pilot reported seeing small luminous bright objects above the aircraft, positioned at the 2 o'clock direction — that is, ahead and to the right. The FAA classified the report under its primary code "OTHER," meaning no specific category was assigned.

Controllers queried two other aircraft in the vicinity, one at flight level 310 and another at flight level 410, but neither crew saw anything matching the description. The report's status is marked as closed, with no indication of further investigation or resolution.

Although the document carries a 2023 release reference number (237UAP00014), the incident itself dates to 2011 — well before the current UAP reporting framework was established. It was released as part of a batch of records through the National Archives.

## What to know

- This is a single-witness observation. The pilot was the only person who reported seeing the objects; crews on two nearby aircraft saw nothing.
- The FAA labelled this report "OTHER" and closed it. There is no recorded explanation, identification, or follow-up investigation in the document.
- The term "UFO" appears in the original report in its literal sense — an unidentified flying object — not as a claim about the object's origin or nature.
- The incident occurred in January 2011 but surfaced in a 2023 National Archives release batch, so it predates the current US government UAP reporting and investigation framework.
- Redaction levels are low, but the document contains limited detail — there is no radar data, imagery, or technical analysis included.
