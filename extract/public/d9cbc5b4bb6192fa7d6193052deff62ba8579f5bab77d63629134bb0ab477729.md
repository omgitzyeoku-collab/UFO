# FAA Report Documents Large Balloon Sighted at Extreme Altitude in 2023

**FAA · 2023**

> This FAA incident report records a general aviation pilot observing a large balloon at approximately 50,000 feet while flying westbound near BQS on 3 February 2023. The sighting was classified under the FAA's "Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon" code and the case was closed with no resolution or follow-up details provided.

On 3 February 2023 at 17:20 UTC, a general aviation aircraft with callsign N7771HM reported spotting a large balloon while flying westbound at FL400 (roughly 40,000 feet), approximately 20 nautical miles north of BQS. The pilot estimated the balloon was at FL500 — around 50,000 feet — or higher. That altitude is well above the ceiling used by commercial airliners.

The FAA logged the sighting through its SKYWATCH incident reporting system and classified it under the primary code "Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon." That classification does not necessarily mean the object was anomalous or unexplained — it is a reporting category used when an observed object has not been positively identified at the time of filing.

The report lists the case status as closed, but provides no details about any follow-up investigation, identification of the object, or explanation for the closure. No radar data or sensor corroboration is included in the document.

The timing is notable: this sighting occurred in early February 2023, the same period when the US military was tracking and ultimately shooting down a Chinese high-altitude surveillance balloon and several other airborne objects. That context may have heightened reporting sensitivity across US airspace, though this document does not draw any connection to those events.

The document shows minimal redaction. It is a short administrative record rather than a detailed investigative file, and contains only the pilot's initial observation as relayed to air traffic control.

## What to know

- This is a single-source pilot observation with no corroborating radar, sensor, or photographic evidence included in the report.
- The FAA's "Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon" classification is a reporting code, not a conclusion that the object was unexplained or anomalous.
- The case is marked closed but no resolution, identification, or follow-up details are provided — we do not know whether the object was later identified.
- The sighting coincided with the February 2023 Chinese surveillance balloon incidents, which prompted heightened vigilance and reporting across US airspace.
- FL500 (approximately 50,000 feet) is an estimate from the pilot, not a confirmed measurement — altitude judgements by visual observation alone carry significant uncertainty.
