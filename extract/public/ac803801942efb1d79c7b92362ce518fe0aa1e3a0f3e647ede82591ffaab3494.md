# Two Pilots Tracked Unidentified Lit Object for 20 Minutes Without Radar Contact

**FAA · 2023**

> This FAA SKYWATCH report documents a Christmas Eve 2010 incident in which a SkyWest Airlines pilot and a second independent aircraft both observed an unidentified object with coloured lights near Wink, Texas, for 15–20 minutes at high altitude. Neither FAA radar facilities nor military agencies (NORAD, CONR, AMOC) detected anything in the area, and the case was closed without explanation.

On 24 December 2010, the pilot of SkyWest Airlines flight SKW4525 — a Canadair Challenger 890 regional jet — reported an unidentified object to Fort Worth Center (ZFW), the FAA's air traffic control facility for the region. The object had been matching the aircraft's speed, heading, and altitude of 36,000 feet for 15 to 20 minutes, approximately 40 miles west of the WINK VOR navigational point in West Texas. The pilot described alternating red, white, and green lights on the object.

A second aircraft, a Beechcraft registered as N449TM, independently corroborated the sighting. That pilot reported the object appeared to be at their own altitude of 41,000 feet and additionally displayed a flashing yellow light. The two aircraft were at different altitudes, meaning the object was observed from two separate vantage points.

Despite two independent pilot reports, no corresponding radar target was detected. Neither Fort Worth Center nor FAA headquarters in Denver observed anything on radar. The FAA also contacted the Continental U.S. NORAD Region (CONR), the North American Aerospace Defense Command (NORAD), and the Air and Marine Operations Center (AMOC) — all confirmed they detected nothing in the area.

At 1256, both pilots reported to Fort Worth Center that the object had faded and was no longer visible. The case was marked "Closed" with no identification or resolution recorded.

The document is filed under FAA record 237UAP00013 with a 2023 date, but the incident itself dates to 2010. The 2023 date likely reflects when the record was compiled for the PURSUE public release.

## What to know

- Two independent pilots at different altitudes corroborate each other, but no radar or military sensor data supports the sighting — the object produced no detectable return on any system.
- The object's described lights (red, white, green) are consistent with standard aircraft navigation lights, though an aircraft matching speed and altitude for 15–20 minutes without any radar signature would be unusual.
- The case is marked 'Closed' with no identification or explanation recorded — this means unsolved, not confirmed as anomalous.
