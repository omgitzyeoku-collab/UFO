# United Airlines Crew Reports Stationary Blinking Lights at 35,000 Feet

**FAA · 2023**

> This FAA incident report documents a United Airlines crew observing three bright white stationary lights blinking in sequence while flying from San Francisco to Chicago on 4 October 2023. The case was closed with no identification or explanation recorded.

On 4 October 2023, the crew of United Airlines flight 476 — a commercial service from San Francisco (SFO) to Chicago O'Hare (ORD) — reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon to air traffic control. The aircraft was cruising at FL350, or 35,000 feet, when the crew spotted something off the left side of the plane.

What they described was three bright white lights, stationary and not changing altitude, blinking in a left-to-right and right-to-left pattern. The sighting occurred approximately 42 nautical miles northeast of Western Nebraska Regional Airport (BFF), near Scottsbluff — a remote area of western Nebraska. The incident was reported to Denver Air Route Traffic Control Center (ZDV), and a Mandatory Occurrence Report was initiated under the reference ZDV-M-2023/10/04-0001.

The FAA logged the event through its SKYWATCH incident reporting system. The case status is marked as "Closed," but the report contains no resolution, identification, or explanation of what the lights were. No corroborating radar data or additional witness accounts are included in the document.

Notably, no aircraft tail number was recorded — unusual for an aviation incident report. The report is a single-source account based entirely on the flight crew's visual observation.

## What to know

- This is a single-source report based solely on the aircrew's visual observation. No radar data, photographs, or independent witness accounts are included.
- The case is marked 'Closed' with no identification or explanation provided — closure does not mean the phenomenon was identified.
- No tail number was recorded for the aircraft, which is unusual for an FAA incident report.
- The sighting occurred in a remote area of western Nebraska with limited ground-based observation opportunities, making independent corroboration unlikely.
- The document shows low evidence of redaction, but the absence of detail (no resolution, no corroborating data) limits what conclusions can be drawn.
