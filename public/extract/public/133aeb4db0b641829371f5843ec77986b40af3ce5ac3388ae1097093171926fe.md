# Pentagon Office Briefing Outlines Its Mission to Track Unidentified Phenomena

**OSD · 2023**

> This is an unclassified briefing deck from the All-Domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), the Pentagon's dedicated UAP unit, presented by its director Dr. Seán Kirkpatrick. It explains AARO's mission, calls for better reporting from military and civilian aviators, and flags national security concerns about unidentified phenomena near US military sites — but discusses no specific incidents or evidence.

In early 2023, Dr. Seán Kirkpatrick, director of the All-Domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), prepared a briefing deck explaining what his office does and why it exists. AARO sits under the US Department of Defense and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence. Its job is to detect, track, and resolve what the government calls unidentified anomalous phenomena — unexplained observations in the air, at sea, in space, or crossing between those domains. The document was cleared for public release on 4 January 2023.

The briefing makes clear that most Defence Department UAP sightings occur near US military facilities and operating areas. It warns that UAP near strategic capabilities could threaten deterrence and civil safety, and notes a separate concern: that adversaries might wrongly blame unexplained phenomena on the United States. Key partners listed include the Department of Justice, NASA, the FAA, the Department of Energy, and the nuclear security agency NNSA.

A recurring theme is the reporting problem. The briefing states that historical reluctance to report UAP has limited the government's ability to address aerial safety and security threats. It credits recent efforts to encourage military aviators to come forward with substantially increasing both the quantity and quality of UAP data. AARO says it is working with military, civil, and industry partners to build better reporting channels.

Notably, the document contains no specific UAP cases, no photographic or sensor evidence, and no conclusions about what any particular sighting was. It is an organisational overview — a pitch for cooperation and data sharing, not an intelligence assessment.

## What to know

- This is a public-facing briefing deck, not an operational or intelligence report. It describes AARO's goals and structure, not specific UAP incidents or findings.
- The document was cleared for open publication on 4 January 2023, meaning any sensitive or classified material was removed before release.
- No specific UAP cases, sensor data, or evidence of any kind are discussed — the content is entirely organisational and procedural.
- No redactions were noted in the released version, but that reflects pre-release sanitisation rather than full transparency.
- Both allies and strategic competitors are noted as applying resources to observe and attribute UAP, but no details are given about what any party has found.
