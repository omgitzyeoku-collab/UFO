# Military Video Shows Unidentified Object Tracked Over CENTCOM Region

**Department of War · 2020 · CENTCOM**

> This is a 4-minute 44-second video recorded by a U.S. military sensor platform in the Central Command (CENTCOM) area of responsibility on 16 September 2020, showing a tracked unidentified aerial phenomenon. The video was released after eight members of the U.S. House of Representatives requested access to 51 potentially UAP-related records in March 2026.

On 16 September 2020, a U.S. military platform operating in the CENTCOM area of responsibility — a region covering the Middle East and parts of Central and South Asia — recorded a video of an unidentified aerial phenomenon using a full-motion video sensor. The recording runs 4 minutes and 44 seconds. The first 1 minute and 17 seconds contain no content. At the 1:18 mark, an area of contrast enters the bottom of the frame. The sensor pans, zooms, and attempts to hold the object in the centre of the frame as it moves.

The video was uploaded to a classified network in September 2020. It was later identified by the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) as part of a collection of potentially UAP-related materials. On 6 March 2026, eight members of the U.S. House of Representatives formally requested access to 51 such records, and this video was among the responsive materials AARO located.

The Department of War describes this as a video "likely" derived from a U.S. military platform — notably using the word "likely" rather than confirming the source outright. The callsigns in the document title are redacted, so the specific military unit or aircraft involved is not publicly identified.

Neither AARO nor the Department of War has offered a formal identification or explanation of what the object in the video is. The title's use of "UAP" reflects the uploader's description, not an official classification. The department also notes that many of the materials in this collection lack a substantiated chain of custody, meaning the full history of how this video was recorded, handled, and stored cannot be independently verified.

## What to know

- The Department of War says the video was 'likely' recorded by a U.S. military platform — this is an assessment, not confirmed provenance.
- The materials lack a substantiated chain of custody, meaning the video's origin and handling history cannot be fully verified.
- Callsigns in the title are redacted, so the specific unit, aircraft, or crew involved cannot be identified from public information.
- Available metadata only describes the video up to the 2:25 mark; the content from 2:25 to 4:44 is not described, so roughly half the video's content is uncharacterised in official descriptions.
- The label 'UAP' comes from the person who uploaded the video, not from a formal assessment by AARO or the Department of War.
