# U.S. Embassy Cable Profiles Turkmen UFO Group Turned NGO Partner

**Department of State · 11/5/04 · Turkmenistan**

> A November 2004 unclassified cable from the U.S. Embassy in Ashgabat describes how the Union of UFOlogists of Turkmenabat — originally founded to study extraterrestrial life — became one of America's most reliable civil society partners in Turkmenistan. The cable is not about a UFO sighting; it documents an NGO that used universal local interest in UFOs to maintain good relations with authorities while doing development work.

In November 2004, the American Embassy in Ashgabat sent an unclassified cable (document ID 04 ASHGABAT 1028) reporting on an unusual but effective partner organization: the Union of UFOlogists (UOU) of Turkmenabat, in Turkmenistan's Lebap province. Despite its name, the group had evolved well beyond its original mission of studying extraterrestrial life and had become a workhorse NGO for U.S.-funded programs in the country.

The UOU held a notable distinction — it was the first NGO to register in Turkmenistan after independence in 1992, and the first to successfully reregister under a restrictive 2003 NGO law. By the time of the cable, it had over 1,000 members and at least nine member organizations. During the 1990s, it helped 187 enterprises register, from farmers' unions to a shoe factory, all pro bono. It also partnered with the State Department-funded CHAP humanitarian assistance program, administered by Counterpart International, to distribute aid to refugees from the Tajikistan civil war and Afghan refugees.

At the time of the cable, the UOU was working on an $8,532 grant to help other local NGOs navigate registration requirements. USAID, through Counterpart International, was considering a $15,000 capacity-building grant, and a separate $30,000 proposal for printing equipment to launch a newsletter was under review. On the newsletter, the cable noted a characteristic workaround: since independent media printing was illegal in Turkmenistan and a license was needed for anything over 1,000 copies, the UOU planned to print exactly 999.

Regarding actual UFO activity, the cable is explicit: the UOU's president said there had been no confirmed sightings of UFOs in Turkmenistan, though the Turkmen military and government authorities had consulted him about mysterious occurrences in Turkmen airspace. The cable's author described the group as "crazy like a fox" — leveraging the fact that "everyone is interested in UFOs" to maintain goodwill with local authorities while doing substantive development and civil society work.

The document was released in full by John Powers, Acting Director at the U.S. Department of State, on February 25, 2026. It carries no redactions of substance.

## What to know

- This cable is not about a UAP sighting or encounter. It appears in UAP document collections because of the organization's name, not because of substantive UAP content.
- The cable explicitly states there have been no confirmed UFO sightings in Turkmenistan. The mention of 'mysterious occurrences in Turkmen airspace' is secondhand, brief, and unelaborated.
- The document is unclassified and shows low evidence of redaction, meaning what you see is essentially the full record.
- All claims about the UOU's activities come from the embassy's reporting and the organization's own president — they are single-source and not independently corroborated in this document.
- The cable was released in full in February 2026; it reflects conditions as understood by the U.S. Embassy in late 2004.
