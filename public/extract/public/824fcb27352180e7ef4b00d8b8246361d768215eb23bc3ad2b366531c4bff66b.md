# American Airlines Pilot Reports Mysterious Object Over Iowa City

**FAA · 2023**

> This FAA SKYWATCH report documents an American Airlines crew on flight AAL403 reporting an unidentified aerial phenomenon while flying at 37,000 feet over Iowa City on 11 February 2023. The object appeared to circle at or above their altitude and kept appearing and disappearing; the FAA noted a similar sighting the previous week had been attributed to a satellite train, but the case was closed without a formal conclusion.

On 11 February 2023, the crew of American Airlines flight AAL403 — an Airbus A320 flying from Denver to Philadelphia — reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon to the FAA. The aircraft was eastbound at 37,000 feet over Iowa City, Iowa, when the crew observed something off the left side of their aircraft.

The pilots described the object as appearing to circle at or above their altitude, repeatedly appearing and disappearing. The FAA logged the sighting through its SKYWATCH incident reporting system, which tracks unidentified aerial phenomena reported by pilots.

The FAA's Air Traffic Organization Military Operations Center (AMOC) noted that an identical report had come in the previous week and had been informally attributed to a "train" of satellites — likely a string of Starlink-type satellites visible in low orbit. However, the document does not confirm this explanation applied to this specific sighting.

The case is marked as Closed, but no formal resolution or conclusion is documented. There is no radar data, sensor evidence, or independent corroboration included in the file. The aircraft's tail number field is blank.

## What to know

- This is a single-source pilot report with no accompanying radar data, photographs, or sensor evidence.
- The satellite train explanation comes from a prior, separate sighting — it is not confirmed as the cause of this incident.
- The case is marked Closed but contains no formal resolution or documented conclusion.
- The aircraft tail number field is blank, which limits independent verification of the specific flight.
- The document is an administrative incident log, not an investigation report — it records what was reported, not what was determined.
