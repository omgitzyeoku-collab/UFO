# Pilot Reports Three White Lights at 41,000 Feet on Houston-JFK Flight

**FAA · 2023**

> An FAA SKYWATCH incident report documents a pilot sighting of three white lights in trail formation while flying at 41,000 feet on a Houston-to-JFK route on 15 September 2024. The lights were not detected on air traffic control radar, and the case was closed without identification.

This is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report (record 237UAP00378) documenting an unidentified aerial phenomenon observed by the crew of flight EJA538 on 15 September 2024 at 07:15 UTC. The aircraft was travelling northeast from Houston (HOU) to New York's JFK airport at flight level 410 — roughly 41,000 feet — when the crew reported the sighting.

The pilot described three white lights arranged in trail formation at a slightly higher altitude, visible in the distance. The aircraft was approximately 50 nautical miles east of the VXV navigational fix at the time. The report was logged under MOR ID ZTL-M-2024/09/15-0001, indicating it was processed through the Atlanta Air Route Traffic Control Centre (ZTL).

Critically, the phenomenon was not detected on the ATC facility's radar system, meaning the only evidence is the pilot's visual observation. The FAA closed the case without recording any identification or resolution of what the lights were.

The document carries minimal redaction. It is a straightforward single-page incident log rather than an investigative file — it records what was reported but does not attempt to explain it.

## What to know

- This is a single-source pilot observation with no radar corroboration — the lights did not appear on ATC radar.
- No physical description is given beyond 'three white lights in trail formation.' The distance, size, and speed of the lights are not specified beyond 'in the distance.'
- The case is closed with no resolution or identification noted — the FAA recorded the report but did not determine what the lights were.
- Three lights in trail formation at high altitude could have a range of explanations (satellites, aircraft, atmospheric phenomena) — the report does not rule any in or out.
- The document title references 2023, but the verified incident date in the report is 15 September 2024.
