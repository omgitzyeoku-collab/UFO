# FAA Report: Pilot Saw Stationary Lights Above 37,000 Feet Near Arkansas

**FAA · 2023**

> This is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report documenting a commercial airline pilot's observation of two to three stationary lights appearing and disappearing above flight level 370 (37,000 feet), approximately 58 nautical miles south of Little Rock, Arkansas, on 7 August 2022. No other aircraft confirmed the sighting, and the FAA closed the case without explanation.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report — a formal record the Federal Aviation Administration creates when a pilot reports an unidentified aerial phenomenon (UAP). It was filed under the code 237UAP00098 and categorised with the primary code "Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon."

The report describes an observation by the crew of American Airlines flight AAL2561, a commercial flight. On 7 August 2022 at 06:30, while flying eastbound at 37,000 feet roughly 58 nautical miles south of Little Rock, Arkansas, the crew reported seeing two to three stationary lights that appeared and disappeared above the aircraft's altitude.

The FAA contacted other aircraft in the area, but no other crew observed the lights. The report contains no radar data, sensor readings, or photographs — it rests entirely on the verbal account of one flight crew.

The case is marked "Closed." The FAA provided no resolution, identification, or explanation for what the lights were. There is little evidence of redaction in the document; the information is simply sparse rather than withheld.

## What to know

- This is a single-source report: only the crew of AAL2561 observed the phenomenon. No other aircraft or ground radar corroborated it.
- The report contains no sensor data, photographs, or radar tracks — it is based solely on a verbal pilot account.
- The FAA closed the case without providing any resolution or explanation for what was observed.
- The document shows low evidence of redaction, meaning the limited detail reflects what was recorded rather than what was concealed.
- Flight details such as the callsign and route are verifiable against public flight-tracking records, but the observation itself cannot be independently confirmed from this document alone.
