# FAA Logged Unidentified Lights Above 40,000 Feet Over Missouri in 2009

**FAA · 2023**

> This is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report documenting multiple sightings of unidentified lights at extreme altitude between St. Louis and Springfield, Missouri on 10 September 2009. NORAD and the Continental NORAD Region confirmed no space debris reentry was occurring, and the case was closed with no explanation offered.

On 10 September 2009 at 06:45, the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) received several reports of unidentified lights observed above flight level 400 — roughly 40,000 feet — along the corridor between St. Louis and Springfield, Missouri. The reports were logged under the FAA's SKYWATCH system with the primary code "OTHER" and assigned the identifier 237UAP00009.

The FAA consulted two military bodies: NORAD (North American Aerospace Defense Command) and CONR (Continental NORAD Region). Both confirmed there were no reports of space debris reentry at the time, effectively ruling out one possible conventional explanation. No other explanation was proposed in the document.

The incident was marked as closed with no further action noted. The report is a single-page summary and contains minimal detail — no observer names, no aircraft types, and no information about how long the lights were visible. Multiple witnesses are referenced but none are identified.

One notable detail: although the sighting occurred in 2009, the record carries an incident year of 2023, suggesting it was retrospectively included in the Department of Defense's PURSUE collection rather than being a recent event.

## What to know

- This is a single-page summary with minimal detail — not a full investigation report.
- No witnesses are named and no aircraft types are identified, making independent verification difficult.
- The only explanation investigated (space debris reentry) was ruled out; no alternative explanation was offered or pursued.
- The 2009 incident date does not match the file's stated 2023 incident year, indicating retrospective inclusion in the PURSUE archive rather than a new event.
- The case was closed without resolution — 'closed' means no further FAA action, not that an explanation was found.
