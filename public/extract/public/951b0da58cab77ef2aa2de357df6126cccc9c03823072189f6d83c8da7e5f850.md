# Military Video Shows Spherical Object Tracked by Sensors in 2021

**Department of War · 2021 · CENTCOM**

> This is a roughly five-minute military video from April 2021 showing sensors aboard a U.S. military platform tracking a spherical unidentified object in the Middle East region. The footage lacks a verified chain of custody, and the platform's identity is redacted.

In April 2021, a U.S. military platform operating in the area overseen by United States Central Command (CENTCOM, covering the Middle East and Central Asia) recorded a four-minute, 49-second video of a spherical unidentified anomalous phenomenon (UAP). The footage, catalogued as DOW-UAP-PR062, appears to come from a full-motion video camera paired with an infrared sensor. Throughout the recording, the sensor pans and adjusts zoom to keep a bright area of contrast centred in frame.

The video was uploaded to a classified government network in May 2024 — roughly three years after the incident — by an unidentified user. It was later flagged by the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) as responsive to a March 2026 congressional request, in which eight members of the U.S. House of Representatives sought access to 51 potentially UAP-related records held by the Department of War.

AARO's own language is notably cautious. The agency says the footage is "likely derived from" the sensor platform rather than confirming it outright. It also warns that many of the materials in this batch lack a substantiated chain of custody, meaning the provenance of the video cannot be independently confirmed. The callsign of the military platform has been redacted, and the official description of the video's content is limited to the sensor-tracking behaviour — no assessment of the object's speed, altitude, or anomalous properties is provided.

The description text released alongside the video appears truncated, suggesting additional context may have been cut off or withheld.

## What to know

- AARO states the video lacks a substantiated chain of custody — there is no verified record of who handled it between the 2021 incident and the 2024 upload to a classified network.
- The military platform's callsign is redacted, so independent verification of which unit or aircraft recorded the footage is not possible.
- AARO uses qualifying language ('likely derived from') rather than definitively attributing the video to a specific sensor or platform.
- The official description says only that a contrast area was tracked — no characterisation of the object's behaviour, speed, size, or anomalous properties is included.
- The video description text appears truncated, meaning some context may be missing from the public release.
