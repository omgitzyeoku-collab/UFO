# CIA Memo Detailed British Government's UFO Committee and Yorkshire Sighting

**CIA · December, 1952**

> A secret CIA memo from December 1952, written by the Assistant Director of Scientific Intelligence, summarised British government efforts to investigate UFO reports — including a standing committee that had been running since mid-1951 and a recent sighting at a Yorkshire RAF base witnessed by officials and pilots. The memo also outlined defence concerns: that UFO reports could weaken early-warning systems, enable enemy-induced mass hysteria, or overload emergency communications.

On 18 December 1952, H. Marshall Chadwell, the CIA's Assistant Director of Scientific Intelligence, wrote a secret internal memo titled "British Activity in the field of Unidentified Flying Objects." The memo recorded what a messenger — whose name is redacted — had learned from conversations with R.V. Jones, a senior British scientific intelligence figure.

According to the memo, Britain had created a standing committee on flying saucers roughly sixteen months earlier (around mid-1951), which Jones had apparently inherited responsibility for. The committee's conclusion at that point was straightforward: the observations were not enemy aircraft, and none had been over Britain. Activity had been "quiet and normal" — until about ten to twelve days before the memo was written.

Around that time, a sighting at a Yorkshire RAF base changed the tone. High-ranking RAF officials and pilots reportedly witnessed what was described as a "perfect flying saucer." So many people saw it that the story reached the public press, which troubled Jones because managing public perception fell within his responsibilities. The memo also notes that Jones referenced the 1946 Swedish ghost-rocket incidents and that the US had shared information with him about the Tremonton film — footage taken by a Naval non-commissioned officer in the United States.

The memo's second section — which may be from an attached or related document — laid out three specific defence concerns: that difficulty identifying unknown objects could weaken early-warning systems, that an enemy could deliberately trigger mass hysteria through faked UFO reports at a critical moment, and that a flood of sighting reports could overload emergency communications. The CIA planned to raise these concerns with the Federal Civil Defense Administration and the Joint Communications and Electronics Committee.

The document is classified SECRET and contains moderate redactions, primarily obscuring the messenger's identity and some contextual details.

## What to know

- The entire memo is single-source intelligence: it relies on one unnamed messenger's account of conversations with R.V. Jones, not on direct observation or multiple independent reports.
- The messenger's name and some contextual details are redacted, making it impossible to assess the source's reliability or position.
- The Yorkshire sighting description is second-hand — the memo author was not present and is relaying what the messenger reported.
- The second section discussing defence concerns may come from an attached or related document rather than the same memo; its relationship to the main text is not explicitly stated.
