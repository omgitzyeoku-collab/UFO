# NASA's Gemini 7 Crew Debriefing Covers Visual Sightings From 14-Day Spaceflight

**NASA · December 19-21, 1965 · Cape Kennedy, Florida**

> This is the full technical debriefing of astronauts Frank Borman and Jim Lovell after their 14-day Gemini 7 mission in December 1965. The transcript covers spacecraft systems, orbital operations, and a dedicated section on visual sightings — including a satellite, meteors, aurora, lightning, and debris — though the crew described these observations matter-of-factly, not as unidentified phenomena.

In December 1965, astronauts Frank Borman and Jim Lovell completed a 14-day spaceflight aboard Gemini 7, at the time one of the longest crewed missions ever flown. Between 19 and 21 December, they sat down at the Crew Quarters at Cape Kennedy, Florida, for a detailed technical debriefing recorded on voice tape. The resulting transcript, published 23 December 1965, runs through every phase of the mission from countdown to recovery.

The document includes a dedicated Section 10 on Visual Sightings, broken into observations during countdown, powered flight, orbital flight, and reentry. The crew reported seeing a satellite at about 16 hours and 40 minutes into the flight, describing it as being on a slightly higher inclination orbit and passing below them. They also noted that during a perigee adjustment burn, the spent booster's venting suddenly lit up and "looked like we were flying through a lot of foreign objects or debris." At first-stage engine cutoff, the entire spacecraft was engulfed in a red flame. None of these observations were described by the crew as unexplained — the satellite was identified as a satellite, the debris was attributed to booster venting, and the flame was a known effect of engine cutoff.

Beyond visual sightings, the debriefing captures notable engineering details: the insertion velocity matched its target of 25,804 feet per second exactly, the digital clock did not lose a single second over 14 days, and the crew tracked the launch of Gemini 6 from orbit using infrared instruments. The mission was not without difficulties — the water boiler froze at about 266 hours, dropping suit temperatures below 40 degrees, and overnight drift rates reached roughly 7 degrees per second caused by water boiler venting.

The document was marked as potentially exempt from public disclosure under the Freedom of Information Act. However, there is low evidence of redactions in the released version. It is worth noting that while the table of contents lists the full Visual Sightings section, the body text of that section was not fully present in the extracted source material available for review.

## What to know

- The text was produced by OCR of a 1965 document and contains scanning artefacts; some details may be garbled or misread.
- Section 10 (Visual Sightings) — the section most relevant to UAP research — appears in the table of contents but its full body text was not included in the extracted source reviewed here, so its complete contents remain unverified.
- This is a preliminary rough-edited transcript, not a final reviewed report; crew statements are informal and sometimes ambiguous.
- The satellite sighting at 16:40 GET was described by the crew as a satellite, not as an unidentified object. The 'debris' sighting was attributed to booster venting. No observation in the available text is presented as unexplained.
- The document carries a FOIA exemption notice, but the released version shows low evidence of material redaction.
