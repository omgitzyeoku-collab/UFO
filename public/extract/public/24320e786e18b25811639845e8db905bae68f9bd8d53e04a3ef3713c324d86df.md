# Pentagon Office Explains Mysterious UFO Wakes as Camera Artifacts

**AARO**

> The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), a Pentagon unit, reviewed three UAP (unidentified anomalous phenomena) cases from the Middle East and Mediterranean Sea where drone operators recorded objects with apparent atmospheric wakes on infrared cameras. AARO concluded with high confidence that the wakes were sensor artifacts caused by objects moving quickly across the camera's field of view, not signs of unusual propulsion. The objects themselves were identified as ordinary aircraft, including a military plane and an Airbus A380 airliner.

In May 2023, the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) — the U.S. Department of Defense unit responsible for investigating unidentified anomalous phenomena — published a case resolution report covering three UAP incidents from 2022 and 2023. The incidents occurred in the Middle East and the Mediterranean Sea, and all were reported by military drone operators using infrared cameras.

In each case, the drone footage appeared to show objects leaving visible atmospheric wakes — trails that could suggest unusual or advanced propulsion. AARO, working with two unnamed science and technology partners, analyzed the footage and concluded that the trails were camera artifacts. When an object moves rapidly across an infrared camera's field of view, it can leave a visual streak that looks like a wake but is not physically real. Other objects in the same videos also left similar trails, reinforcing this explanation.

Of the objects themselves, AARO identified the one in Case Three as a specific Airbus A380 commercial airliner, confirmed through commercial flight data and photogrammetry analysis showing the object's size closely matched an A380. It was flying along a recognized travel corridor. In Case Two, one object was matched to a known military aircraft using location data; a second object remained unidentified but was assessed with medium confidence as a small conventional aircraft. The object in Case One was never identified, but AARO stated it was certain the object was not exhibiting anomalous behavior.

The report resolves all three cases as prosaic — meaning ordinary, non-anomalous. No evidence of unusual technology or unexplained flight characteristics was found. The full-motion video and detailed sensor data are not included in the public document.

## What to know

- The object in Case One was never actually identified — AARO assessed it as non-anomalous but could not say what it was. Ruling something out is not the same as identifying it.
- The two science and technology partners who corroborated AARO's analysis are not named, making independent verification of their work impossible for the public.
- All assessments rely on classified intelligence and sensor data not included in this public report, so the public must take the conclusions on trust.
- One assessment — the second object in Case Two — carries only medium confidence, meaning AARO was less certain about that identification than the others.
- This document covers only three specific cases and should not be read as a blanket statement about all UAP reports involving atmospheric wakes.
