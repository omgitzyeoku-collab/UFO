# American Airlines Crew Reported High-Altitude Balloon at 50,000 Feet in 2023

**FAA · 2023**

> This is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report from 3 February 2023 in which the crew of American Airlines flight AAL799 reported observing a large balloon at approximately 50,000 feet or above while cruising at 35,000 feet. The FAA classified the sighting as an Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon, and the case is now closed.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH Incident Report — a standardised form used by the Federal Aviation Administration to log unusual aerial sightings reported by pilots. It was classified under the primary code "Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon."

On 3 February 2023, the crew of American Airlines flight AAL799 — an Airbus A321 flying eastbound from San Diego to Philadelphia at flight level 350 (approximately 35,000 feet) — reported seeing a large balloon off the left side of the aircraft. The object was located approximately 10 nautical miles northeast of the navigation point BQS. The crew estimated the balloon was at flight level 500 (approximately 50,000 feet) or above.

The sighting was reported to Kansas City Center (ZKC), the air traffic control facility covering that airspace. The case status is listed as closed. No resolution or identification of the object is provided in the report beyond the crew's own description of it as a "large balloon."

It is worth noting that this incident occurred during the same period in early February 2023 when multiple high-altitude balloon incursions over US airspace were widely reported in the news. However, the report itself does not draw any connection to those events.

## What to know

- This is a single-source pilot observation — no corroborating radar data or sensor evidence is mentioned in the report.
- The crew described the object as a 'large balloon,' but this is their assessment, not a confirmed identification. The FAA classified it as an Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon.
- 50,000 feet is an unusually high altitude for a conventional balloon to be visible to the naked eye. No size, shape, or colour details beyond 'large balloon' are provided.
- The geographic reference 'BQS' is a navigation point; exact coordinates are not given in the report.
- The case is closed with no stated conclusion about what the object was.
