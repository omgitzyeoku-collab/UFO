# United Airlines crew reported unexplained lights at 35,000 feet over South Dakota

**FAA · 2023**

> This FAA incident report documents a United Airlines Boeing 737 MAX 9 crew observing lights appearing and disappearing directly ahead of their aircraft at or above 35,000 feet, roughly 25 nautical miles south of Sioux Falls, South Dakota, on 11 February 2023. The case was closed with no explanation provided.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report — the system the Federal Aviation Administration uses to log pilot reports of unidentified aerial phenomena. It covers an event on 11 February 2023 involving United Airlines flight 1786, a Boeing 737 MAX 9 flying eastbound from Los Angeles (LAX) to Cleveland (CLE) at 35,000 feet.

While passing approximately 25 nautical miles south of Sioux Falls, South Dakota, the flight crew reported seeing lights directly ahead of the aircraft that were "coming and going" at or above their altitude. The sighting was logged by Minneapolis Air Route Traffic Control Centre (ZMP), the FAA facility overseeing that airspace. The report notes that "TY" was notified, though this abbreviation is unexplained — it likely refers to an internal FAA office, person, or system.

The case is marked as closed. No resolution, explanation, or follow-up findings appear in the record. There is no mention of corroborating radar data or sensor evidence. Notably, no tail number was recorded for the aircraft, which is unusual for FAA incident documentation.

The report is brief and administrative in nature. It records what the crew said they saw and the basic flight details, but offers no analysis of what the lights might have been. The FAA classified the event under its primary code for Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon.

## What to know

- This is a single-source pilot observation — no corroborating radar, sensor, or independent witness data is referenced in the report.
- The description of the phenomenon ('lights coming and going') is vague and could correspond to a range of conventional explanations, including other aircraft, atmospheric effects, or satellites.
- The case was closed with no resolution or explanation provided, which means the FAA did not publicly determine what the lights were.
- No aircraft tail number was recorded, which is atypical and limits independent verification of the flight details.
- The abbreviation 'TY' in 'TY notified' is unexplained, making it unclear who or what was informed about the sighting.
