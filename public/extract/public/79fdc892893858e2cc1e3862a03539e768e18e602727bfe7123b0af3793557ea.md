# Delta Pilot Reported Mystery Object Later Identified as Starlink Satellites

**FAA · 2023**

> An FAA incident report documents a Delta Air Lines pilot reporting an unidentified aerial phenomenon while flying at 37,000 feet over the North Pacific on 13 February 2023. The sighting was subsequently identified as a Starlink satellite train illuminated by the rising sun, a conclusion confirmed by NORAD. The case is closed.

On 13 February 2023, the crew of Delta Air Lines flight DAL26 — a commercial Airbus A350 flying eastbound at 37,000 feet — reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon off the front of the aircraft. The sighting occurred approximately 1,350 miles north-northwest of Shemya, a remote island in Alaska's Aleutian chain, placing the aircraft over the North Pacific.

The FAA logged the report under its SKYWATCH programme as case 237UAP00217. Investigators determined that the unknown phenomenon correlated with a Starlink satellite train travelling southeast at a time when the rising eastern sun would have illuminated the satellites, making them visible to the flight crew. NORAD — the joint US-Canadian military command responsible for aerospace monitoring — independently concurred with this identification.

As part of the investigation, the FAA's Air Traffic Security Coordination (ATSC) briefed the Office of Military and Intelligence Coordination (OMIC) on using the website Satellitemap.space to cross-reference future pilot reports against known satellite activity.

The case is now closed. There is minimal redaction in the document, and the resolution is straightforward: the phenomenon was a known satellite constellation, not an unexplained object.
