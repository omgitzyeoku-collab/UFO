# DHS Released All Records on Terminated Program KONA BLUE to Defense Department

**AARO**

> In February 2024, the Department of Homeland Security declassified and released all documents related to KONA BLUE, a prospective special access program it had terminated in 2012. The accompanying material includes justifications for restricting a related Pentagon program called AATIP, citing concerns about exotic technologies that could be weaponised by adversaries.

In a letter dated February 5, 2024, the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) informed Deputy Secretary of Defense Kathleen H. Hicks that it had declassified all documents associated with a program called KONA BLUE. The letter was signed by Kristie Canegallo and responded to a September 28, 2023 request. DHS confirmed it had searched all pertinent records and found no additional material beyond what was released.

KONA BLUE was a DHS prospective special access program — meaning it was proposed but never fully stood up. It was terminated on February 10, 2012. The declassified documents use a strike-out method, leaving prior classification markings visible so readers can see what was originally restricted.

Alongside the cover letter, the release includes excerpts from a justification document for restricting AATIP (the Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program, a separate Pentagon effort). That justification argued for special access controls because the programme dealt with what it called exotic technologies — described as 'likely' involving sophisticated concepts in quantum mechanics, nuclear science, electromagnetic theory, gravitics, and thermodynamics. The document warned these technologies had potential for 'catastrophic effects' if obtained by adversaries. It also argued that public awareness of industry partners' involvement could discourage their continued participation with the US government.

Some redactions remain. DHS noted these were applied after a policy and legal review, meaning not everything in the original documents made it into the public release despite the broader declassification.

## What to know

- KONA BLUE was a 'prospective' program that was terminated — there is no evidence in this document that it ever became fully operational.
- The document uses the word 'likely' when describing exotic technologies, indicating these were anticipated or theoretical capabilities, not confirmed ones.
- The relationship between KONA BLUE (a DHS programme) and AATIP (a Pentagon programme) is not clearly explained in the released material; the justification text appears to belong to AATIP, not KONA BLUE itself.
- Despite declassification, some information was withheld after legal and policy review — the release is not complete.
- This is a single administrative document (a cover letter with attached excerpts), not a technical report or investigation finding. It does not describe any specific incidents or objects.
