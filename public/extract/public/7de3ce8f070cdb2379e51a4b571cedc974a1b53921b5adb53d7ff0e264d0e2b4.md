# Military Infrared Video Shows Two Unidentified Objects Over Gulf of Arabia

**Department of War · 2020 · CENTCOM**

> This is a 30-second infrared video reportedly captured by a U.S. military platform in the Gulf of Arabia on 5 May 2020, showing two unidentified objects tracked by a sensor. The video was uploaded to a classified network in July 2020 and later released after eight members of the U.S. House of Representatives requested access to UAP-related records in March 2026.

In March 2026, eight members of the U.S. House of Representatives requested access to 51 potentially UAP-related records held by the U.S. government. The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) identified responsive materials on a classified network, and this video — catalogued as DOW-UAP-PR093 — was among them.

The video is 30 seconds long and is described as likely coming from an infrared sensor aboard a U.S. military platform operating in the U.S. Central Command area of responsibility in 2020. Its uploader-defined title places the incident in the Gulf of Arabia on 5 May 2020 and references "Dual UAP" — two unidentified anomalous phenomena. The footage shows a sensor panning to track an area of contrast, with several such areas entering and exiting the field of view.

The video was originally uploaded to a classified network in July 2020. The callsign and platform type in the title are redacted, so the specific military unit or aircraft involved cannot be identified from the released materials.

AARO has not provided its own assessment of whether the objects are anomalous. The label "Dual UAP" comes from the original uploader, not from any official analytical conclusion. The agency also notes that the video lacks a substantiated chain of custody, meaning the full handling history from capture to release has not been independently verified.

## What to know

- AARO uses the word 'likely' when attributing the video to a U.S. military infrared sensor, meaning this is not confirmed with full certainty.
- The term 'areas of contrast' is a neutral sensor description — it does not confirm the objects are anomalous or unexplained.
- The 'Dual UAP' label was applied by the person who uploaded the video to the classified network, not by AARO or any official investigation.
- The materials lack a substantiated chain of custody, so the full provenance and handling history are not independently verified.
- The callsign and platform type are redacted, preventing identification of which military unit or aircraft recorded the footage.
