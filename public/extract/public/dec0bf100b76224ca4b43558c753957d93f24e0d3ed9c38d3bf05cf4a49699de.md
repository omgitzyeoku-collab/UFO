# Delta Crew Reported Large Balloon at 50,000 Feet Over Nebraska

**FAA · 2023**

> This FAA incident report documents a Delta Air Lines crew observing a large balloon while flying at 36,000 feet near Lincoln, Nebraska on 3 February 2023. The object, estimated at 50,000 feet or higher, was classified as an Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon. The case is closed.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report filed on 3 February 2023 at 15:01 UTC. It records a sighting by the crew of Delta Air Lines flight DAL1242, a commercial flight travelling northbound from Austin to Minneapolis at 36,000 feet.

The crew reported a large balloon off the left side of the aircraft, approximately 29 nautical miles south of Lincoln, Nebraska. They estimated the balloon was at roughly 50,000 feet or above — well above their own cruising altitude and above the ceiling of most commercial air traffic.

The FAA classified the incident under the primary code "Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon" and routed the report through the Denver POD. The reporting facility was listed as AWO. Despite the crew describing the object as a balloon, it received the UAP classification, indicating it was not positively identified at the time.

The case status is marked as closed. The report contains no mention of follow-up investigation, corroborating radar or sensor data, or a final determination of what the object was. There is minimal redaction in this document.

## What to know

- This is a single-source pilot observation with no corroborating sensor or radar data mentioned in the report.
- The estimated altitude of 50,000 feet is approximate, based on visual judgement from the aircraft — not instrument measurement.
- The sighting occurred on 3 February 2023, during the same period as the widely reported Chinese surveillance balloon incidents over the United States, which may be relevant context.
- The crew described the object as a 'balloon' but the FAA classified it as a UAP, meaning it was not positively identified at the time of reporting.
- The case is closed with no stated conclusion about what the object was.
