# Pilot Reports Unidentified Object Above Aircraft Near Yardley, Pennsylvania

**FAA · 2023**

> This FAA incident report documents a November 2023 sighting by a pilot flying a Pilatus PC-12 near Yardley, Pennsylvania. The pilot observed an unidentified object with lights approximately 1,200 feet above the aircraft at around 19,000 feet altitude; air traffic control saw it on radar but received no transponder signal or radio contact from the object.

On the evening of November 3, 2023, at approximately 8:10 PM Eastern time, a pilot flying a Pilatus PC-12 (a single-engine turboprop, registration N822NG) reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon to air traffic control. The aircraft was en route from Farmingdale, New York to DeKalb-Peachtree Airport near Atlanta, travelling southwest at flight level 190 — roughly 19,000 feet.

The pilot observed the object about 1,200 feet above the aircraft, approximately 3 nautical miles northwest of Yardley VOR (a navigation reference point near Yardley, Pennsylvania, at coordinates 40.28°N, 74.89°W). The pilot reported that the object had lights suggesting it was an aircraft. New York ARTCC (Air Route Traffic Control Center) was advised of the sighting.

Air traffic control confirmed that the object appeared on primary radar only — meaning it showed up as a radar return but was not transmitting a transponder signal and was not in radio communication with controllers. No transponder signal and no radio contact are unusual for conventional aircraft operating at that altitude in controlled airspace.

The report does not document any follow-up investigation or resolution. There is no conclusion from the FAA about what the object was. The document shows low evidence of redaction — the information appears to be presented largely as recorded.

## What to know

- This is a single-source report based on one pilot's observation relayed through air traffic control. There is no independent corroboration from other pilots or sensors described in this document.
- The pilot themselves noted the object had lights consistent with an aircraft, meaning it may have been a conventional aircraft flying without an active transponder or radio contact — unusual but not impossible.
- A primary-only radar return (no transponder) is consistent with both an unidentified object and a non-cooperative conventional aircraft.
- No follow-up investigation or resolution is documented in this report — the FAA did not reach a conclusion about what the object was.
- This is an operational incident report, not an investigation finding. It records what was reported, not what was determined.
