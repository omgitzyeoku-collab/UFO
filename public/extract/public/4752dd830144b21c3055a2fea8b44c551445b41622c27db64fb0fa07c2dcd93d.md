# U.S. Air Force Crew Spotted Unidentified Object Over Baghdad in 2022

**Department of War · N/A · Iraq**

> A declassified military mission report records that a U.S. Air Force surveillance crew observed a single possible unidentified aerial phenomenon (UAP) or drone flying west to east near Baghdad, Iraq, on 1 December 2022. The crew did not pursue the object, no further data was collected, and the sighting remains unresolved.

This document is a U.S. military mission report (MISREP) from the 482nd Attack Squadron, originally classified and later released on 8 October 2025 by MG Richard A. Harrison, USCENTCOM Chief of Staff. It records a surveillance flight over Iraq conducted in support of Operation Inherent Resolve, the U.S.-led campaign against ISIS.

On 1 December 2022, a single Air Force intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance (ISR) aircraft took off from OKAS airfield at 1206Z and flew an approximately 19-hour mission over Baghdad. The aircraft's primary sensor was full motion video (FMV), and it also collected signals intelligence (SIGINT) via a system called AIRHANDLER. At 1620Z — roughly four hours into the mission — the crew observed one possible UAP or unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) flying west to east in the vicinity of Baghdad. The aircraft was at approximately 18,000 feet at the time.

The crew did not pursue the object. The report states they continued the mission as tasked and no further events were observed. No altitude, speed, size, or shape data was recorded for the object itself. The report notes no detected electronic signatures from the UAP and no effects on personnel. Weather was not a factor during the mission.

The document is heavily redacted. The aircraft type, tail number, callsign, crew names, and precise coordinates are all blacked out under national security exemptions. The accompanying video is only ten seconds long and shows a contrast area moving across an infrared sensor view — not enough for independent analysis. The report was released to the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) as part of a batch declassification under mandatory declassification review requests MDR 25-0094 through MDR-25-0099.

## What to know

- The crew described the object as a 'possible UAP/UAV' — they did not confirm it was anomalous, and the report explicitly allows for a conventional drone explanation.
- This is a single-source observation: one aircraft crew reported the sighting, with no corroborating sensor data or independent witnesses mentioned in the document.
- No physical characteristics of the object — altitude, speed, size, shape, or trajectory — were recorded. The only altitude documented is the friendly aircraft's own position at 18,000 feet.
- The document is heavily redacted under national security exemptions (1.4a, 1.4c, 1.4g), removing the aircraft type, crew identities, and precise location coordinates.
- The associated video is ten seconds of infrared footage showing a contrast area in motion — insufficient on its own to draw conclusions about the object's nature.
