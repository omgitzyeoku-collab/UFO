# Military Pilot's UAP Near Eglin Base Resolved as Likely Balloon

**AARO**

> AARO (All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office) investigated a January 2023 sighting of an unidentified object near Eglin Air Force Base, Florida, where a military pilot detected four objects on radar and visually observed one. The agency and two independent partners concluded with moderate-to-high confidence that the object was most likely a commercial lighting balloon, with no anomalous characteristics.

On 26 January 2023, a military pilot flying in the training range off Florida's coast near Eglin Air Force Base detected four objects on radar at altitudes between 16,000 and 18,000 feet, apparently flying in formation. The pilot was able to visually observe only one of the four objects and captured two still images of it using the aircraft's electro-optical and infrared sensors. No video was recorded because the aircraft's video recording equipment was inoperable during the flight.

The pilot described the single observed object as roughly 12 feet in diameter, gray with a paneled surface and orange-red coloring at the center. Its shape was compared to an Apollo spacecraft — rounded on the bottom with a cone-shaped top. It appeared to move very slowly or was potentially stationary. The pilot also reported seeing what looked like heat distortion (described as "blurry air") coming from the bottom of the object. In a later discussion with AARO, the pilot added a recollection of a vertically oriented engine on the side of the object — a detail not included in the original report and not visible in the captured images.

The aircraft's radar malfunctioned when it closed to within 4,000 feet of the object, but AARO assessed this was likely unrelated — the same circuit breaker on that specific aircraft had already tripped three times in the months before the incident, though technicians could not conclusively diagnose the underlying cause. After the radar failed, the object descended into the cloud deck and was lost.

AARO, along with an Intelligence Community partner and a science and technology partner, independently analyzed the case. All three concluded the object was very likely a lighter-than-air object such as a large commercial lighting balloon. AARO itself expressed moderate confidence in this identification due to limited data, while both independent partners assessed with high confidence that the object showed no anomalous characteristics. The object's slow movement matched wind conditions at the time, the infrared imagery was consistent with a balloon, and features like the paneling and orange-red color could be explained by balloon seams and red-colored tether points. The case was formally resolved on 14 October 2023.

## What to know

- Only one of the four radar-detected objects was ever visually observed or imaged — the other three remain unanalyzed.
- This was a single-observer event: one pilot saw the object, and no video was captured due to equipment failure.
- AARO's own confidence level is only moderate, not high, because limited data was available for analysis.
- The pilot's description changed between the initial report and a later interview with AARO, adding details (such as a possible engine) that do not appear in the images or the original filing.
- The radar malfunction was attributed to a pre-existing aircraft fault rather than the object, but technicians were unable to conclusively determine the root cause.
