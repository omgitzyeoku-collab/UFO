# United Airlines Crew Reports White-Blue Object Near San Antonio

**FAA · 2023**

> This is an FAA incident report documenting a sighting by the crew of United Airlines flight 1715 (an Airbus A320) on 13 January 2024. The crew observed a white and blue object at roughly 7,000 feet while flying southeast of San Antonio, Texas. The object did not appear on air traffic control radar, no evasive action was taken, and the case is closed with no further investigation noted.

On 13 January 2024 at 20:26, the crew of United Airlines flight UAL1715 — an Airbus A320 flying from San Antonio (SAT) to Denver (DEN) — reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon off the right side of the aircraft. The sighting occurred 16 nautical miles southeast of San Antonio while the aircraft was eastbound at 7,000 feet.

The crew described the object as white and blue in colour, at approximately the same altitude as the aircraft. No evasive action was taken, and the crew reported no impact to flight operations.

The FAA logged the event under its SKYWATCH programme, which tracks unidentified aerial phenomenon reports. Air traffic control checked their radar but did not detect the object. The report was assigned the identifier 237UAP00337 and the mandatory occurrence report ID SAT-M-2024/01/13-0002.

The case status is listed as closed. The document contains no indication of follow-up investigation, no identification of the object, and no additional sensor data beyond the crew's visual observation. The tail number field for the aircraft is blank.

## What to know

- This is a single-source visual observation by the flight crew only — no radar, camera, or other sensor data corroborates the sighting.
- The object was not detected on ATC radar, meaning there is no independent confirmation that it existed as a physical object at that location.
- The case is marked closed with no further investigation noted, so the object was never identified or explained.
- The document shows low evidence of redaction, but the aircraft tail number field is blank, which limits independent verification of the specific aircraft involved.
- An FAA SKYWATCH report documents what a crew reported — it does not represent an FAA conclusion about what the object was.
