# FBI File Compiles 1947 UFO Sightings Across Northeast US and Newfoundland

**FBI · N/A**

> This FBI/Air Defense Command case file gathers military intelligence reports, sworn witness statements, newspaper clippings, and an unsigned intelligence analysis covering multiple UFO sightings in July and August 1947 across the northeastern United States, Newfoundland, and Alabama. An unnamed analyst concluded "something is really flying around" and speculated the objects could be a domestic military project.

This document is a compiled FBI and Air Defense Command case file bringing together reports from multiple UFO sightings during the summer of 1947. It contains military intelligence reports, witness interrogations, sworn statements, and newspaper clippings from locations spanning Massachusetts, Newfoundland, Alabama, and New York.

The most detailed account comes from Alpheus O. Powell, a Pan American Airways captain with over 4,000 hours of command pilot experience, who on August 4, 1947, observed a bright orange cylindrical object near Boston while flying a Constellation aircraft at 8,000 feet. Powell described it as roughly the length of a P-40 fighter fuselage, blunt at both ends, traveling at about 160 mph. He watched it for approximately 30 seconds and ruled out pilot balloons, tow targets, and radiosonde devices as explanations, noting the object had a definite shape with no gaseous dissipation. His navigator, W. White, separately observed what appeared to be a different object on the opposite side of the aircraft.

In Newfoundland, multiple sightings were reported in July 1947. On July 10, witnesses near Harmon Field described a circular disc the apparent size of a C-54 transport aircraft leaving a bluish-black trail roughly 15 miles long. A civilian named Robert Leidy photographed the trail on Kodachrome film, which was forwarded to military intelligence — though the photographs themselves are not included in this file. At Codroy, Newfoundland, on July 11, witnesses including a twelve-year-old boy named Albert Samms described a flame-colored disc. Other sightings were reported from the steamship Burgeo and at Grand Falls, where a constable described four round objects followed by a jellyfish-like object with a phosphorus glow.

In Birmingham, Alabama, on July 6, 1947, a mass sighting prompted numerous civilian reports and newspaper coverage. Staff Sergeant Ira L. Livingston provided a sworn statement describing dim glowing objects at roughly 2,000 feet altitude traveling an estimated 500–600 mph. A local newspaper copy reader named Crossland photographed objects over Birmingham. A searchlight or carnival beacon at Siluria was proposed as an explanation for the Birmingham sightings but was never conclusively investigated.

The file includes an unsigned, undated intelligence analysis memo concluding that "this 'flying saucer' situation is not all imaginary" and that "something is really flying around." The analyst described the objects as metallic, circular or elliptical, flat-bottomed and slightly domed, roughly the size of a C-54 aircraft, capable of speeds above 300 knots, and possibly powered by liquid rocket engines. The analyst also noted that the lack of high-level government inquiries into the sightings lent weight to the possibility that the objects were part of a domestic project known to the President — though this was speculation, not evidence.

## What to know

- The intelligence analysis memo is unsigned, undated, and has no identified originating office — its authorship and authority within the file are unclear.
- Multiple sightings from different locations and dates are compiled into a single case file, which can create a misleading impression that they represent a single coordinated phenomenon.
- The Kodachrome photographs taken by Robert Leidy are referenced but not included in this file; their content and fate are unknown from this document alone.
- Several pages have poor OCR quality with partial text loss, and the Birmingham newspaper accounts contain highly varied and contradictory witness descriptions typical of mass excitement events.
- Some pages carry RESTRICTED or CONFIDENTIAL classification markings with partial declassification stamps, suggesting portions of the file may have been withheld historically. The analyst's suggestion of a domestic project 'about which the President knows' is speculative inference from the absence of top-level inquiries, not positive evidence.
