# Multiple Airline Crews Reported Circling White Lights Over Kansas

**FAA · 2023**

> This FAA incident report documents a sighting on 27 December 2023 in which crews from at least three commercial flights observed two bright white lights at 41,000 feet, roughly 30 nautical miles north of Salina, Kansas. The lights appeared to change their separation distance and move in circles, but were not detected on air traffic control radar. The FAA closed the case without explanation.

On 27 December 2023 at 10:30, the crew of United Airlines flight UAL348 reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon while flying eastbound at 41,000 feet, approximately 30 nautical miles north of Salina, Kansas. They described two bright white lights directly ahead of the aircraft that were increasing and decreasing in distance from each other and moving in circles.

The sighting was not limited to one aircraft. Crews from at least two other commercial flights — Spirit Airlines flight NKS2804 and American Airlines flight AAL2014 — independently reported the same phenomenon. The observations were relayed through air traffic control under Kansas City Center (ZKC) airspace.

Despite multiple visual reports, the phenomenon did not appear on the ATC facility radar system. No tail number was recorded for the initial reporting aircraft, and no photographic or sensor evidence is referenced in the file.

The FAA logged this under its SKYWATCH programme and assigned it case number 237UAP00333. The case status is marked as Closed, but the document provides no resolution, identification, or explanation for what the crews observed.

## What to know

- This is a visual-only sighting — no radar, photographic, or sensor evidence corroborates the crew reports.
- The case is marked Closed with no explanation or identification offered by the FAA.
- All observer descriptions are secondhand, relayed through air traffic control reporting rather than direct witness statements.
- Multiple independent crews reported the same phenomenon, which adds some weight, but the observations are still limited to unaided human vision at night.
- No tail number was recorded for the primary reporting aircraft, limiting traceability.
