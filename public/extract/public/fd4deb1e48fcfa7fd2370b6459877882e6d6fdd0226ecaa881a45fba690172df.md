# US Military Reports Unexplained Light During Syria Surveillance Mission

**Department of War · N/A · Syria**

> A declassified military mission report documents an unexplained light observed by a US Air Force special operations aircrew during a surveillance flight over Syria on 20 October 2024. The crew saw a misshapen ball of white light flash across their video camera feed five times over 45 minutes, assessed it as benign, and took no action.

This document is a military mission report (MISREP) from the 12th Special Operations Squadron, part of Air Force Special Operations Command, describing an intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance (ISR) flight conducted under Operation Inherent Resolve in Syria. The report was originally classified SECRET and releasable to the US and Five Eyes allies. It was declassified on 24 October 2025 by Major General Richard A. Harrison, USCENTCOM Chief of Staff.

On 20 October 2024, a single aircraft took off at 0823Z and flew a mission lasting 20 hours and 24 minutes, spending over 14 hours on station collecting full-motion video (FMV) and signals intelligence. The aircraft was orbiting at 20,088 feet and 144 knots when, starting at 1559Z, the crew began observing unexplained light activity on their FMV camera feed. Over the next 45 minutes, they logged five separate events: at 1559Z, 1602Z, and 1644Z a light or glare crossed directly across the camera, and at 1609Z and 1620Z a halo effect appeared at the top of the feed. The crew described the phenomenon as a misshapen and uneven ball of white light.

The observer — an E-4 (Senior Airman), a junior enlisted rank — reported that the light did not respond to the aircraft's presence and showed no signs of intelligent control or advanced capabilities. The aircrew assessed the phenomenon was not a lasing event (a laser deliberately aimed at an aircraft) and considered it benign with no impact on their mission. The report's structured form categorises the object's physical state as "plasma," though this appears to be a form-field selection rather than a measured finding. No independent sensor was used to corroborate what the camera showed, and the crew did not attempt to engage or interrogate the phenomenon further.

The document is heavily redacted. Aircraft type, callsign, tail number, grid coordinates, personnel names, and unit-level details are all blacked out under national security and personal privacy exemptions. Notably, the associated video footage is only 5 seconds long, despite the observation window spanning 45 minutes — meaning the vast majority of the event was either not captured or not released.

## What to know

- The phenomenon was observed only through the FMV camera feed — no independent sensor (radar, infrared, signals intelligence) was used to corroborate it, and the report explicitly notes that interrogation of the UAP was not attempted.
- The sole observer was an E-4 (Senior Airman), a junior enlisted rank, and no third-party observers were reported.
- The crew's own assessment was that the event was benign with no mission impact — this is an operational judgment by the aircrew, not an analytical conclusion by investigators.
- The report is heavily redacted: aircraft type, exact location coordinates, personnel identities, and other operational details are withheld under national security and privacy exemptions.
- Only 5 seconds of video footage accompanies the report despite a 45-minute observation window, so outside reviewers cannot independently evaluate the full event.
