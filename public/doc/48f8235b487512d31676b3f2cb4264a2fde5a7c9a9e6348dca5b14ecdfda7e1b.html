Pilot Reports Unexplained Lights at 28,000 Feet Over Kansas

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This FAA incident report documents a general aviation pilot's sighting of an unidentified object displaying red and white lights near Chanute, Kansas, on 10 February 2023. The case was closed with no explanation or identification of the object provided in the report.

On 10 February 2023 at 03:13 UTC, a general aviation pilot flying a B350 aircraft (callsign N378BG) reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon to Kansas City Center (ZKC), the FAA facility handling that airspace. The aircraft had departed from BBG and was heading northwest at flight level 280 — roughly 28,000 feet. The pilot observed an object off the left side of the aircraft, approximately 15 nautical miles south of Chanute, Kansas (identified by the aviation code CNU). The object displayed red and white lights and was travelling northwest at approximately the same altitude as the reporting aircraft. The report was logged through the FAA's SKYWATCH system, which tracks unidentified aerial phenomena. Entities identified as TY/AWO were notified, though the document does not explain what those abbreviations refer to or what role they play in the reporting chain. The case is marked as closed. No resolution, identification, or follow-up explanation appears in the document. There is no mention of radar data, additional witnesses, or further investigation.

What to know

  • This is a single-source pilot observation. No corroborating radar data or additional witness accounts are included in the report.
  • Red and white lights at cruise altitude are consistent with standard aircraft navigation lights. The report does not rule in or rule out a conventional explanation.
  • The case was closed without any stated resolution — the document does not say what the object was.
  • Some abbreviations in the report (TY/AWO) are unexplained, making part of the notification chain unclear.
  • Redaction levels appear low, but the document is brief and contains limited detail overall.
