FAA Report: Commercial Jet Crew Spotted Unidentified Object at 43,000 Feet

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

An FAA SKYWATCH incident report documents a commercial aircraft crew observing an unidentified aerial phenomenon while flying at 43,000 feet over the Akron-Canton area on 20 June 2021. The crew provided no physical description of the object, no radar or sensor data is mentioned, and the case was closed without further detail.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report — a standard form the Federal Aviation Administration uses to log reports of unidentified aerial phenomena (UAPs) in US airspace. It was filed under case number 237UAP00058 and is classified with the primary code "Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon." On 20 June 2021 at 20:05, the crew of a Cessna Citation Excel (a mid-size business jet) with callsign SLH4, operated by SLH, reported observing something unidentified while flying east-northeast at flight level 430 — roughly 43,000 feet. The aircraft was en route from Lincoln, Nebraska to Newark. At the time of the sighting, the aircraft was over the Akron-Canton (ACO) area. The phenomenon was reported at approximately the same altitude as the aircraft. Beyond that, the flight crew provided no physical description whatsoever — no shape, size, colour, or behaviour. The direction the object was travelling is listed as unknown. The report notes that law enforcement was not notified. A person identified only as "JH" was notified, though the document gives no context for who JH is or what role they serve. The aircraft's tail number field is blank, which limits independent verification of the specific aircraft involved. The case status is listed as closed. No radar data, photographs, or additional sensor confirmation is mentioned anywhere in the report. This is a single-source account from the flight crew, recorded on a standard FAA form, with no apparent follow-up investigation documented.

What to know

  • This is a single-source report from one flight crew. No radar data, sensor readings, or independent corroboration is mentioned in the document.
  • The crew provided no physical description of what they saw — no shape, size, colour, or movement details. It remains entirely uncharacterised.
  • The aircraft tail number is blank, making it harder to independently verify the specific flight.
  • Law enforcement was not notified, so no outside investigation was initiated.
  • A person referred to only as 'JH' was notified, but the document does not explain who they are or what authority they represent.
