1953 CIA Memo Shows Air Force UFO Project Running on Skeleton Crew

CIA December, 1953 ✓ verified against source

A December 1953 internal CIA memo reviews the status of the Air Force's UFO investigation, Project Blue Book. It reveals the project had been reduced to just one officer, one airman, and a secretary, with field investigations halted and resources being transferred to Air Defense Command.

This document is an internal CIA memorandum dated 17 December 1953, sent from the Chief of the Physics and Electronics Division to the Assistant Director of Scientific Intelligence. It was prepared at the verbal request of a Mr. Brent in the Executive office of Scientific Intelligence, and provides a status update on how the Air Force was handling its UFO investigation programme. The memo describes the Air Force's Project Blue Book (officially designated No. 10073), which was run out of the Air Technical Intelligence Center (ATIC). By late 1953, the entire project was staffed by just three people: Captain Charles A. Hardin, Airman First Class Max G. Futch, and a secretary. The CIA's Physics and Electronics Division had taken on responsibility for monitoring the project earlier that year. ATIC had stopped sending its own personnel to investigate UFO sightings in the field. Instead, it relied on USAF intelligence officers stationed closer to sighting locations and confined its own work to receiving and processing reports from a desk. The memo also notes that ATIC was in the process of transferring resources to Air Defense Command, and that a section within ATIC worked directly with the Public Information Office of the Secretary of the Air Force. Only the first page of what appears to be a multi-page memo is available — the document cuts off mid-sentence. Some passages are redacted or obscured, and the original scan quality is poor, making portions of the text difficult to read with certainty.

What to know

  • Only the first page of a multi-page memo survives in this release; the document cuts off mid-sentence, so the full scope of its conclusions is unknown.
  • The source is a degraded image scan with significant OCR errors, meaning some details may be misread at the character level.
  • Some passages are redacted or obscured, rated as moderate redaction by reviewers.
  • The memo describes the CIA's view of the Air Force programme — it is a single-source bureaucratic status update, not an investigation report or analysis of any specific sighting.
  • A referenced detail about Canada establishing a laboratory for recording phenomena is mentioned in the catalogue but does not appear in the surviving extracted text, suggesting missing pages.
