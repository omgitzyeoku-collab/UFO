Federal Agent Reports Multiple Unexplained Lights Over Western US in 2023

Department of War October, 2023 Westen United States ✓ verified against source

This Department of War document is a first-hand witness statement submitted to the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) by a federal law enforcement agent who observed multiple unexplained aerial phenomena over two days in October 2023 in the western United States. The witness describes hovering lights, a red orb that released smaller orbs, a low-altitude light pair that moved laterally without resistance, and unexplained ground markings — but the document is heavily redacted and includes no sensor data or corroborating witness accounts.

This document, dated 2 June 2026 and signed by AARO Director Jon T. Kosloski, contains the narrative statement of "Witness 5," one of several U.S. federal law enforcement special agents who reported observing unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP) over two days in October 2023 in the western United States. It was submitted to the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office, the Pentagon body responsible for investigating UAP reports. On the first evening, the witness reported seeing a large orange light hovering over a ridge at 6:58 PM with zero resistance or movement, lasting about a minute. Twenty minutes later, at 7:18 PM, a large red orb appeared and released two smaller red orbs that moved westward; all lights vanished within roughly ten seconds. The red orb reappeared at 7:37 PM, and a white flash of light was observed in the western sky. The witness also reported a radio anomaly during this period: other teams could not hear transmissions from the witness's group. The following morning, around 4:30 AM, the witness observed a pair of lights — one white and square, one round and red — hovering two to three feet above the ground. The light pair moved laterally at an estimated 15–20 mph with zero resistance or change in height. After the lights switched off, night vision goggles revealed a thin horizontal line roughly four feet wide. Later, the witness observed a formation of faint lights moving together in a precise pattern that appeared to shift position as if trying to blend into the stars. Near the sighting location, symmetrical round scrapes were found in the dirt, described as fresh and sharp despite calm conditions the previous night. The document is heavily redacted throughout: specific dates, names, locations, coordinates, and institutional identifiers have been removed, making independent verification impossible. No photographs, video, or sensor data accompany the narrative — only hand-drawn sketches. The statement is a single witness's account; no corroborating statements from the other agents present are included in this document. The witness openly acknowledges uncertainty in several observations. AARO has not published any analytical conclusion about these events within this document.

What to know

  • This is a single witness's account. Other agents were present but their statements are not included in this document, so there is no corroboration within it.
  • The document is heavily redacted — dates, names, locations, coordinates, and institutional details are blacked out, preventing independent verification of where and when these events occurred.
  • No sensor data, photographs, or video are included despite a camera being deployed at the scene. The only visual aids are hand-drawn sketches.
  • Observations took place in low-light and nighttime conditions, which can significantly affect visual perception and object identification.
  • The witness acknowledges uncertainty in several places (using phrases like 'I think' and 'I could not judge how far away'), indicating honest but imprecise recollection rather than definitive claims.
