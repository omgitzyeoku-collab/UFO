United Airlines Crew Reported High-Altitude Balloon Near Missouri in 2023

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This FAA SKYWATCH incident report documents a United Airlines crew observing a large balloon at an estimated 50,000 feet or above while flying at 34,000 feet on 3 February 2023. The object was logged under the FAA's "Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon" code and the case is now closed, with no resolution or identification included in the report.

On 3 February 2023 at 15:40, the crew of United Airlines flight UAL2372 reported a large balloon off the right side of their aircraft. The flight was travelling northbound at flight level 340 (roughly 34,000 feet) and was approximately 13 nautical miles west of St. Joseph, Missouri, within Kansas City Center (ZKC) airspace. The flight's route ran from Washington Dulles (IAD) to Las Vegas (LAS). The crew estimated the balloon was at flight level 500 — approximately 50,000 feet — or above. That altitude estimate was visual, not instrument-based. The FAA filed the sighting under its primary code for Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon, though the crew themselves described the object as a balloon. The report is classified as a SKYWATCH Incident Report, a standard FAA format for logging unusual aerial observations. The case status is marked as closed. No further investigation findings, radar corroboration, or object identification appear in the document. This incident occurred during the same period as a series of high-altitude balloon events across North America, including the Chinese surveillance balloon that was shot down on 4 February 2023 — one day after this sighting. The report itself does not draw any connection to those events.

What to know

  • The altitude estimate of 50,000 feet or above was a visual guess by the flight crew, not an instrument reading.
  • This is a single-source observation — no corroborating radar data or sensor records are included in the report.
  • The object was filed under the UAP code but described by the crew as a balloon, suggesting the classification reflects reporting procedure rather than a determination that the object was unexplained.
  • The report is dated 3 February 2023, one day before the widely reported Chinese surveillance balloon was shot down — the two events may or may not be related.
  • The case is closed with no stated conclusion about what the object was.
