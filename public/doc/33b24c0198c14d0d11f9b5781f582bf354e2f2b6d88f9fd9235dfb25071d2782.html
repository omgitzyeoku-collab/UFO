Military Infrared Video Shows Brief Unidentified Object Over Africa

Department of War 2020 AFRICOM ± partially verified

This is a nearly five-minute infrared video from a U.S. military platform operating in Africa in October 2020. The actual unidentified object appears for roughly one second, and the government notes the video lacks a verified chain of custody.

In October 2020, someone uploaded a short infrared video to a classified U.S. military network. The video, catalogued as DOW-UAP-PR082, was recorded by a sensor aboard a military platform operating within the United States Africa Command (AFRICOM) area of responsibility — the region covering the African continent. The video runs four minutes and 46 seconds, but the event of interest is brief: between the 00:28 and 00:29 marks, an area of contrast moves across the frame from the middle-left to the upper-right. That is the entirety of the observable phenomenon — roughly one second out of nearly five minutes of footage. The Department of War's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) assessed that the footage "is likely derived from an infrared sensor aboard a U.S. military platform," but used hedging language, indicating the source is assessed rather than confirmed. The callsign identifying the specific platform or unit was redacted from the title. The video became public as part of a broader release. On March 6, 2026, eight members of the U.S. House of Representatives requested access to 51 potentially UAP-related records, and this video was among the materials made available through the PURSUE public disclosure collection. AAR0 explicitly notes that many of these materials "lack a substantiated chain of custody," meaning the government itself cannot independently verify the video's authenticity or handling history.

What to know

  • The visible event lasts approximately one second (00:28–00:29) out of a nearly five-minute video, making independent analysis very limited.
  • AARO states the materials lack a substantiated chain of custody — the government cannot confirm how this video was handled or whether it has been altered.
  • AARO uses hedging language ('likely derived'), meaning the platform and sensor identification is an assessment, not a confirmed fact.
  • The callsign in the original title is redacted, so the specific military unit or aircraft involved is unknown.
  • All facts come from the government's own metadata description — there is no extracted text from the video itself to cross-check against.
