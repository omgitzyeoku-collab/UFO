FAA Report: Pilot Spotted Circular Red-Green Lights Over Kansas in 2018

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This FAA SKYWATCH incident report documents a pilot's sighting of a circular red and green light pattern moving slowly westbound at 9,000 feet near Goodland, Kansas, on 6 March 2018. The FAA classified the object as "OTHER" and closed the case without identifying it.

This document is an FAA (Federal Aviation Administration) SKYWATCH incident report, filed under case number 237UAP00032. It records a pilot's sighting of an unidentified aerial phenomenon on 6 March 2018, logged at 07:31. The reporting aircraft was a Beech King Air (a twin-engine turboprop), flying under callsign LN77HE. It was travelling from Centennial Airport (APA) near Denver to Goodland Municipal Airport (GLD) in western Kansas. During the flight, the pilot reported seeing a circular pattern of red and green lights moving slowly westbound at 9,000 feet. The sighting position was recorded as GLD270018, which likely refers to a bearing and distance from the Goodland VOR navigational beacon, though the report does not decode the exact coordinates. The FAA categorised the object under its primary code as "OTHER", meaning it did not fit any standard classification. The case status is listed as closed. The report contains no indication that radar data, additional witnesses, or follow-up investigation corroborated the pilot's account. No operator name or aircraft tail number appears in the report, and no personnel are named. The document shows low evidence of redaction — it appears to be a brief, routine incident log rather than a detailed investigation file.
