Three Airliners Report Bright Lights Maneuvering Near Jacksonville at Night

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This FAA preliminary incident report documents a UAP sighting on August 4, 2023, near Jacksonville, Florida, where three commercial aircraft independently reported bright white lights maneuvering rapidly at high altitude. One crew described the phenomenon as hazardous, but no radar data, sensor recordings, or follow-up investigation results are included in the document.

On August 4, 2023, at 2:35 AM Eastern time, the FAA's Washington Operations Center logged a report of an unidentified aerial phenomenon near Jacksonville, Florida. The object or objects were described as bright white lights that appeared and maneuvered rapidly at altitudes between 33,000 and 41,000 feet — standard cruising altitude for commercial jets. Three separate commercial flights reported or confirmed the sighting. NetJets 631 initially reported the phenomenon to Jacksonville Air Route Traffic Control Center (ARTCC). Spirit Airlines flight 348, operating an Airbus A319, independently reported it and described it as hazardous — though the document does not elaborate on what made it hazardous. Amerijet flight 523, a Boeing 767, also confirmed the sighting. The document records that law enforcement was not notified of the incident. No radar data, no sensor recordings, and no results from any follow-up investigation appear in this report. This is a preliminary operations center log — essentially a first notice that something was reported. It is not a full investigation, and the FAA drew no conclusions in this document about what the lights were. The descriptions are secondhand, relayed from flight crews through air traffic control to the operations center.

What to know

  • This is a preliminary FAA operations center report, not a completed investigation or conclusion about what was observed.
  • All descriptions are secondhand — relayed from aircrew through air traffic control to the operations center, not recorded directly.
  • No radar data, sensor recordings, photographs, or follow-up investigation results are included in this document.
  • Spirit 348's use of the word 'hazardous' is not defined or elaborated anywhere in the report.
  • While three aircraft reported or confirmed the sighting, the document does not establish whether all three observed the same object at the same time.
