FAA Ordered All Air Traffic Centers to Report UFO Sightings Over Denver

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This is a brief FAA administrative directive from February 2008 instructing all air traffic control centers to report pilot observations of UFOs, debris fields, or meteor showers over Denver. The document contains no details about what prompted the instruction or whether any reports were received.

This single-page FAA document is a SKYWATCH Incident Report dated 21 February 2008 at 12:47. It was issued by the NCRCC (National Capital Region Coordination Center) and classified under the primary code "OTHER." The directive instructed all ATC (Air Traffic Control) Centers to report any PIREPS (Pilot Reports) over DEN (Denver) involving UFOs, debris fields, or meteor showers. The report's status is marked as closed, and no personnel were paged in connection with it. Notably, the document provides no information about what triggered this instruction — whether pilots had already reported something unusual, or whether this was a precautionary measure. There is no follow-up indicating whether any pilot reports were subsequently received. The document is administrative in nature rather than an observation or investigation report. It records that an instruction was given, not that anything was observed or confirmed. The text includes the string "UFA" appended to "meteor showers," which appears to be a typo or truncation artefact from the original record.

What to know

  • This is a one-page administrative directive, not an investigation or eyewitness account. It records that an order was issued, nothing more.
  • No context is provided for why the SKYWATCH instruction was triggered — there may have been a routine reason such as a known meteor event.
  • There is no indication whether any pilot reports were actually received in response to this directive.
  • Redaction levels are low, but the document is inherently sparse — brevity is not the same as transparency.
  • The incident date listed in the release metadata says 2023, but the document itself is dated 21 February 2008 — the discrepancy likely reflects when it was catalogued or released, not when the event occurred.
