Alaska Airlines Crew Reported Balloon-Like Object at 40,000 Feet Over Montana

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

An FAA incident report documents a commercial Alaska Airlines crew (flight ASA18) reporting a balloon-like object between 40,000 and 45,000 feet while flying eastbound near Billings, Montana on 2 February 2023. The FAA classified it as an Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon and closed the case with no further explanation recorded.

On 2 February 2023 at 00:09 UTC, the crew of Alaska Airlines flight ASA18 — a commercial flight from Portland (PDX) to New York (JFK) — reported an object to air traffic control. The crew described it as a balloon positioned off the left side of their aircraft, at an altitude between flight level 400 and 450 (roughly 40,000 to 45,000 feet). Their own aircraft was flying eastbound at flight level 350 (about 35,000 feet), meaning the object appeared to be 5,000 to 10,000 feet above them. The sighting occurred approximately 10 nautical miles southeast of Billings, Montana. The FAA logged the incident under its SKYWATCH system and assigned it the primary code "Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon." No radar data, photographs, or corroborating reports from other aircraft are included in this document. The case is now closed. The report does not record any follow-up investigation, identification of the object, or conclusion about what the crew saw. No aircraft tail number is listed in the record, which is unusual but not necessarily a sign of deliberate redaction. It is worth noting that this sighting took place in early February 2023, during a period of heightened public and government attention to aerial objects following the Chinese surveillance balloon incidents.

What to know

  • This is a single-source pilot observation — no radar data, imagery, or second-aircraft corroboration is included in the report.
  • The crew described the object as a 'balloon.' That is the observer's characterisation, not a confirmed identification by the FAA or any other agency.
  • The timing (early February 2023) overlaps with the wave of US balloon/UAP awareness after the Chinese surveillance balloon shootdowns, which may have influenced reporting thresholds.
  • The FAA closed the case without recording any conclusion about what the object was. 'Closed' does not mean 'explained.'
  • No tail number is recorded for the reporting aircraft, which is atypical but does not on its own indicate redaction.
