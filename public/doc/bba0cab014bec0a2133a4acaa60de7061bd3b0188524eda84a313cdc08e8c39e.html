Airline Crew Reports Splitting Light at 50,000 Feet Over Buffalo

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

An FAA incident report records that the crew of United Airlines flight 541, flying from Denver to Boston on 28 October 2023, observed a single light split into two lights moving in a criss-cross pattern at 50,000–60,000 feet over the Buffalo, NY area. No evasive action was taken, and the report is marked as preliminary.

This document is an FAA Washington Operations Center incident report filed on 28 October 2023 at approximately 04:20 Eastern time. It records a UAP sighting reported by the crew of United Airlines flight 541, a Boeing 737 MAX 8 operating from Denver to Boston. According to the report, the flight crew observed a single light that split into two lights flying in a criss-cross pattern. The objects were estimated at flight level 500–600, corresponding to roughly 50,000–60,000 feet — well above the service ceiling of most commercial and military aircraft. The sighting occurred over the Buffalo, NY area, at coordinates 42.84°N, 79.51°W. The crew did not take evasive action. The observation was relayed through Cleveland Air Route Traffic Control Center (ARTCC) to FAA Operations. The report carries the reference code WOC 7-3333 JGJF. The document is labelled 'PRELIM INFO', meaning it represents an initial account rather than a completed investigation. There is no indication in the report of any follow-up analysis, radar corroboration, or agency conclusion about what the crew observed.

What to know

  • This is a single-source report from one flight crew. No corroborating radar data or independent witness accounts are included in the document.
  • The report is marked as preliminary information, not a finalised investigation or conclusion.
  • The estimated altitude of 50,000–60,000 feet exceeds the operational ceiling of most aircraft, which may make the crew's altitude estimate unreliable.
  • No details on weather conditions, visibility, or instrument readings are provided in the report.
  • The FAA report records what was reported but does not offer any explanation or identification of what was observed.
