Southwest Airlines Pilot Reported Strobe Light Above 38,000 Feet Near Parkersburg

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This FAA incident report documents a Southwest Airlines pilot observing an unidentified flashing light above their aircraft at 38,000 feet near Parkersburg, West Virginia, on 15 December 2014. Other pilots in the area later suggested the light was likely a satellite or space debris, and the case was closed.

On 15 December 2014, the pilot of Southwest Airlines flight SWA2346 — a Boeing 737-800 cruising at 38,000 feet — reported seeing a strobe light flashing roughly once every 30 seconds, about 20 nautical miles west of Parkersburg. The light appeared to be above the aircraft. The pilot in command formally reported the incident as a UFO. Indianapolis Center (the FAA air traffic control facility handling the area, identified as ZID) queried other aircraft nearby. Initially, no other pilots confirmed seeing the light. However, other aircraft subsequently reported that the light was high in the atmosphere and was possibly a satellite or piece of space debris. The flight had been travelling from Chicago Midway (MDW) to Parkersburg (PKB), but the destination was changed to Baltimore-Washington International (BWI). The report does not explain whether the diversion was related to the sighting. The FAA logged this under its SKYWATCH incident reporting system and closed the case. The report contains no radar data, no sensor recordings, and no record of any follow-up investigation. It is a single-page narrative summary of pilot communications.

What to know

  • This is a single-source pilot observation with no radar data or sensor recordings to corroborate it.
  • Other pilots in the area attributed the light to a satellite or space debris — the sighting was not confirmed as anomalous.
  • The incident occurred in 2014, before formal UAP reporting frameworks existed, so it was not investigated under current standards.
  • The report does not explain why the flight diverted from Parkersburg to Baltimore-Washington — it may be entirely unrelated to the sighting.
  • There is minimal redaction in this document, but equally minimal detail — it is a brief incident log, not an investigation report.
