Multiple Airline Crews Reported Fast-Moving Bright Light Over Idaho

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This FAA SKYWATCH incident report documents a May 2015 sighting in which the crews of United Airlines flight 935 and Alaska Airlines flight 794, both cruising near 40,000 feet over southwestern Idaho, independently reported a bright light above them moving northeast at excessive speed. Multiple other aircraft in the area corroborated the sighting, and the FAA notified the Western Air Defense Sector.

This document is a single-page FAA SKYWATCH Incident Report, a standardised form the Federal Aviation Administration uses to log unusual airspace events. It was filed at 23:46 UTC on 17 May 2015 through Salt Lake City Air Route Traffic Control Center (ZLC) and later released as part of a 2023 batch of FAA UAP records. The report describes an incident over rural southwestern Idaho, at coordinates 44°35'N / 116°48'W. The crew of United Airlines flight 935, a Boeing 777-200 flying from Los Angeles to London Heathrow at 40,000 feet, reported a bright light above them traveling northeast at what they called an excessive rate of speed. The crew of Alaska Airlines flight 794, flying at 39,000 feet in the same area, independently reported the same object. Multiple additional aircraft also confirmed the sighting. The FAA's Denver headquarters instructed Salt Lake City center to forward the report to the UFO Reporting Center and notified WADS, the Western Air Defense Sector, for situational awareness. The report's status is marked "Closed." No identification of the object is offered anywhere in the document. It is described only as a bright light — no size, shape, altitude estimate, or radar data is referenced. The report contains no follow-up investigation, no sensor data, and no photographs. The FAA did not reach or record a conclusion about what the object was.

What to know

  • This is a single-page closed report with no follow-up investigation attached — it records that a sighting happened, not what the object was.
  • The object description is limited to 'bright light' at 'excessive speed' — no radar tracks, sensor readings, photographs, or altitude estimates for the object itself are included.
  • The sighting is based entirely on visual reports from pilots. Multiple independent crews corroborated it, which adds weight, but there is no instrument data to confirm speed, altitude, or trajectory.
  • The incident occurred in 2015 but was released as part of a 2023 FAA records batch — the document is not newly created, just newly public.
  • The report shows minimal redaction, but also minimal detail — the brevity itself limits what can be concluded.
