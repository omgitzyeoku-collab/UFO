Commercial Pilot Reports Jetpack-Like Object at 6,000 Feet Near Florida

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This is a one-page FAA incident report from March 2023 in which a commercial aircraft crew reported seeing what appeared to be a jetpack travelling southbound at 6,000 feet near Ormond Beach, Florida. The FAA logged it as an unidentified aerial phenomenon and closed the case without documented resolution.

On 26 March 2023 at 17:05, the crew of commercial flight JTZ623 — a Bombardier CL-35 operated by JTZ — filed a report with the FAA while flying northbound at 6,000 feet, roughly 10 nautical miles northeast of Ormond Beach, Florida. They described seeing what appeared to be a jetpack heading in the opposite direction, southbound, at approximately the same altitude. The FAA logged the sighting under its SKYWATCH system with the primary code "Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon." A facility identified in the report as "TY" was notified. The case was subsequently closed. The report is notably brief — just one page — and contains minimal investigative detail. There is no mention of corroborating radar data, no additional witness accounts, and no tail number recorded for the reporting aircraft. The case was closed without any documented explanation of what the object was. The description itself is unusual. Conventional jetpacks rarely operate at 6,000 feet altitude, which raises the possibility that the crew may have been observing something else entirely. The FAA report does not explore this question. No people are named in the document, and redaction appears minimal — the report is simply sparse rather than heavily censored.

What to know

  • This is a single-source pilot report with no corroborating radar data or independent witness accounts included in the document.
  • The case was closed without any documented resolution or identification of the object.
  • Conventional jetpacks rarely reach 6,000 feet, so the pilot's description may reflect a best guess rather than a confirmed identification.
  • The report is unusually brief (one page) with minimal investigative detail, which limits what can be concluded from it.
  • No tail number was recorded for the reporting aircraft, which is atypical for FAA incident reports.
