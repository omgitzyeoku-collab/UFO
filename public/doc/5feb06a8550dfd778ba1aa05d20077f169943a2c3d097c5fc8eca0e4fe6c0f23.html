FAA Report: Pilot Saw Vast Field of Flashing Lights Over Kansas

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report documenting a general aviation pilot's sighting of unidentified flashing white lights while flying at 35,000 feet on 27 September 2022. The lights stretched below the aircraft for over 100 nautical miles near Liberal, Kansas, and the case was closed with no explanation provided.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report — a standard form the Federal Aviation Administration uses to log unusual aerial sightings reported by pilots. It was filed under the code "Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon" with reference number 237UAP00116. On 27 September 2022 at 06:00, the crew of a Gulfstream IV business jet (callsign N127RR) was flying southeast at 35,000 feet on a route from Portland (PDX) to Oklahoma City (OKC). Approximately 50 nautical miles west-northwest of Liberal, Kansas, the crew reported seeing what they described as "a sea of flashing white lights" below their altitude. The lights were not very bright but were visible for over 100 nautical miles — a substantial distance. No evasive action was taken. The report was logged under the Kansas City Air Route Traffic Control Center (ZKC) with MOR ID ZKC-M-2022/09/27-0001. Aviation Weather Operations (AWO) was advised of the sighting. The FAA marked the case as Closed, but the report contains no resolution, explanation, or follow-up analysis of what the lights were. Several fields in the report are blank, including the aircraft operator name and tail number, which limits the ability to independently verify the flight. No corroborating radar data, satellite imagery, or additional witness accounts are included in the document.

What to know
