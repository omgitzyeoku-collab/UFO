Frontier Airlines Crew Reports Strange Lights Over Georgia

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This FAA incident report documents a sighting by the crew of Frontier Airlines Flight 101 on April 8, 2023. While flying at 38,000 feet near Dublin, Georgia, the crew observed intermittent white lights moving back and forth in the upper atmosphere. No evasive action was taken and no follow-up investigation is documented.

On the night of April 8, 2023, the crew of Frontier Airlines Flight 101 — an Airbus A320neo flying from San Juan, Puerto Rico to Atlanta — spotted something unusual in the sky. While cruising northwest at 38,000 feet near Dublin, Georgia, they reported seeing intermittent white lights moving roughly 20 degrees left and right in the upper atmosphere. The crew did not take evasive action, and the sighting was relayed through Atlanta Air Route Traffic Control Center (ARTCC) to the FAA's Washington Operations Center, which logged it as UAP record 237UAP00498. The document is a short operational report, not an investigation. It records the crew's account but includes no radar data, no sensor readings, and no description of the object's shape, size, or speed beyond the light characteristics. There is no documented follow-up or resolution. The report shows minimal redaction. The coordinates (32.56°N, 82.98°W), flight details, and crew observations are all recorded in full. What it does not contain is any attempt by the FAA to explain or identify what the crew saw.

What to know

  • This is a single-source pilot report with no corroborating radar or sensor data mentioned in the document.
  • The FAA logged the sighting but the document contains no follow-up investigation or explanation of what the lights were.
  • The description — white lights moving 20 degrees left and right — could be consistent with various atmospheric or optical phenomena. The report does not rule anything in or out.
  • No details about the object's shape, size, or speed are provided beyond the light characteristics.
  • This is preliminary operational information, not an analytical assessment or conclusion by the FAA.
