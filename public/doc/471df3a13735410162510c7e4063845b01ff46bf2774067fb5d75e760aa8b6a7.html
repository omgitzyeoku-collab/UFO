Delta Pilot Reports Recurring Light Above 40,000 Feet Near Memphis

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This FAA SKYWATCH incident report documents a Delta Air Lines pilot who observed a single unidentified light above their aircraft six times while flying near Memphis in September 2022. The light would shine for 3–4 minutes then fade, appeared above 40,000 feet, and the case was closed with no explanation provided.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report — a standardised form the Federal Aviation Administration uses to log reports of unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP). It was filed under the identifier 237UAP00110 and references an event from September 2022. According to the report, a Delta Air Lines Airbus A321 operating as flight DAL2583 was flying northwest at 36,000 feet when the crew spotted something above them. The initial sighting was approximately 100 nautical miles southeast of Memphis. The object is described as a single light that would shine for 3–4 minutes and then fade. The pilot in command reported seeing the light a total of six times, later placing it roughly 60–80 nautical miles north of Memphis and above 40,000 feet — meaning it was higher than the aircraft's own cruising altitude. The report lists the operator type as commercial and references a facility code of ZME (Memphis Center). The tail number field is blank, and no radar data or sensor corroboration is mentioned anywhere in the document. The entire account rests on the pilot's visual observation. The FAA marked the case as Closed. No resolution, identification, or follow-up explanation is provided in the document. The report simply records what the pilot reported and stops there.

What to know

  • This is a single-source report based entirely on one pilot's visual observation — no radar data, photographs, or sensor corroboration is mentioned.
  • The case was marked Closed with no resolution or explanation, which means the FAA neither identified nor ruled out any cause.
  • The aircraft tail number field is blank, which limits the ability to independently verify the specific aircraft involved.
  • The incident date references in the document (September 11–12, 2022) suggest the sighting may have occurred late on September 11 UTC, though the report is dated September 12.
  • Redaction levels appear low — this is not a heavily censored document, but it is also a brief form with limited detail to begin with.
