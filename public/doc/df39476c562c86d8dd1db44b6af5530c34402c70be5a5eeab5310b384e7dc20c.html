Two Aircraft Reported Bright Fast-Moving Objects Over Georgia in 2010

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This FAA SKYWATCH incident report documents two aircraft reporting unidentified flying objects near Alma, Georgia on 17 November 2010. The pilots described two fast-moving targets with light too bright to be a satellite; NORAD investigated but no military activity was found in the area, and the case was closed without a recorded explanation.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report — a standardised form the Federal Aviation Administration uses to log unusual airspace events. It records an incident from 17 November 2010, though it was released publicly as part of a 2023-era collection of UAP records. On that date, two aircraft — registered as LN30LJ and N27JH — reported seeing unidentified flying objects near AMG (Alma, Georgia) airspace. The pilots described the objects as two fast-moving targets emitting very bright light, brighter than a satellite would appear. The FAA contacted both EADS (Eastern Air Defense Sector) and NORAD (North American Aerospace Defense Command) to check for military activity in the area. Neither organisation reported any. NORAD was noted as investigating the reports. No other aircraft filed similar sightings. The FAA recommended that ZJX (Jacksonville Air Route Traffic Control Center) interview the pilots in command and offer them official UFO reporting forms, should they choose to file. The case status is marked as Closed, but no resolution or explanation for the sightings is recorded in the document. No pilot names appear in the report — only aircraft registration numbers are provided. The document contains no instrument data, photographs, or follow-up investigation results.

What to know

  • This is a single incident report with no follow-up results or resolution included — the case is marked Closed without explanation.
  • The descriptions of the objects come from pilot observations only, with no instrument data or radar records included in this document.
  • Pilot identities are not named; only aircraft registration numbers are given.
  • The document dates from 2010 but was released under a 2023-era collection, meaning it is a historical record rather than a recent incident.
  • The brightness comparison ('too bright to be a satellite') is subjective and not corroborated by any measurement.
