FAA Report: Jellyfish-Shaped Object With Colored Lights Seen Near La Crosse

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

An FAA SKYWATCH incident report documents an unidentified aerial phenomenon observed by air traffic controllers near La Crosse, Wisconsin on 7 October 2022. The object, described as resembling a jellyfish with flashing green, red, and blue lights, was tracked briefly on radar before contact was lost, and no aircraft were ever identified.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report filed under case number 237UAP00117. It records an unidentified aerial phenomenon observed on 7 October 2022 at 01:56 UTC near La Crosse Regional Airport (LSE) in Wisconsin. The reporting facility was Minneapolis Center (ZMP), and the case is marked as closed with no resolution or identification noted. Controllers in the La Crosse air traffic control tower (LSE ATCT) visually observed an object approximately 5 nautical miles south of the airport at an altitude of 1,500 feet, traveling westbound. They described it as having flashing green, red, and blue lights and resembling a jellyfish. No aircrew reported seeing the object — the tower controllers were the only observers. Minneapolis Center (ZMP) detected a primary radar target in the same area, meaning the object showed up on radar but was not transmitting a transponder signal. By 02:05, roughly nine minutes after the initial observation, ZMP reported no further radar returns. No aircraft were identified in the area, and the report's fields for callsign, aircraft type, tail number, and operator are all blank. The case was closed without any explanation or identification of the object. Internal FAA notifications were made (AWO/TY notified), and the report notes that a page was issued. No further investigation or follow-up is documented.

What to know

  • This is a single-source observation — only the La Crosse tower controllers saw the object visually. No pilots or other witnesses corroborated the sighting.
  • The radar return was a primary-only target, meaning no transponder signal was received. Primary-only returns can be caused by weather, birds, or other non-aircraft objects, which limits what radar detection alone can confirm.
  • The case is marked 'Closed' with no identification or conclusion recorded. This does not mean the object was confirmed as anomalous — it means no explanation was documented.
  • The 'jellyfish' description comes from a single observer's visual impression and is inherently subjective.
  • Redaction levels in this document appear low, but several key fields (callsign, aircraft, tail number, operator) are simply blank rather than redacted, suggesting no associated aircraft was ever identified.
