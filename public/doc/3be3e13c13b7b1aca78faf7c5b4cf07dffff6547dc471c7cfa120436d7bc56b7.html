Aeromexico Crew Reports Three White Lights Over San Antonio at 35,000 Feet

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This FAA Washington Operations Center notification documents a UAP sighting reported by the crew of Aeromexico Flight 692 on May 17, 2023. While flying at 35,000 feet near San Antonio, Texas, the crew observed an unidentified aerial phenomenon with three white lights directly ahead of their aircraft. No evasive action was taken and no investigation findings are included.

On May 17, 2023, at 2:15 AM Central Daylight Time, the crew of Aeromexico Flight 692 — a Boeing 737 MAX 8 flying from Mexico City to Toronto — reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon to air traffic control. The aircraft was at Flight Level 350 (approximately 35,000 feet) and roughly 5 nautical miles north of San Antonio, Texas, at coordinates 29.534°N, 98.469°W. The crew described the object as having three white lights, positioned at the 12 o'clock position — meaning directly ahead of the aircraft. Despite the proximity implied by the sighting, no evasive action was taken. Houston Air Route Traffic Control Center (ARTCC) was the facility that received the report. The document is an FAA operations center incident notification — essentially a first-pass record that an event occurred. It is not a completed investigation and contains no conclusions about what the crew observed. No law enforcement was notified in connection with the sighting. The record contains minimal redaction. However, it is a single-source report: only the flight crew's account is documented, with no mention of corroborating radar data, other aircraft witnesses, or follow-up analysis.

What to know

  • This is a preliminary FAA operations center notification, not a completed investigation or formal conclusion about what was observed.
  • The report is single-source — it reflects only the flight crew's account, with no corroborating radar data or additional witness statements included in the document.
  • No evasive action was taken and no law enforcement was notified, which may indicate the crew did not perceive an immediate collision risk.
  • The aircraft type (Boeing 737 MAX 8) is listed at amber confidence, meaning it appears in the source but with less certainty than other fields.
  • The document does not identify or attempt to explain the phenomenon — it records only that the sighting was reported.
