Air Force Report Models Space-Booster Failures, Not UFO Incidents

Department of War 9/10/96 ✓ verified against source

This 1996 Department of the Air Force report, prepared by Research Triangle Institute, is a technical risk-engineering study on how to model unlikely space-booster failure modes for launch-area safety calculations. Despite appearing in a UAP document collection, it contains no UFO or unidentified aerial phenomena content — it covers conventional rocket failure data for Atlas, Delta, Titan, and Thor vehicles.

In September 1996, the Research Triangle Institute's Launch Systems Safety Department delivered a 180-page technical report to the Air Force under contract FO4703-91-C-0112. The report, numbered RTI/5180/77-43F, was sponsored by the safety offices at Patrick Air Force Base in Florida and Vandenberg Air Force Base in California. Its purpose was narrow and practical: improve how a risk-analysis program called DAMP (facility DAMage and Personnel injury) predicts where debris might land when a rocket goes off course. The study catalogs six possible ways a launch vehicle can behave — five failure modes and one normal mode. It focuses especially on "Mode-5" failures, which are rare malfunctions that cause a rocket to veer sharply from its intended path, such as executing a sustained turn away from the flight line. Though unlikely, Mode-5 failures can produce nearly all the risk to populated areas located uprange or far from the flight line, making them critical to model correctly. To build its models, the report draws on historical launch data covering 1,186 flights — 532 Atlas, 232 Delta, 337 Titan, and 85 Thor — from the beginning of each program through August 1996. It includes specific failure case studies: an Atlas 145D in July 1962 that deviated 60 degrees in yaw due to faulty guidance equations; an Atlas 68E in December 1980 that lost pitch control and pivoted into a retrofire attitude before breaking apart; a Joust rocket in June 1991 that pitched up radically after structural failure at roughly 14 seconds into flight; and a Red Tigress sounding rocket in August 1991 that made a near 90-degree right turn within seconds of clearing the launcher at Cape Canaveral. The document was marked unclassified but had its distribution limited to US Government agencies and contractors. Its authors include James A. Ward, Jr., Robert M. Montgomery, Martin Kinna, and Louis J. Ullian, Jr. There is low evidence of redaction in the document.

What to know

  • This is not a UAP or UFO incident report. It is a conventional risk-engineering study about rocket launch failures. Its presence in a UAP document collection (war.gov/UFO/PURSUE) may be misleading.
  • All events described in the document have known, conventional explanations — rocket malfunctions caused by structural failures, faulty guidance, or loss of control.
  • The document's distribution was restricted to US Government agencies and contractors; its current declassification status should be verified independently.
  • The report's incident date of September 10, 1996 appears to be the report's delivery or publication date, not the date of any specific incident.
  • Some data points, including sponsoring agency details and specific failure case dates, carry amber confidence ratings, meaning minor textual discrepancies exist between the verified quotes and the extracted facts.
