Delta Crew Reports Stationary White Triangle at 36,000 Feet Over Idaho

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

An FAA incident report documents the crew of Delta Air Lines flight DAL1480 observing a stationary white triangular object at cruising altitude near Pocatello, Idaho on 14 July 2024. The object did not appear on air traffic control radar, and the case was closed without explanation.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report — part of the agency's system for logging unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP) reported by pilots. It records an observation made by the crew of Delta Air Lines flight DAL1480, a commercial flight travelling from Dallas/Fort Worth (DFW) to Seattle (SEA) on 14 July 2024 at 22:46 UTC. While flying westbound at flight level 360 (approximately 36,000 feet), the crew reported seeing an unidentified object off the right side of the aircraft, roughly 18 nautical miles east of Pocatello, Idaho. They described it as a white triangle that appeared stationary at approximately the same altitude as their aircraft. Air traffic control checked their radar but did not detect the object. The report notes plainly: "The UAP was not observed on ATC facility radar system." No further identification or explanation is provided in the document. The case is marked as closed. There is no indication of follow-up investigation, additional witness statements, or any agency conclusion about what the object was. The document does not mention whether photographs, flight recorder data, or other corroborating evidence were collected.

What to know

  • This is a single-source pilot report with no radar corroboration — the object was not detected by any ATC radar system.
  • The report does not state how many crew members observed the object or how long the sighting lasted.
  • The case is marked closed with no resolution: the FAA did not identify the object or offer an explanation.
  • There is a date discrepancy: the CSV metadata lists the incident year as 2023, but the document itself is dated 14 July 2024.
  • No redactions are evident in this document, but the level of detail is limited — it is a brief incident log, not an investigation report.
