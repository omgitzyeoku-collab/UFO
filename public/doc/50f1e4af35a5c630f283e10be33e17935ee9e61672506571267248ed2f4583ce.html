FBI Files Show Eight Years of UFO Reports Across Washington State

FBI July 1952-1960 Washington State, United States ✓ verified against source

This FBI file compiles memoranda, complaint forms, and inter-agency correspondence between the FBI's Seattle office and the Air Force regarding multiple UFO sighting reports in Washington State from 1952 to 1960. The reports range from civilian eyewitness accounts to a radar-tracked incident that triggered fighter jet scrambles, though at least one set of reported objects was later identified as military flares.

This document is a collection of FBI records from the Seattle field office covering UFO sighting reports across Washington State between July 1952 and early 1960. The FBI coordinated these reports with the Air Force Office of Special Investigations (OSI) at McChord Air Force Base, and findings were forwarded to the Air Technical Intelligence Center at Wright-Patterson AFB in Ohio. The file contains several distinct incidents. In June 1954, a ground observer group supervisor named Gail Plock reported two half-moon shaped objects at roughly 1,000 feet altitude over Pasco. Around the same time, a radar station at Neah Bay tracked an unidentified target about 38–40 miles to its southwest, prompting the Air Force to scramble jet interceptors from McChord Field. As the fighters approached, the radar blip split into two separate contacts. An intelligence summary tentatively evaluated the radar targets as atmospheric phenomena comparable to large aircraft with speeds near Mach One — but that evaluation was described as tentative, not conclusive. Other reports in the file include a Seattle woman, Delma J. Segram, who observed a large silvery object high above Sand Point Naval Air Station; a Kennewick police sergeant, Gordon Richardson, who described a shining metallic sphere roughly 30 feet across hovering at 200–300 feet before departing at extreme speed; and a self-described design supervisor in Richland who claimed to have watched an object through 30-power glasses before it vanished eastward in about 10 seconds. Notably, one set of reported UFOs near Cape Disappointment was positively identified as Mark Six aircraft flares, which were later destroyed by a demolition squad. The document is heavily redacted throughout, with many pages nearly illegible due to poor scan quality and official redactions. Portions were withheld under FOIA exemption b(3), citing protection of intelligence sources and methods. Significant context is missing as a result.

What to know

  • The document is heavily redacted and many pages are nearly illegible, meaning substantial context is missing from what can be read.
  • Most sightings are single-witness accounts with no independent corroboration noted in the surviving text.
  • At least one reported UFO incident (Cape Disappointment) was positively identified as military flares — not all reports in this file represent unexplained phenomena.
  • The intelligence evaluation of the Neah Bay radar incident as 'atmospheric targets' was described as tentative, not a firm conclusion.
  • Material was withheld under FOIA exemption b(3) for intelligence sources and methods, so this release does not represent the complete file.
