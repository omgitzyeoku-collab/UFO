Proposed DHS Program to Study Non-Human Materials Was Rejected and Never Operated

AARO ✓ verified against source

KONA BLUE was a proposed Department of Homeland Security special access program intended to investigate "non-human biologics" and advanced aerospace vehicles, building on earlier Pentagon-funded research. It was shut down six months after being proposed in 2011 due to inadequate justification, and no materials, data, or funding were ever transferred to it.

This document, published by the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), is a historical summary of a proposed government program called KONA BLUE. It is not the proposal itself — it is AARO's account of what was proposed, what happened, and why the program never got off the ground. KONA BLUE grew out of an earlier effort called AAWSAP/AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Application Program / Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program), which was run by the Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) from 2009 to 2012. That program was funded through congressional earmarks, with Bigelow Aerospace — a Nevada-based company — serving as the primary contractor. The DIA eventually terminated AAWSAP/AATIP, citing a lack of merit and lack of utility in the reports Bigelow produced. In 2011, the DHS Under Secretary for Science and Technology established KONA BLUE as a Prospective Special Access Program (PSAP), describing it as a sensitive compartment to protect the retrieval and exploitation of "non-human biologics." Congressional interest and possible homeland security impacts were cited as justification. Six months later, the DHS Deputy Secretary disapproved the program and ordered its immediate termination, citing inadequate justification and insufficient information on personnel and budget. AARO states plainly that no data or material of any kind was ever transferred to or collected by DHS under KONA BLUE. Information from the earlier AAWSAP/AATIP program remains in DIA's archived holdings. The documents were declassified jointly by the Department of Defense and DHS for public release. Most redactions were made by the DoD, with a single attorney-client redaction by DHS on page 38.

What to know

  • This document is AARO's summary of the KONA BLUE proposal, not the proposal itself — it is a secondary source describing primary documents.
  • The claimed purpose of protecting "non-human biologics" came from interviewee claims and the proposal text; AARO found no evidence any such materials existed or were transferred.
  • KONA BLUE was never operational. All claims about its intended work come from an unapproved proposal that was shut down within six months.
  • Redactions were made by the Department of Defense, meaning some information from the original proposal documents is not visible in the released materials.
  • The confidence ratings on the stated purpose (retrieval of non-human biologics) are lower than on the program's administrative history, because the purpose claim traces to proposal language and interviews rather than independently verified fact.
