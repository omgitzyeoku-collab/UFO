Navy Crew Tracked Round Cold Object Over Gulf of Aden in 2020

Department of War 10/15/20 Arabian Sea ✓ verified against source

A U.S. Navy Range Fouler Reporting Form records a one-minute encounter with an unidentified round object detected on infrared sensors over the Gulf of Aden on October 15, 2020. The object was slow-moving at roughly 20 mph but made abrupt directional changes, and the document is heavily redacted throughout.

On October 15, 2020, a military crew from the 1172 ATKS squadron was flying an intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance (ISR) mission at 19,073 feet above terrain over the Gulf of Aden in the Arabian Sea. At 14:18 Zulu time, their infrared sensor picked up an unauthorized object in controlled airspace. The encounter lasted approximately one minute. The crew described the object as round and cold on infrared. Their sensor was set to "black hot" mode, meaning warm objects appear dark and cold objects appear bright — and this object showed up bright white, indicating it was colder than the surrounding environment. It was traveling northwest at roughly 20 miles per hour and made several abrupt directional changes during the brief observation. The sensor was pointed about 50 degrees below the aircraft, with the object at a slant range of 4.06 nautical miles and a ground range of 4.78 kilometers. The reporting form was filed by a junior officer (O-2 rank) and documents a single crew member's observation. No corroborating sensor data or independent witnesses are referenced anywhere in the form. The document itself notes that all descriptive language reflects the reporter's subjective interpretation at the time. The form is heavily redacted under privacy exemptions. The observer's name, email, geographic coordinates, aircraft side number, bureau number, and contact details are all blacked out. The document was approved for public release on March 27, 2026, under USCENTCOM case numbers MOR 26-0038 through 26-0046.

What to know

  • This is a single observer's account recorded on a standard reporting form. No radar data, video, or independent corroboration is referenced in the document.
  • Heavy redactions remove the observer's identity, exact coordinates, and aircraft identifiers, making independent verification difficult.
  • The object's cold infrared signature and low speed (20 mph) could be consistent with a balloon or other slow-moving conventional object, though the reported abrupt directional changes would be unusual for such objects.
  • The source PDF has poor OCR quality, meaning some numerical values may contain transcription errors — for example, one field reading '261130' has an ambiguous meaning.
  • The form's own instructions warn that all descriptive and estimative language reflects the reporter's subjective interpretation, not an official agency conclusion about what the object was.
