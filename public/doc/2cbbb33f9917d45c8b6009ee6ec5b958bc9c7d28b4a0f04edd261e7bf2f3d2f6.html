U.S. Senator's Party Reported Two Disc-Shaped Aircraft Over Soviet Azerbaijan

Department of War 10/14/55 Azerbaijan ✓ verified against source

A formerly secret U.S. Air Force intelligence report documents eyewitness accounts from Senator Richard Russell's traveling party, who observed two disc-shaped aircraft ascending near-vertically in the trans-Caucasus region of the USSR on or around October 1955. The report was flagged as relevant to the USAF's Unidentified Flying Objects project and is heavily redacted.

This document is an Air Intelligence Information Report (AF Form 112) produced by the U.S. Department of War. It was originally classified SECRET with a NOFORN (no foreign distribution) restriction. The report was prepared by Lt. Col. Thomas S. Ryan and reviewed by Col. B.O. Batton, based on information from the U.S. Air Attaché. The report describes what happened when a group of three American visitors — referred to as the "Russell Group" after U.S. Senator Richard Russell — were traveling by train through the Soviet Union on a route from Kiev through Baku, Azerbaijan, and Tbilisi. While in the trans-Caucasus region, the group observed two disc-shaped aircraft in sequence. The first disc ascended near-vertically, revolving slowly to the right, reaching roughly 6,000 feet before leveling off and heading north. A second disc repeated the same behaviour approximately one minute later. A searchlight beam was also observed in connection with the sighting. The three witnesses were debriefed in Prague on 12–13 October 1955 by U.S. intelligence personnel. One member of the group, identified as Hathaway, was noted as an excellent observer during debriefings. The Russell group had been invited to the USSR by Soviet authorities, and the broader trip included observations of Soviet military infrastructure — airfields, IL-28 bombers, and other military aircraft — suggesting the UFO sighting was incidental to a wider intelligence-gathering effort. The report explicitly notes the sighting's relevance to the USAF's project on Unidentified Flying Objects. However, the document is heavily redacted, and the extremely poor OCR quality of the released version makes many passages illegible or ambiguous. No definitive conclusion about what the objects were is visible in the available text.

What to know

  • The document is heavily redacted and the OCR scan quality is extremely poor, making many passages illegible — key details may be missing or misread.
  • The observations were made from a moving train, which limits how long and how clearly the witnesses could see the objects.
  • There is a time gap between the sighting and the debriefing in Prague (12–13 October 1955), which could affect the accuracy of witness recall.
  • While Senator Russell's involvement lends political weight to the report, the account still rests on a small number of witnesses (three) in a single incident with no known independent corroboration documented here.
  • Several key facts — including the exact shape description and the travel route — carry only amber confidence ratings due to poor document legibility, meaning they are probable but not fully certain readings of the original text.
