FAA Reports Unexplained Lights Over Great Falls Near Nuclear Base

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

An FAA incident report documents five or more unidentified lights seen circling at roughly 40,000 feet northeast of Great Falls, Montana on 2 December 2023. The lights were visible to air traffic controllers and independently confirmed by security at Malmstrom Air Force Base, but never appeared on radar. The case was closed with no explanation offered.

On 2 December 2023, air traffic controllers at the Great Falls (GTF) tower in Montana reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon to the northeast of their facility. They described five or more lights at approximately 40,000 feet and above, moving in a circular pattern at varying intensities and altitudes — a motion they compared to an aircraft holding pattern, though the movement was not always circular. The lights did not appear on the facility's radar system, meaning the observation was entirely visual. Controllers could not determine the distance to the phenomenon. Separately, security personnel at Malmstrom Air Force Base confirmed they could also see the lights. The nearby HAYS Military Operations Area was not active at the time, ruling out routine military exercises in that airspace. The FAA filed this as a SKYWATCH incident report under case number 237UAP00325, with the primary code "Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon." The report carries a secondary code of NOTIFICATION-LATE, indicating it may not have been filed at the time of the event. The case status is closed, but the document offers no resolution or identification of what the lights were. Malmstrom Air Force Base is home to intercontinental ballistic missile silos, making it one of the most security-sensitive installations in the United States. The report itself draws no connection between the sighting and the base's nuclear mission, but the proximity is notable context.

What to know

  • The phenomenon was observed visually only — it never appeared on radar, so there is no independent electronic corroboration of its presence or altitude.
  • The altitude estimate of 40,000 feet is approximate; controllers stated they could not determine the distance to the lights.
  • The report is marked NOTIFICATION-LATE, meaning it was not filed in real time and may reflect details recalled after the fact.
  • The case is closed with no stated resolution — the FAA did not identify what the lights were, but also did not characterise them as unexplainable.
  • Malmstrom AFB security confirmed seeing the lights, but the report provides no detail on what they observed or any follow-up investigation.
