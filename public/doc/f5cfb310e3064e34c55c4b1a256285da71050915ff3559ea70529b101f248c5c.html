FBI Agents Report Unexplained Lights Near a Pond in Late 2024

FBI November, 2024 Northeastern United States ✓ verified against source

An FBI internal report documents two special agents who personally observed unexplained pulsating and moving lights at treetop level during a planned field visit to a witness's property in the northeastern United States in November 2024. The document is heavily redacted, with all names, specific locations, and exact dates removed.

This document is an FBI FD-1057 electronic communication — a standard internal reporting form — filed after two FBI special agents visited a civilian witness's property somewhere in the northeastern United States in November 2024. The purpose of the visit was to follow up on UAP (Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon) reports and video the witness had been submitting to the FBI over the previous three years. The agents arrived at approximately 4:45pm in overcast, 48-degree weather with no rain. Shortly after, one agent observed a white pulsating light appear near the northern edge of a pond. It moved horizontally with erratic movements and lasted less than ten seconds. The light appeared at or below treetop level and did not reflect off the water or illuminate the area beneath it. Additional white and blue pulsing lights were later seen at treetop height in the tree line, along with plumes of broad light followed by red or white flashes. At approximately 5:30pm, both agents simultaneously observed what they described as a bright white light above a bright red light hovering at treetop height at the far end of the pond. The paired lights moved quickly from right to left, disappeared, then reappeared at their original position before repeating the movement and vanishing again. The agents ended their observation at roughly 6:10pm and departed by 6:15pm. Five photos were taken, but they were mostly blurry due to long exposure times without a tripod, providing little useful documentation. The document does not offer any conclusions about what the lights were. It records the observations without attempting to explain or classify them. The FBI has not publicly stated what, if anything, resulted from this visit or the broader investigation suggested by related documents in the same series (FBI-UAP-D004 through D008).

What to know

  • The document is heavily redacted: all agent names, the witness's identity, the specific location (including town and pond name), exact dates, and the case ID are all blacked out, making independent verification impossible.
  • The observations come from only two FBI agents and one civilian witness — a small number of observers for the described events.
  • The photographic evidence was described as 'mostly blurry' and providing little context, so there is minimal visual corroboration.
  • The lights were seen at or below treetop level near a wooded pond area, which could be consistent with various conventional explanations that the document does not explore or rule out.
  • The witness had been reporting UAP activity for three years prior; the document does not evaluate whether this reflects a persistent phenomenon or possible observer bias.
