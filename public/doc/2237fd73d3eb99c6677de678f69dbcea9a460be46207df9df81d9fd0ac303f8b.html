Learjet Pilot Reports Silver Softball-Sized Object Near Nantucket at 18,000 Feet

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

An FAA incident report documents a Learjet 45 pilot sighting a small, silver, softball-sized object while descending through 18,000 feet on approach to Nantucket on 19 October 2023. The case was reported to Boston Center and closed with no explanation provided.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report filed under the category "Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon." It records an event on 19 October 2023 at 11:41, involving a Learjet 45 (callsign N823HL) flying from Northeast Philadelphia Airport (PNE) to Nantucket Memorial Airport (ACK) as a general aviation flight. While descending through flight level 180 — roughly 18,000 feet — the pilot reported an unidentified object off the right side of the aircraft. The object was described as silver, about the size of a softball, and moving in a west/southwest direction. The sighting was reported to Boston Center (ZBW), the FAA facility responsible for air traffic control in that region. The FAA marked the case as closed. The document does not include any resolution, identification of the object, or further investigation notes. There is no corroborating radar data or additional witness testimony in the report. No tail number or operator name is recorded in the document beyond the callsign, which limits the ability to independently verify the flight details. The report is a single-page administrative record rather than a detailed investigation file.

What to know

  • This is a single-source pilot report — no radar data, photographs, or additional witnesses are included in the document.
  • The case is marked as closed with no identification or explanation of the object provided.
  • No operator name or tail number beyond the callsign N823HL is recorded, limiting independent verification.
  • The document shows low evidence of redaction, but it is also a brief administrative record rather than a full investigation.
  • A single visual sighting from a moving aircraft at 18,000 feet offers limited basis for determining the nature, size, or distance of the object.
