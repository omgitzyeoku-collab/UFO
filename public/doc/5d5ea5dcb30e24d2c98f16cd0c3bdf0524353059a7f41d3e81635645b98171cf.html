FAA Report: Commercial Pilot Saw Unidentified Lights at 50,000 Feet Over Missouri

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report from January 2024 documenting a commercial pilot's sighting of unidentified lights while flying at 40,000 feet over southern Missouri. The lights were estimated at 50,000–60,000 feet, moving northwest, and were not detected on air traffic control radar. The case was closed with no indication of follow-up.

This document is an FAA incident report filed under the agency's SKYWATCH programme, which tracks unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP) reported by pilots and air traffic controllers. It carries the identifier 237UAP00339 and references a Mandatory Occurrence Report (MOR) logged by Memphis Air Route Traffic Control Centre (ARTCC) on 19 January 2024. The report describes a sighting by the crew of a commercial flight operating under callsign EJA466, flying an E55P aircraft. The aircraft was southbound at flight level 400 (40,000 feet), travelling from St. Louis (STL) to an airport coded LBX. While over coordinates 36°54'N, 91°51'W — placing the aircraft over southern Missouri near the Arkansas border — the crew observed an unidentified aerial phenomenon off the right side of their aircraft. The crew described the phenomenon as lights travelling northwest at an estimated altitude of flight level 500 to 600, which corresponds to roughly 50,000–60,000 feet. That altitude range is well above the ceiling of most commercial aircraft and was based solely on the pilot's visual estimate, not instrument measurement. Air traffic control checked their radar systems and found no corresponding target — the lights did not appear on the ATC facility radar. No tail number was recorded for the reporting aircraft in the document. The report's status is listed as closed. There is no indication in the document of any follow-up investigation, resolution, or determination of what the lights were. The level of redaction in the document is low.

What to know

  • This is a single-source report based entirely on one pilot's visual observation — no radar data, no photographs, and no independent corroboration from other aircraft or ground observers.
  • The estimated UAP altitude of 50,000–60,000 feet is a visual guess made from a moving aircraft at night or in-flight conditions, not an instrument reading. Altitude estimates by eye are notoriously unreliable.
  • The case was closed with no documented follow-up investigation or explanation, which means the FAA neither confirmed nor ruled out any particular cause.
  • No aircraft tail number was recorded, which limits the ability to independently verify the flight details.
  • The coordinates and routing (STL origin, Memphis ARTCC facility) are internally consistent, placing the sighting over southern Missouri — but consistency does not itself confirm what was observed.
