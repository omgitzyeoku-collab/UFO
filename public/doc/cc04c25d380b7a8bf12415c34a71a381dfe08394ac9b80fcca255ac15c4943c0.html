Southwest Airlines Pilot Reported Flashing Lights Above Thunderstorm in 2023

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This FAA incident report documents a Southwest Airlines pilot observing three flashing lights moving above a thunderstorm while flying at 37,000 feet near Savannah, Georgia on April 15, 2023. No evasive action was needed, the UFO Reporting Center was notified, and the case was closed with no indication of further investigation.

On April 15, 2023, the crew of a Southwest Airlines Boeing 737 MAX 8 — flying under callsign SWG615 — reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon to the FAA. The aircraft was cruising at flight level 370 (37,000 feet) on a commercial route when the crew spotted something unusual ahead. What they described were three lights, flashing on and off, moving above a thunderstorm approximately 10 to 15 nautical miles in front of the aircraft and roughly 40 nautical miles east of Savannah. The lights appeared to be above the aircraft's own altitude of 37,000 feet. The crew did not need to take evasive action. The FAA logged the sighting through its SKYWATCH incident reporting system. The UFO Reporting Center was notified, and an Air Watch Officer (AWO) was advised. The report's status is listed as closed, with no indication that any follow-up investigation was conducted or that radar or sensor data corroborated the observation. The document is a short administrative form rather than a detailed investigative file. It records the basic facts of the sighting — time, location, altitude, and description — but does not offer any explanation or analysis of what the lights may have been.

What to know

  • This is a single-source pilot observation. The report does not mention any corroborating radar data, sensor recordings, or observations from other aircraft.
  • Flashing lights above a thunderstorm could have natural explanations — such as sprites, blue jets, or other upper-atmospheric electrical phenomena — none of which are explored in this report.
  • The case is marked as closed with no sign of further investigation or any conclusion about what was observed.
  • The aircraft tail number field is blank in the report, which limits independent verification of the specific aircraft involved.
  • Redaction levels appear low — this is a brief administrative form rather than a heavily censored document.
