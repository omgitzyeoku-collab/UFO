Federal Agents Reported Four UAP Sightings in Western U.S., October 2023

Department of War October, 2023 Westen United States ✓ verified against source

This Department of War document is a notional illustration map depicting four separate UAP (unidentified anomalous phenomena) incidents reported by U.S. federal law enforcement agents over several days in October 2023 in the western United States. The objects were described as a "fiery orb," a "dark kite-shaped object" with coloured lights, and a "translucent kite-shaped object" — but all imagery in the document is AI-generated and all locations shown are explicitly not real.

In October 2023, U.S. federal law enforcement special agents reported four separate sightings of unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP) in the western United States over a period of several days. The Department of War produced this document — designated DOW-UAP-D078 — as an illustrated summary of those reports. The incidents involved three distinct types of object. In one case, agents observed a large "fiery orb" projected against a ridgeline at an estimated distance of roughly 1,000 yards. A separate incident involved a "thin or dark kite-shaped object" with one red light and one white light seen at close range. A fourth incident described a "translucent kite-shaped object," also at close range. The description for a second incident appears to be truncated or missing from the document, possibly due to redaction or corruption. Critically, none of the images in this document are photographs or sensor data. Every illustration was artificially generated using genai.mil, the U.S. government's AI image generation tool. The document explicitly states that all locations shown are notional — they do not represent actual incident positions — and that distances and scale are not accurate. The document presents the agents' self-reported observations but does not include any conclusions about what the objects were. No corroborating sensor data, radar returns, or independent verification is mentioned. The agency neither confirms nor denies any explanation for the sightings.

What to know

  • All imagery in this document is AI-generated, not photographs or sensor captures — it is an illustration of reported events, not evidence of them.
  • Locations shown on the map are explicitly stated to be notional and do not represent where the incidents actually occurred.
  • All observations come from self-reported accounts by law enforcement agents — no corroborating sensor data or independent verification is mentioned.
  • The description for one of the four incidents (Incident 2) appears truncated or missing, suggesting partial redaction or document corruption.
  • The document draws no conclusions about what the reported objects were — it presents the reports without analysis or explanation.
