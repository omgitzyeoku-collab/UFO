Southwest Airlines crew reported flashing lights above their 737 over Virginia

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This FAA preliminary incident report documents a March 2023 sighting by the crew of Southwest Airlines flight 1307, who observed flashing lights above their aircraft at an estimated 100-mile distance while flying at 41,000 feet near Lawrenceville, Virginia. No evasive action was taken and no further investigation is recorded in this document.

On 22 March 2023, the crew of Southwest Airlines flight 1307 — a Boeing 737 MAX 8 flying from Fort Myers, Florida to Baltimore, Maryland — reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon to air traffic control. The aircraft was cruising northbound at Flight Level 410 (41,000 feet) approximately 10 nautical miles east of the Lawrenceville VORTAC navigation aid in Virginia when the crew spotted flashing lights on the left side of the aircraft, above them, at an estimated distance of 100 miles. The sighting was logged by the FAA's Washington Operations Center as preliminary information relayed through Washington ARTCC (Air Route Traffic Control Center), the facility managing that airspace. The crew reported no evasive action was taken. The document is a standard FAA Operations Center incident report — a brief, structured form capturing the basics of what was reported. It is not an investigation finding or a conclusion about what the lights were. The FAA logged the event and recorded the crew's account, but the report contains no follow-up analysis, no radar data, and no sensor corroboration. There is little evidence of redaction in this document. The report is simply brief by design — these preliminary logs capture who reported what, where, and when, without attempting to explain the phenomenon.

What to know

  • This is a preliminary report, not an investigation conclusion. The FAA logged what the crew said but did not assess what the lights were.
  • The 100-mile distance estimate is a visual guess by the flight crew — at night and altitude, such estimates can be highly imprecise.
  • Flashing lights at that distance above 41,000 feet could be consistent with satellites, astronomical objects, or other conventional sources. The report does not explore explanations.
  • This is a single-source account from one flight crew with no corroborating radar data, sensor readings, or independent witnesses mentioned.
  • The coordinates place the sighting over rural southern Virginia, near the North Carolina border.
