CIA Forwarded a German Scientist's Theory on Flying Discs in 1950

CIA 1950 ✓ verified against source

In July 1950, the CIA circulated a translated article by a German scientist proposing an aerodynamic explanation for 'Flying Discs.' The CIA explicitly marked the report as unevaluated information and made no judgment on its accuracy.

On 31 July 1950, the Central Intelligence Agency distributed Report No. SO DD-2743 — a one-page cover sheet forwarding a translated article by a German scientist. The article, titled 'The Mystery of the Flying Discs, a contribution to its possible explanation,' had been submitted for publication in Condor, a German-language magazine published in Chile. The information was acquired in Santiago, Chile, and dated to prior to mid-1950. The article proposed that Flying Discs could be explained through aerodynamic principles. However, only the CIA's cover sheet survives in the extracted text — the attached translation of the scientist's full article is not included, so the details of the theory are unavailable from this document alone. The CIA stamped the report 'THIS IS UNEVALUATED INFORMATION,' meaning the agency passed along the article without assessing whether its claims were accurate. The source was classified as documentary. The country subject line lists both Chile and Germany, reflecting the article's origin and the scientist's nationality. Moderate redaction is present in the classification markings and portions of the cover sheet. The scientist's name is garbled in the OCR text, making positive identification difficult.

What to know

  • The CIA explicitly marked this as unevaluated information — they made no assessment of the scientist's theory.
  • Only the cover sheet is available; the actual translated article with the scientist's full argument is missing from the extracted text.
  • The scientist's name is badly garbled by poor OCR quality and cannot be reliably identified from this document.
  • This is a single-source report with no corroboration noted by the CIA.
  • Moderate redaction is present in classification markings and parts of the cover sheet.
