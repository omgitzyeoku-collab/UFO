FBI Flying Disc Files Show Routine Forwarding and About 20 Percent Unexplained

FBI ✓ verified against source

This 1952 section of the FBI's flying disc case file (62-HQ-83894) is mostly administrative correspondence: Director Hoover's office acknowledged public sighting reports and forwarded them to the Air Force. It also contains an FBI lab analysis that cast doubt on a Florida scoutmaster's story, an anonymous German letter claiming saucers were Russian V-weapons, an occult group's claim of contact with saucer operators, and a Department of Defense statement noting roughly 20 percent of about 1,500 aerial-phenomena reports remained unexplained.

This document is a batch of internal FBI correspondence and forwarded reports from mid-1952, all filed under the Bureau's central flying disc case number 62-HQ-83894. Most of the material is procedural: letters from members of the public describing things they saw in the sky, followed by form replies from FBI Director J. Edgar Hoover's office explaining that such matters fall under the Air Force and forwarding the reports to the Air Force Office of Special Investigations (OSI). Field offices were instructed, via Bureau Bulletin #57 and SAC Letter #38, to collect basic details from anyone reporting a flying disc and pass the information to their local OSI contact. A few items stand out from the routine traffic. On August 8, 1952, two employees of the E. I. Du Pont Company reported seeing a blue light with an orange fringe, shaped like a saucer, flying at high speed in a northeasterly direction over the Savannah River Plant's 400 Area at about 9:30 PM. Separately, in the Paterson–Passaic, New Jersey area, two men named John K. Riley and George J. Spock reportedly photographed an unidentified aerial object on July 31, 1952. In Jackson, Minnesota, a resident found shiny material in a pasture over burned clover patches arranged in a triangle, and a neighbour reported seeing a steady white light circling the farm. The file also includes more unusual material. The FBI's Cincinnati office forwarded a translated anonymous German letter claiming that flying saucers were disk-shaped V-weapons about 48–50 metres in diameter, equipped with 45–50 automatic circular nozzles and a plexiglass sphere at the centre, now allegedly in Russian hands with a range of 30,000–35,000 kilometres. The letter named a constructor called Riedel as having worked on such weapons. None of this was corroborated. From San Diego, a field office reported on a group called Borderland Sciences Research Associates, whose members claimed to have conversed with the operators of flying saucers. According to the group, the operators said they were on a peaceful mission but possessed disintegrator weapons they would use against pursuing aircraft. The FBI treated the group's claims as occult-adjacent and unverified. The FBI Laboratory examined a burned cap belonging to a scoutmaster named D. Desvergers from West Palm Beach, Florida, who said he had encountered a hovering object. The lab found the singeing on the cap was non-uniform and noted an absence of singeing under a fold, suggesting the cap may not have been worn when the damage occurred. No residue was found to determine the cause. A Department of Defense public information statement included in the file noted that the Air Force had investigated approximately 1,500 reports of aerial phenomena since setting up a dedicated project on December 30, 1947. About 20 percent of those reports remained unexplained, but the statement said no pattern had emerged that could be interpreted as a menace to the United States. Of all reports, roughly 25 percent came from military personnel and about 8 percent from civil airline pilots, with the majority from civilians. Notably, the Air Force's Captain Ruppelt stated that the FBI had, to his knowledge, never been called upon to furnish reports on flying saucers — despite this file showing the Bureau routinely forwarded such reports to the Air Force.

What to know

  • The FBI's role here was almost entirely administrative — it collected public reports and passed them to the Air Force rather than conducting its own investigations into the sightings themselves.
  • The anonymous German letter about a Russian V-weapon flying saucer is completely unverified and uncorroborated within this file. It should not be treated as evidence of anything beyond the existence of the claim.
  • The Borderland Sciences Research Associates' claims about communicating with saucer occupants are described by the FBI field office as occult-adjacent and unverified.
  • OCR quality throughout the document is very poor, with many garbled characters and some pages nearly unreadable, meaning some details may be incomplete or slightly misread. A multi-page handwritten letter (pages 48–59) is largely illegible.
  • Redaction is moderate throughout the file, and multiple pages are cover sheets, routing slips, or heavily degraded scans with no readable content.
