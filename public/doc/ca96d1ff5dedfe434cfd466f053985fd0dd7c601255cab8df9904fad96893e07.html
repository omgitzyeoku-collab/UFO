NRC Emails Show Unidentified Object Near Pilgrim Nuclear Plant Stumped Multiple Agencies

NRC 2023 ✓ verified against source

This internal email chain from the Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) records an unidentified object spotted near the Pilgrim Nuclear Power Station in Plymouth, Massachusetts in late September 2015. Plymouth Police, the FAA, and Massachusetts State Police all responded but none could identify the object, which was officially classified as a UFO; a weather balloon was suggested but never confirmed.

In late September and early October 2015, staff at the Nuclear Regulatory Commission exchanged emails about an unidentified object spotted near the Pilgrim Nuclear Power Station in Plymouth, Massachusetts. The object was at thousands of feet altitude and was indiscernible from the ground. Plymouth Police arrived on site, the Federal Aviation Administration was contacted, and Massachusetts State Police dispatched a helicopter — but none of these agencies could identify the object. A weather balloon was floated as a possible explanation, but as the emails note, "no one was definite." The object was officially classified as a UFO. Despite the sighting occurring near a nuclear facility, NRC staff determined it posed no threat and no security incident report was filed. One staff member noted drily, "For the record we don't regulate UFOs." Senior NRC official Raymond Lorson added a sardonic reference to the Air Force using the weather balloon explanation frequently in the 1950s — a nod to the historical pattern of such explanations during the Project Blue Book era. The emails involve several named NRC personnel including Brett Klukan (Region I Regional Counsel), Dana Caron, Anthony Dimitriadis, John Cherubini, and Raymond Lorson. The tone throughout is informal and candid, reflecting internal discussion rather than a formal investigation. This document is notable not for what it proves but for what it reveals about how a federal nuclear safety agency handled an unexplained sighting near a sensitive facility: multiple agencies responded, none could explain it, and the matter was closed without further action.

What to know

  • This is an informal internal email chain, not a formal investigation report — it reflects staff discussion, not official NRC findings.
  • The metadata lists a 2023 incident date, but the emails are clearly dated September–October 2015; the 2023 date likely refers to when the records were compiled or released.
  • The weather balloon explanation was explicitly noted as uncertain — no agency confirmed it.
  • There is minimal redaction in this document, but no independent corroboration of the sighting details beyond the NRC staff's own account of what responding agencies told them.
  • Raymond Lorson's reference to 1950s Air Force weather balloon explanations suggests some internal scepticism, but should not be read as an official NRC position.
