Southwest Airlines crew reported strobing object at 55,000 feet over Virginia

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This is an FAA incident report documenting a sighting by the crew of Southwest Airlines flight SWA2067 on 28 August 2021. While flying at 40,000 feet over Richmond, Virginia, the crew reported a strobing object in a racetrack formation at an estimated 55,000 feet or above — well beyond the ceiling of most conventional aircraft. The case was closed with no explanation provided.

On 28 August 2021, the crew of Southwest Airlines flight SWA2067 — a Boeing 737 flying from Fort Myers, Florida to Baltimore, Maryland — reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon to air traffic control. The aircraft was northbound at 40,000 feet over Richmond, Virginia, under the watch of Washington Center (ZDC), the FAA facility responsible for that airspace. The crew described the object as strobing and moving in a racetrack formation ahead of the aircraft, near the airspace around Reagan National Airport (DCA). They estimated its altitude at approximately 55,000 feet or above — a height that exceeds the operational ceiling of nearly all conventional aircraft. The object was no longer visible after 06:37 UTC. The FAA notified multiple internal operations centres following the report, including AWO, TSOC, WOC, and CM. The incident was logged under the FAA's SKYWATCH programme, which tracks unidentified aerial phenomena. The case is marked as closed. No resolution, explanation, or identification of the object is provided in the document. There is no indication that radar data or additional witness accounts were included or consulted.

What to know

  • This is a single-source pilot report. No corroborating radar data or independent witness accounts are included in the document.
  • The estimated altitude of 55,000 feet or above exceeds the service ceiling of most conventional aircraft, but this is a visual estimate from the crew, not an instrument reading.
  • The phrase 'racetrack formation' is ambiguous — it could describe one object's flight path or multiple objects arranged in a pattern.
  • The case is marked 'Closed' with no explanation or identification provided, which means neither confirmed nor ruled out as a known object.
  • Redaction levels in this document appear low, but the report itself is brief and contains limited detail beyond the crew's initial observation.
