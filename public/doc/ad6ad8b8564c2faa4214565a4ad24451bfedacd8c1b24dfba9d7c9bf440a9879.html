Two Flight Crews Reported Bright Lights Moving Above 39,000 Feet

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This FAA SKYWATCH report documents a May 2022 incident in which a commercial pilot flying at 39,000 feet spotted a line of equally spaced bright lights moving eastward well above their altitude. A second aircraft independently confirmed the same sighting, but no further investigation was conducted and the case was closed.

On 12 May 2022 at 08:05 UTC, the crew of a commercial flight (callsign VTE611) reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon while flying northeast at FL390 (39,000 feet) over CSN VORTAC. The lights appeared directly ahead of the aircraft at the 12 o'clock position. The crew described an equally spaced line of very bright lights moving eastward, well above their cruising altitude. A second aircraft — UPS1114 — independently reported the same sighting, providing corroboration from a separate flight crew. The FAA did not notify law enforcement. Instead, the flight crew was directed to a website to self-report the sighting. The agency's internal office (AWO) was notified, but no follow-up investigation is documented in the file. The case is marked as closed. The report contains no radar data, photographs, or instrument readings. It relies entirely on visual observation by the two flight crews. No conclusion about what the lights were is offered in the document.

What to know

  • The description — equally spaced bright lights moving in a line — is consistent with Starlink satellite trains, which were frequent and widely reported in 2022. The document does not rule this out or consider it.
  • Two independent flight crews corroborated the sighting, which strengthens the observation but does not identify the phenomenon.
  • No radar data, photos, or instrument readings are included. The entire report rests on visual observation.
  • No follow-up investigation is documented. The crew was directed to a self-reporting website and the case was closed.
  • Redaction levels are low — this is a straightforward incident report, not a heavily censored intelligence document.
