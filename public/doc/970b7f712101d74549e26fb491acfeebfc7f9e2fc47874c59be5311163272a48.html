Boeing 747 crew and second aircraft report triangular lights over Vermont

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

An FAA incident report records that on 12 February 2023, a commercial Boeing 747-8 crew flying at 35,000 feet observed an erratically moving light estimated above 60,000 feet near Burlington, Vermont. Three lights repeatedly formed a triangle and merged into one; a second aircraft independently reported the same observation. The case was closed with no explanation provided.

This is a single-page FAA SKYWATCH incident report filed on 12 February 2023 at 08:25 UTC. It documents what the crew of a commercial Boeing 747-8, operating under callsign ICV93PK on a cargo route from Chicago O'Hare to Luxembourg, observed while flying eastbound at 35,000 feet. Approximately 43 nautical miles northwest of Burlington, Vermont, the crew reported a light estimated to be above 60,000 feet moving in an erratic pattern. They described three lights that appeared to form a triangle shape, then merged into a single light — a process that repeated several times. A second aircraft, flying northbound toward Montreal, independently reported seeing the same phenomenon. This multi-witness aspect is notable: two separate crews in different locations observed the same thing. The FAA's Aviation Weather Office (AWO) was notified. The case status is listed as "Closed" — but the report contains no resolution, no follow-up investigation details, and no explanation for what the crews observed. No radar data or instrument readings are referenced; all observations are visual only. The document is brief and administrative in nature. It records the event but does not analyse or explain it.

What to know

  • This is a single-page report with no follow-up investigation or resolution — 'Closed' does not mean 'explained'.
  • All observations are visual only; no radar data, photographs, or instrument readings are referenced in the document.
  • The altitude estimate of the phenomenon (above 60,000 feet) is the crew's visual estimate, not a measured value.
  • The tail number field is blank, which limits independent verification of the reporting aircraft.
  • Two crews reported the same observation, but no additional corroboration (ground radar, satellite, etc.) is documented.
