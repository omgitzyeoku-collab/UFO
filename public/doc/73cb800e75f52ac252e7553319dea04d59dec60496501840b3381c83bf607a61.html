Delta pilot reported multiple orbiting lights over Alabama in 2024

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

An FAA SKYWATCH incident report documents a Delta Air Lines pilot on flight DAL757 observing multiple lights orbiting at varying altitudes while cruising at 37,000 feet near Huntsville, Alabama on 14 February 2024. The lights were not detected on air traffic control radar, and the case was closed without explanation.

This is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report filed under case number 237UAP00353. It records a UAP sighting reported by the crew of a commercial Delta Air Lines flight (callsign DAL757) travelling eastbound from Phoenix to Atlanta. The sighting occurred on 14 February 2024 at approximately 09:30 UTC. The aircraft was at Flight Level 370 (37,000 feet) and roughly 50 nautical miles west of Huntsville, Alabama. The pilot described seeing multiple lights orbiting well ahead of and above the aircraft at varying altitudes. Memphis Air Route Traffic Control Center (ZME) filed the report. Controllers checked their radar systems and confirmed the phenomenon was not observed on ATC facility radar. The FAA marked the case as Closed. No resolution or explanation for what the pilot observed is provided in the document. No tail number for the aircraft is recorded, and no corroborating evidence beyond the pilot's visual observation is referenced. The report contains minimal redaction. Its primary limitation is that it relies entirely on a single pilot's visual account with no supporting sensor data.

What to know

  • This is a single-source report based solely on one pilot's visual observation — no radar, video, or instrument data corroborates the sighting.
  • The case is marked 'Closed' with no explanation offered for what the lights were, which means the FAA neither confirmed nor ruled out conventional explanations.
  • No aircraft tail number is recorded, limiting independent verification of the specific flight.
  • The description 'multiple lights orbiting at varying altitudes' is subjective and could match several conventional phenomena, including satellite constellations or military operations.
  • Low redaction means this document is largely complete as released, but its brevity also means little investigative follow-up is documented.
