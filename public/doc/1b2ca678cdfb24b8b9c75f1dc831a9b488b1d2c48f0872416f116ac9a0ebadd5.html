FBI Surveyed a Northeastern US Site After Repeated UAP Sightings

FBI November, 2024 Northeastern United States ✓ verified against source

This FBI document records an investigative site survey at a property in the northeastern United States where unidentified aerial phenomena had been repeatedly observed in November 2024. The bureau catalogued photographic evidence — treeline photos and trail camera images — but the document is heavily redacted, hiding the witness's identity, exact location, and any conclusions.

This document is an FBI Electronic Communication (an internal form known as FD-1057) related to an unidentified aerial phenomenon case. It records that FBI personnel conducted a site survey at a location in the northeastern United States where UAP had been repeatedly observed. During the survey, agents documented photographic evidence from the scene. This included treeline photographs taken to the west of the witness's home and photos captured by a trail camera on the witness's property. The document catalogues these evidence items but does not describe what the photographs actually depict or offer any conclusions about the phenomena. The witness's identity, exact location, case ID number, and the names of the approving and drafting officials are all redacted. The redaction is heavy throughout the document, leaving significant gaps in what can be understood from the visible text alone. The original document appears to be a low-quality scan, and the text contains OCR errors — for instance, 'Electronic' is rendered as 'Eectrenic' and 'Unidentified' as 'U-icontifiod'. Despite these artefacts, the document's purpose and basic facts are legible. Notably, the FBI's involvement indicates the bureau took the reported sightings seriously enough to send personnel to the location and formally document evidence. However, no analysis, explanation, or conclusion about the aerial phenomena is visible in the released material.

What to know

  • The document is heavily redacted: the witness's identity, exact location, case number, and officials involved are all blacked out.
  • This is a single-source witness account with no corroboration described in the visible portions of the document.
  • The document catalogues photographic evidence but does not describe what the photos show or draw any conclusions about the phenomena.
  • Several key details (incident date, general location) come from the release metadata rather than the body text itself.
