U.S. Air Force Crew Spotted Four UAP Over the Arabian Gulf in 2020

Department of War ✓ verified against source

A heavily redacted U.S. Air Force mission report documents four unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP) observed during a surveillance flight over the Arabian Gulf in 2020. The crew reported no advanced capabilities, but cloud cover prevented clear visual confirmation, and nearly all operational details are blacked out.

This document is a U.S. military Mission Report (MISREP 8799515), originally classified SECRET, produced under Air Forces Central Command (AFCENT). It records a UAP sighting that occurred during an intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance (ISR) operation in the Arabian Gulf region in 2020. The report was filed through the military's secure SIPRNet email system and is not scheduled for declassification until 2048. At approximately 17:36 Zulu time, the aircrew observed four UAP beneath their aircraft over a 27-second window. The sequence was specific: one object appeared at 17:36:22, two appeared side by side at 17:36:30, and a fourth appeared at 17:36:49. Cloud coverage prevented the crew from tracking the objects or obtaining a clear visual on them. The reporting crew assessed that the UAP did not exhibit advanced capabilities or materials. However, several UAP-related fields in the report — covering maneuverability, propulsion, signatures, and signs of intelligent control — were left blank rather than answered, so the assessment is incomplete. The redactions in this document are extreme. The aircraft type, exact location coordinates, altitude, speed, trajectory, sensor data, targeting pod designation, mission specifics, crew callsign, and all personnel identities have been withheld under national security and personal privacy exemptions. This means independent analysis of the sighting is effectively impossible based on this document alone.

What to know

  • Nearly every operational detail — aircraft type, location, altitude, speed, sensor used, and UAP physical description — is redacted, making independent evaluation impossible.
  • This is a single-source report from one aircrew with no listed corroborating observers.
  • Cloud cover limited observation quality; the crew could not get a clear visual on the objects.
  • The crew reported no advanced capabilities, but key UAP assessment fields (maneuverability, propulsion, signatures, intelligent control) were left blank rather than filled in with 'no'.
  • The document is not fully declassified — it remains classified SECRET until 2048, and what is available is a redacted release.
