United Airlines crew reported coloured lights above their jet near Kansas City

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

An FAA incident report documents the crew of United Airlines flight 1893 observing red, green, and white lights fading in and out high above their Boeing 737 at 37,000 feet, roughly 78 nautical miles east of Kansas City on 17 December 2022. The reporting facility suggested satellites as a possible explanation, and the case was closed.

This is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report — a standardised form used when pilots report something in the airspace that doesn't match known traffic. It was filed under primary code "Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon" and assigned case number 237UAP00139. On 17 December 2022 at 09:55 UTC, the crew of United Airlines flight 1893 — a Boeing 737-900 flying eastbound from San Francisco to Indianapolis at 37,000 feet — reported seeing red, green, and white lights fading in and out, positioned high above their aircraft. The observation occurred approximately 78 nautical miles east of Kansas City. The report was handled by Kansas City Center (the FAA's area control facility, identified as ZKC). The facility noted that the lights were "possible satellites." No further investigation is documented in the report, and its status is listed as closed. No corroborating sensor data, radar returns, or additional witness accounts are mentioned in the document. The report is a single-page pilot observation with no redactions of note.

What to know

  • This is a single-source pilot observation — no radar data, photos, or independent witnesses are cited in the document.
  • The FAA facility itself suggested satellites as a likely explanation, indicating low confidence in anything anomalous.
  • The case was closed without further investigation noted, which is standard for low-priority sightings.
  • No information is redacted in any meaningful way; the document is straightforward and brief.
  • The incident date (December 2022) predates the record's 2023 filing year — the report was logged after the event.
