Commercial Pilot Reports Unidentified Light at 34,000 Feet Over Arizona

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

An FAA incident report documents a commercial Frontier Airlines pilot encountering an unidentified light while flying at 36,000 feet near Page, Arizona on 21 September 2022. The pilot had to halt their descent because the object occupied the altitude they were descending into. The FAA closed the case without identifying the phenomenon.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report filed under case number 237UAP00114, classified under the primary code "Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon." It records an encounter reported by the pilot-in-command of Frontier Airlines flight FFT2117, a commercial operation, on 21 September 2022 at 04:28 UTC. According to the report, the aircraft was flying westbound at flight level 360 (36,000 feet), approximately 40 nautical miles south of Page, Arizona, when the pilot observed a light ahead of the aircraft travelling northbound at roughly flight level 340 (34,000 feet). The light then switched off and continued moving north. The pilot had to stop their planned descent because the unidentified object was at the altitude the aircraft needed to pass through. The report notes that TY/AWO were notified, though the document does not explain what these designations refer to. The case status is marked as "Closed," with no resolution or identification of the phenomenon provided. No radar data or corroborating witness accounts are mentioned in the document. The report is based entirely on the pilot's visual observation. No tail number for the reporting aircraft was recorded.

What to know

  • This is a single-source report based solely on the pilot's visual observation — no radar data or additional witnesses are mentioned.
  • The case was closed without any identification or explanation of what the pilot saw.
  • No tail number was recorded for the reporting aircraft, limiting independent verification.
  • The incident date in the document (September 2022) predates the file's catalogue year (2023), suggesting a reporting or processing delay.
  • The notification recipients (TY/AWO) are not explained in the document, so their role in the response is unclear.
