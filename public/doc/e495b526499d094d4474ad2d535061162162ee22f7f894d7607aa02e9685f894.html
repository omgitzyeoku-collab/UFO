CIA Report on 1957 Budapest UFO Sightings Is Unreadable

CIA 1957 Budapest, Hungary ± partially verified

A 1957 CIA information report documents unknown flying objects observed over Budapest, Hungary. The document exists only as an image file so degraded or redacted that no meaningful text can be read from it, leaving its actual findings unknown.

In 1957, the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) produced an information report on unidentified flying objects seen over Budapest, Hungary. The report is catalogued as CIA-UAP-009 in the US Department of War's public release archive. Unlike most documents in the archive, this one exists only as an image file rather than a searchable PDF. When optical character recognition (OCR) was applied to extract the text, the result was entirely garbled — no intelligible words could be recovered. This means the image is either too degraded, too low-resolution, or too heavily redacted to read by any automated method. Because the document text is unreadable, nothing is known about what the CIA actually reported — who witnessed the objects, what they looked like, how the agency assessed the sightings, or what conclusions were drawn. Every fact available comes solely from the file's title and the catalogue metadata, not from the document's contents. The war.gov release notes indicate that a more redacted version of this document was previously available on the CIA's own website, suggesting this version may contain additional material. However, that cannot be confirmed without a manual review of the original image file. The document is classified as having extreme redaction evidence.

What to know

  • No text from the actual document body is readable — all available information comes from the filename and catalogue metadata alone.
  • The redaction level is rated as extreme, meaning significant content may have been deliberately obscured.
  • No witness names, object descriptions, agency conclusions, or specific dates beyond the year 1957 can be verified from this file.
  • A previous, more redacted version reportedly existed on the CIA's website, but whether this version contains additional useful content is unknown without manual image review.
  • This document cannot corroborate or contradict any UFO claim on its own — it is, in its current form, essentially a placeholder confirming only that such a report once existed.
