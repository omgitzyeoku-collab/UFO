Frontier Airlines Pilot Reports Unexplained Flashes Over Kansas at 36,000 Feet

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

An FAA incident report documents a Frontier Airlines pilot observing unexplained flashes off the right side of the aircraft while flying at 36,000 feet near Kansas City on 2 March 2023. The pilot suggested the flashes might be space debris; the FAA closed the case with no further investigation noted.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report — a standardised form the agency uses to log pilot-reported unidentified aerial phenomena. It covers an event on 2 March 2023 at 02:10 UTC involving Frontier Airlines flight FFT647, an Airbus A320neo flying from Cincinnati (CVG) to Denver (DEN). While heading westbound at flight level 360 (36,000 feet), approximately 73 nautical miles north-northeast of Kansas City, the pilot reported seeing flashes off the right side of the aircraft. The pilot's own assessment was that the flashes might be space junk to the north of their position. The report was filed through FAA facility ZKC (Kansas City Center) under mandatory occurrence report ID ZKC-M-2023/03/01-0009. The case status is listed as closed. No law enforcement information was provided in the report, and no follow-up investigation or additional corroboration is mentioned. No tail number for the aircraft was recorded. The document contains minimal redaction. What it lacks is not blacked-out information but rather any corroborating evidence — there are no radar returns, no sensor data, and no mention of additional witnesses. The entire report rests on a single pilot observation.

What to know

  • This is a single-source report based solely on one pilot's observation, with no corroborating radar data, sensor readings, or additional witnesses mentioned.
  • The pilot themselves suggested the flashes 'may be space junk,' indicating uncertainty about what was observed.
  • The FAA closed the case without any noted follow-up investigation or law enforcement involvement.
  • No aircraft tail number was recorded in the report, which limits independent verification.
  • The incident location is approximate — 73 nautical miles north-northeast of Kansas City — not a precise coordinate.
