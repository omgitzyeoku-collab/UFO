CIA Sent Its 1953 UFO Panel Report to the Secretary of Defense

Department of War 1953 ✓ verified against source

This 1953 document is a CIA cover letter forwarding the Robertson Panel's scientific report on Unidentified Flying Objects to the Secretary of Defense. The panel found UFOs posed no direct physical threat but warned that UFO reports could clog military communication channels — an indirect national security risk.

On 17 March 1953, the CIA transmitted a scientific panel's report on Unidentified Flying Objects — also referred to as "Flying Saucers" — to the Secretary of Defense. The cover letter was signed by Richard D. Drain of the Intelligence Advisory Committee. According to the letter, the panel had been convened at the direction of General Smith, following a recommendation by the Intelligence Advisory Committee. The panel's key conclusion, as described in the cover letter, was that UFOs did not pose a direct physical threat to national security. However, the panel warned of indirect dangers — specifically, that a flood of UFO sighting reports could clog communication channels, potentially interfering with military operations. The panel suggested ways to eliminate these indirect risks. The CIA's cover letter also made clear that the agency did not consider UFO sightings to be primarily its concern, though it offered to assist. Copies of the report were also sent to the Chairman of the National Security Resources Board. The document was classified under US espionage laws (Title 18, Sections 793 and 794) at the time of its creation. What survives today is the transmittal copy — a routing slip and cover letter — rather than the full panel report itself. The attached pages of the actual scientific panel report are nearly illegible due to severe degradation of the source material. This is an important distinction: most of the panel's detailed findings, evidence review, and specific recommendations cannot be read from this copy. The illegibility stems from poor document quality rather than formal redaction, though the document also shows evidence of heavy redaction throughout.

What to know

  • This is the transmittal copy (cover letter and routing slip), not the full Robertson Panel report — the actual panel findings attached are nearly illegible due to source degradation.
  • OCR quality is extremely poor on most pages; only the cover letter and routing slip are substantially readable.
  • Heavy redaction is present throughout the document, separate from the degradation issues.
  • The panel's conclusion that UFOs posed 'no direct physical threat' and the concern about 'clogging communication channels' are only partially legible in the source text and should be treated with moderate confidence.
  • The document was classified under espionage laws when created; its current availability does not mean all contents are recoverable or complete.
