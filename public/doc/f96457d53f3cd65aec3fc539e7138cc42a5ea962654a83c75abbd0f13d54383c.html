US Navy Ordered Stations to Report Flying Disc Sightings in 1948

Department of War ✓ verified against source

This December 1948 confidential memorandum from the Fifth Naval District relays a directive from the Chief of Naval Operations ordering all naval stations to report sightings of 'Flying Discs' to the nearest Air Force command and Naval District Intelligence Office. The directive states that US Air Force intelligence believed a cyclical reappearance of Flying Discs was imminent.

This is a confidential memorandum dated 13 December 1948, issued from the headquarters of the Fifth Naval District in Norfolk, Virginia. It was sent by the Commandant of the Fifth Naval District to activities listed in a distribution list. The memo forwards a directive originating from the Chief of Naval Operations regarding the reporting of 'Flying Disc' sightings. The core instruction is straightforward: the US Air Force Director of Intelligence had informed the Navy Department that a cyclical pattern of 'Flying Disc' reappearances was becoming apparent and that a new interval was imminent. Based on this assessment, the Air Force requested that sightings be reported as quickly as possible and investigated. The directive ordered all naval stations to report any sighting of Flying Discs or other unidentified aerial objects to the nearest Air Force command and to the Naval District Intelligence Office. Personnel were also instructed to obtain photographic evidence whenever possible. The intelligence chain was clearly defined: District Intelligence Officers would forward information to the Chief of Naval Intelligence, who would then relay it to the USAF Director of Intelligence by the fastest means available. The original directive was signed by Thos. B. Inglis, and the forwarding memo by R. C. Hudson, identified as Chief of Staff. The document does not describe any specific sighting, nor does it provide the data or methodology behind the Air Force's assessment of a cyclical pattern. It is an administrative directive establishing reporting procedures.

What to know

  • The document was originally classified CONFIDENTIAL. Its current declassification status should be independently verified.
  • The reference to a 'cycle of reappearance' implies the Air Force had conducted pattern analysis, but no supporting data or methodology is provided in this document.
  • OCR quality is poor with numerous character substitutions, making some text ambiguous — confidence on several facts is rated amber rather than green.
  • This is a forwarded directive, not a first-hand report of any sighting. The original Chief of Naval Operations letter is quoted, not attached, so context may be incomplete.
  • Pages 1 and 3 contain fragmentary, largely unreadable text that appears to be filing or routing metadata.
