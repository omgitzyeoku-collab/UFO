Two Commercial Flights Reported Unexplained Lights Near Little Rock in 2023

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

An FAA SKYWATCH incident report documents a pilot sighting of unidentified lights during a commercial flight on 25 August 2023. A second aircraft independently reported seeing the same phenomenon in the same area, but the case was closed without explanation.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report filed under case ID 237UAP00292 (MOR ID: ZME-M-2023/08/25-0002). It records a pilot's sighting of an unidentified aerial phenomenon during a commercial flight on 25 August 2023. The reporting aircraft, callsign BYA651 operated by BYA, was flying eastbound at FL 270 (27,000 feet) in the vicinity of Little Rock when the crew observed dim white lights that would get brighter and moved in circles and up-and-down patterns. The crew did not report the altitude of the phenomenon, which means no reliable estimate of its distance or size is possible. A second aircraft, JetBlue flight 358, independently reported seeing the same phenomenon in the same area. Two separate crews observing the same thing adds weight to the report, though both accounts are visual-only — no radar or instrument data is referenced in the document. The FAA marked the case as closed. No resolution or explanation for the sighting is provided in the report. There is minimal evidence of redaction in the document.

What to know

  • Both sightings are based solely on pilot visual observations — no radar, sensor, or instrument data is referenced in the report.
  • The altitude of the phenomenon was not reported, making it impossible to estimate its size, distance, or speed.
  • The case is marked as closed with no explanation or resolution provided by the FAA.
  • Two independent flight crews corroborate the sighting, which is stronger than a single-witness report, but both are still visual-only accounts.
  • This is a routine FAA incident report, not a detailed investigation — it records what was reported, not what was concluded.
