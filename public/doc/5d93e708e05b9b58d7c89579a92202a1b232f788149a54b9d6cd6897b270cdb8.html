American Airlines Crew Reports High-Altitude Balloon Over Missouri in 2023

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This FAA incident report documents a sighting by an American Airlines crew on 3 February 2023. While flying at 34,000 feet near St. Joseph, Missouri, the pilots observed a large balloon at roughly 50,000 feet that appeared to drop 20,000 feet in altitude. The FAA classified it as an Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon and closed the case.

On 3 February 2023, the crew of American Airlines flight AAL1743 — an Airbus A319 flying from Cedar Rapids to Dallas/Fort Worth — reported an unusual object while passing 13 nautical miles west of St. Joseph, Missouri. The aircraft was heading north at 34,000 feet (FL340) when the crew spotted what they described as a large balloon off the right side of the aircraft. The pilot in command estimated the balloon was at approximately 50,000 feet (FL500) or above — well beyond the typical ceiling for most known civilian balloons. The crew then observed the object drop roughly 20,000 feet in altitude. Kansas City Center (ZKC) was the air traffic facility that received the report. The FAA logged this under its SKYWATCH system and assigned it the primary code "Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon." The case is now closed. Notably, the report does not mention any corroborating radar data, and no tail number was recorded for the aircraft. The crew described the object as a balloon, but the FAA still coded the event as a UAP rather than confirming that identification. This incident took place during February 2023, a period when several high-altitude balloon sightings were reported across US airspace and attracted significant public attention. The document itself is brief and contains limited additional detail beyond the crew's account.

What to know

  • This is a single-source pilot observation. The report does not mention any radar data or other sensor confirmation of the object.
  • The crew called it a 'balloon,' but that is their interpretation — the FAA still classified it as an Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon rather than confirming the identification.
  • An estimated altitude of 50,000 feet is above the certified ceiling of most civilian balloons, making the crew's altitude estimate uncertain.
  • No aircraft tail number was recorded, which limits the ability to independently verify flight details.
  • The case is closed with no stated conclusion about what the object was.
