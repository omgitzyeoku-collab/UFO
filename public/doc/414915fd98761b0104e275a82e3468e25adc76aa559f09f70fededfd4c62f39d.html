Virgin Atlantic Crew Reported Three Rotating Lights Over Iowa

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This FAA incident report documents a sighting by the crew of a Virgin Atlantic Boeing 787 on 10 March 2023. While flying at 37,000 feet near Fort Dodge, Iowa, the crew observed three very bright lights high above them, rotating around each other with varying brightness. The FAA closed the report with no identification of the phenomenon.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report — the system the Federal Aviation Administration uses to log unidentified aerial phenomena reported by pilots. It covers a sighting on 10 March 2023 by the crew of Virgin Atlantic flight VIR156T, a Boeing 787 flying from Las Vegas to London Heathrow. The crew was heading east-northeast at 37,000 feet, in the vicinity of Fort Dodge, Iowa, when they observed an unidentified aerial phenomenon off the left side of the aircraft. They described three very bright lights positioned high in the sky above them — notable given the aircraft was already at cruising altitude. The lights varied in brightness and were rotating around each other rapidly. The crew did not take evasive action, indicating the phenomenon was not considered an immediate collision risk. The Airways Weather Office was advised of the sighting. The FAA assigned the report the identifier 237UAP00238 and the mandatory occurrence report ID ZMP-M-2023/03/10-0001. The report's status is marked as Closed. No resolution or identification of the lights is recorded in the document. There is no mention of corroborating radar data, additional witness accounts from other aircraft, or any follow-up investigation.

What to know

  • This is a single-source pilot report. No corroborating radar data or independent witness accounts are included in the document.
  • The crew described the lights as 'very high in the sky' relative to an aircraft already at 37,000 feet, but no altitude estimate for the lights themselves is given.
  • The report was closed with no explanation or identification of the phenomenon — 'closed' does not mean 'explained'.
  • Redaction levels appear low, but the document is brief and contains limited investigative detail to begin with.
  • FOD refers to the Fort Dodge Regional Airport area in Iowa — the sighting occurred in that vicinity, not necessarily at the airport itself.
