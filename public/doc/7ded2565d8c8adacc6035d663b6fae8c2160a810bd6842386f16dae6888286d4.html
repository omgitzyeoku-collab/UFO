Two Airline Crews Reported a UFO Over North Carolina in 2012

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

An FAA SKYWATCH incident report documents two aircraft crews flying at roughly 40,000 feet who reported seeing an unidentified object ten miles south of Elizabeth City, North Carolina, on 13 January 2012. No radar evidence confirmed the sighting, no other aircraft were in the area, and the case was closed without explanation.

This is a one-page FAA SKYWATCH incident report filed under record 237UAP00015. It documents a UFO sighting reported on 13 January 2012 at 23:55 UTC. Two aircraft — callsigns FIV546 (a Cessna Citation 560) and WJA2668 — were flying southbound at flight level 400 (approximately 40,000 feet) when both crews reported seeing an unidentified object ten miles south of Elizabeth City, North Carolina. The sighting was reported up the chain by a Washington Centre (ZDC) Operations Manager. Controllers checked radar and found no positive tracks in the area. They also confirmed there were no other known aircraft nearby. Two additional contacts were made: PXT Approach (a local radar facility) and Giant Killer (a military coordination unit that manages warning areas off the US East Coast). Neither reported any aircraft spilling out of restricted airspace. The report offers almost no description of the object itself — no shape, size, colour, speed, or behaviour is recorded. The case status is listed as "Closed" with no resolution or identification provided. The FAA primary code for the incident is simply "OTHER." One administrative quirk: the record appears in a 2023 dataset, but the actual incident occurred in January 2012. The 2023 date most likely reflects when the record was processed or released to the public, not when the event took place.

What to know

  • This was a visual-only sighting. No radar, sensor, or photographic evidence corroborated what the crews saw.
  • Two independent crews reported the same object, which adds some weight, but both were in the same patch of sky at the same time — this is one event, not two separate ones.
  • The report contains almost no descriptive detail about the object. There is nothing here to support any specific identification, mundane or otherwise.
  • The case was closed with no stated conclusion. 'Closed' does not mean 'explained' — the FAA simply stopped investigating.
  • Redaction levels are low; the document appears to be released largely intact, but its brevity means there was very little information to begin with.
