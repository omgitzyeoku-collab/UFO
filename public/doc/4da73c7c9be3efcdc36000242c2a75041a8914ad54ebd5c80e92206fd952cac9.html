Multiple Aircraft Reported Unidentified Phenomenon Above Them in 2023

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This FAA SKYWATCH incident report documents a March 2023 event in which multiple commercial aircraft reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon above their position. The report contains no descriptive details about the object — no shape, speed, or altitude — and was closed without law enforcement involvement.

On 2 March 2023 at 01:09 UTC, the FAA logged a SKYWATCH incident report under record 237UAP00233. The report was filed in connection with commercial flight LET6164, operated by LET, and references a Mandatory Occurrence Report (MOR) originating from the Houston Center (ZHU) dated 1 March 2023 — suggesting the sighting occurred late on 1 March and was formally recorded the following day. Multiple commercial aircraft reported an unidentified aerial phenomenon above their position. Despite this, FAA records noted no other reported traffic in the area at the time. The report provides no further detail about the phenomenon itself — no shape, size, speed, altitude, colour, or direction of movement is recorded. The FAA's MOR entry states that no position or directional information about the phenomenon was provided. Law enforcement was not notified. The case status is listed as closed. This is a procedural incident report, not an investigation file. It confirms that something was reported by multiple aircraft and logged through official channels, but it contains no analysis, no radar data, no photographs, and no conclusion about what the phenomenon was.

What to know

  • This is a single FAA operational report — it contains no radar data, photographs, or supplementary investigation materials.
  • The report is procedural, not descriptive: it records that a sighting was reported but includes no details about the phenomenon's appearance or behaviour.
  • Multiple aircraft corroborated the sighting, but the report does not specify how many aircraft were involved or identify them beyond the primary callsign LET6164.
  • The incident location is not explicitly stated in the document, though FAA facility codes ZHU (Houston Center) and DEN appear in the record.
  • The case was closed with no indication of follow-up investigation or explanation.
