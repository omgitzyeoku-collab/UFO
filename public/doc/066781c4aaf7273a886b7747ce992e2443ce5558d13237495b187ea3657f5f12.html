Commercial Pilot Reports Oblong Object at 37,000 Feet Near Texas

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

An FAA SKYWATCH incident report documents a Spirit Airlines crew observing a round, black-and-white oblong object — roughly the size of a regional jet — while flying at 37,000 feet on 21 August 2023. The case was closed with no identification or explanation provided.

This is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report filed under case number 237UAP00290, categorised as an Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon. It records an observation made by the crew of Spirit Airlines flight NKS797, a commercial service from Houston (IAH) to Fort Lauderdale (FLL), on 21 August 2023. While flying eastbound at flight level 370 (approximately 37,000 feet), the crew reported seeing an object off the left side of the aircraft, located 11 nautical miles southwest of the PCU navigation waypoint. They described it as round, black and white, and oblong in shape — approximately the size of an Embraer E-145 regional jet. The object was travelling westbound (opposite to their direction) at between 36,000 and 37,000 feet. No evasive action was taken and no operational impact was reported. The FAA's internal record (MOR ID: ZHU-M-2023/08/21-0003, indicating Houston Center airspace) notes that AWO was notified. The case status is listed as Closed. The report contains no resolution, no identification of the object, and no indication that radar data or other sensor evidence was reviewed. There is no mention of corroborating witnesses or follow-up investigation in the document.

What to know

  • This is a single-source report based entirely on one aircrew's visual observation — no radar data, sensor confirmation, or independent witnesses are included.
  • The size estimate (comparable to an Embraer E-145) is a subjective judgment made by a pilot at altitude, not a measured value.
  • The case is marked 'Closed' with no explanation of what the object was — closure does not imply identification.
  • Redaction levels appear low, but the report is brief and contains no analytical assessment or follow-up findings.
  • The PCU waypoint location would need cross-referencing with FAA navigation data to pinpoint the exact geographic coordinates of the sighting.
