Aeromexico Crew Reports Orbiting Lights Over Central US at 37,000 Feet

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

An FAA SKYWATCH report documents the crew of Aeromexico flight AMX692, a Boeing 737 MAX 8 flying from Mexico City to Toronto, observing several lights performing an orbital pattern at very high altitude roughly 60–80 miles ahead of their aircraft. Neither air traffic control radar nor a nearby FedEx flight could confirm the sighting, and the FAA closed the case without identifying the objects.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report filed under case number 237UAP00086. It records a UAP (Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon) sighting reported by the flight crew of Aeromexico flight AMX692, a Boeing 737 MAX 8 operating a commercial route from Mexico City (MMMX) to Toronto (CYYZ). The aircraft was cruising at FL370 (37,000 feet), heading 041 at 504 knots, over the central United States — likely the Kentucky/Indiana area based on navigational references in the report. The crew reported observing several lights performing an orbital pattern at very high altitude, estimated 60 to 80 miles ahead of their position. Memphis Center (ZME), the FAA air traffic control facility handling the area, checked radar and found no returns showing any objects in the vicinity at any altitude. A FedEx cargo flight, FDX1, was also in the area at FL380 (38,000 feet), approximately 70 miles west of Louisville. That crew reported seeing nothing anomalous. Memphis Center filed a Mandatory Occurrence Report (MOR) for the record and notified AWO, an FAA operations office. The case is marked Closed with no explanation or identification of the lights provided. No tail number was recorded for the Aeromexico aircraft, which limits further verification.

What to know

  • This is a single-source visual observation by one flight crew. No radar data, photographs, or instrument readings support the sighting.
  • A nearby FedEx aircraft in the same general area reported seeing nothing unusual, meaning there is no independent corroboration.
  • The FAA closed the case without resolving what the lights were — 'Closed' does not mean identified or explained.
  • The aircraft's tail number field is blank in the report, which limits the ability to cross-reference the specific flight independently.
  • Navigational fix references suggest the sighting occurred over the Kentucky/Indiana region, but no specific location is stated outright in the document.
