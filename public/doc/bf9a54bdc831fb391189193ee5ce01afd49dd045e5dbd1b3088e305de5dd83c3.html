United Airlines crew reported black UFO 100 feet from their Boeing 737

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

An FAA preliminary incident report records that the crew of United Airlines flight 1986, a Boeing 737-800 flying from San Francisco to Seattle, reported a black unidentified object roughly 100 feet from their aircraft at 8,000 feet altitude near Oakland, California. The document is a brief operations-centre log entry, not a full investigation, and includes no conclusions about what the object was.

This document is a preliminary incident report from the FAA's Washington Operations Center, originally logged on 6 March 2024. It records a UAP (unidentified anomalous phenomena) sighting reported by the flight crew of United Airlines flight 1986, a Boeing 737-800 operating the San Francisco to Seattle route. According to the report, the crew observed a black unidentified object approximately 100 feet from the aircraft's 1 o'clock position — that is, slightly to the right and ahead of the nose. At the time, the aircraft was heading northwest at 8,000 feet altitude, roughly 2 nautical miles north-northeast of Oakland, California (coordinates 37.75°N, 122.21°W). Despite the close proximity, the crew took no evasive action. The report notes that law enforcement was not notified. The document carries an internal tracking reference (WOC 7-3333) and staff initials, but no follow-up investigation, analysis, or conclusion about the nature of the object is included. The only physical description given is the colour: black. No size, shape, speed, or behaviour of the object is recorded. This is a single-page operational log entry — not a finished investigation or analytical product. It represents the earliest stage of FAA incident documentation: a crew report forwarded through the operations centre. Whether any further inquiry took place is not indicated in this document.

What to know

  • This is a preliminary operations-centre log, not a completed investigation or official finding. No conclusions about the object are drawn.
  • The account comes from a single source — the flight crew. No corroborating radar data, sensor readings, or independent witness reports are mentioned in the document.
  • The claimed 100-foot proximity at 8,000 feet altitude would represent a serious near-miss if accurate, but pilot distance estimates at altitude are inherently imprecise.
  • The only physical detail provided is the object's colour (black). No size, shape, speed, or flight behaviour is described.
  • No follow-up investigation is included in this document, so it is unknown whether additional inquiry was conducted.
