American Airlines Pilot Reported Unexplained Lights Over Kansas at 39,000 Feet

FAA 2023 ✓ verified against source

This FAA SKYWATCH incident report documents a pilot aboard American Airlines flight AAL2654, a Boeing 737-800, reporting red, green, and white lights fading in and out high above the aircraft while flying at 39,000 feet near Kansas City on 17 December 2022. The FAA noted the lights were possibly satellites and closed the case without further analysis.

This document is an FAA SKYWATCH incident report — a standardised form used by the Federal Aviation Administration to log pilot-reported unidentified aerial phenomena (UAPs). It was filed under case number 237UAP00137 and assigned MOR ID ZKC-M-2022/12/17-0001 by the Kansas City Air Route Traffic Control Center (ZKC). On 17 December 2022, the crew of American Airlines flight AAL2654, a commercial Boeing 737-800 flying from Las Vegas to Chicago O'Hare, reported seeing something unusual while eastbound at flight level 390 (39,000 feet). The aircraft was approximately 38 nautical miles northwest of Kansas City (MCI) at the time. The pilot described red, green, and white lights fading in and out, positioned high above the aircraft. The report itself notes a possible prosaic explanation: satellites. No corroborating radar data or sensor evidence is mentioned in the document. The case is marked as closed. No resolution, follow-up investigation, or additional analysis is documented. No tail number for the aircraft was recorded in the report.

What to know

  • This is a single-source pilot observation — no radar, sensor, or second-witness corroboration is mentioned in the document.
  • The FAA's own report suggests the lights were possibly satellites, which is a common explanation for fading lights seen at high altitude.
  • The case was closed with no documented resolution or follow-up analysis.
  • No aircraft tail number was recorded, limiting independent verification of the specific flight.
  • Low redaction evidence — the document appears largely complete, but the lack of detail reflects the brevity of the report itself rather than deliberate withholding.
