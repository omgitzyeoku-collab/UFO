Press kit

For journalists, researchers, and editors covering the US government UAP/UFO disclosures. Everything on this page is free to use, no embargo, no clearance needed.

One-line description

UAP Files is a free public archive of every declassified UAP/UFO document released by the US government, translated into plain English with source-cited verification.

One-paragraph description

UAP Files mirrors every artefact on war.gov/UFO/ (PURSUE Releases 1–4) plus the US National Archives UAP series, AARO and the FBI Vault — documents, videos, NASA Apollo audio recordings. Each item is processed by a 5-agent QA pipeline (extractor → independent verifier → adjudicator → citation linker → rewriter) that produces a plain-English summary with claim-level confidence tiers (green = source-verified, amber = partial, red = unverified, no summary published). The site lets readers browse 965 records as a grid, plot incidents on a world map, explore a force-directed graph of how documents connect via shared entities, or scrub through a timeline by incident year. Free, no signup, no ads, no tracking; corpus is downloadable as a single archive. Open source.

Story angles under-reported elsewhere

The Apollo 12 medical debrief

NASA-UAP-D008 contains a 150 MB audio recording of Pete Conrad, Richard Gordon, and Alan Bean discussing "streaks of light" they saw with their eyes closed during the mission. Often misreported as cosmic-ray retinal impacts but the debrief audio shows the team taking it seriously and ruling out radiation as the only explanation.

The CENTCOM four-object IR video

DOW-UAP-PR050 — a 20-second infrared video showing four areas of contrast in formation over water, dated 26 August 2022, filed under CENTCOM. Widely reported as "four UFOs over Iran"; be careful with that framing. The record's own caveats state the "Iran" reference is uploader-defined and not independently verified by AARO, AARO says only that the footage is "likely" from a US military infrared sensor, and the materials "lack a substantiated chain of custody" — the video reached the classified network two years after the purported date. We mirror the video and every one of those caveats. The interesting story here is the provenance gap, not the object.

Geographic concentration — and why we won't claim it

Of the post-2020 records that carry a location at all, 54 of 139 (39%) are in the Persian Gulf / Middle East theatre. We are not offering that as a finding, and you shouldn't print it as one: only 139 of 702 post-2020 documents have a location field. The records that do are mostly Pentagon mission reports (Gulf-heavy by deployment); the 575 FAA pilot reports carry no location and are overwhelmingly US-domestic. So the pattern is most likely an artefact of which records have a location, not of where things were seen. If you want to pursue it, the honest version is a question — "why do only 20% of post-2020 records carry a location?" — and we'll help you get the data.

The "unverified" tier

88 of 965 documents (9%) failed our verification pass — the citation-linker couldn't find a verbatim source quote supporting the summary, so we publish no summary for them at all, only the original file and the document's own official description. They are browsable at /?tier=red — nothing is hidden from you except our own unverified opinion. Most are heavily-redacted or badly-scanned material. Happy to walk a reporter through why any individual one failed.

How our QA pipeline works

Detailed in /methodology. Five-stage pipeline, every claim must have a verbatim quote from the source document, agents work in independent contexts to prevent confirmation bias. We deliberately surface what we couldn't verify rather than papering over uncertainty.

What we will NOT do (editorial policy)

  • Run ads
  • Paywall content
  • Sell user data
  • Take money from anyone whose involvement would compromise editorial neutrality (governments, defence contractors, media outlets with active UAP coverage)
  • Make "disclosure"-style claims that extend beyond what source documents actually say
